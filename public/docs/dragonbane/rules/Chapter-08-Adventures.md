# CHAPTER 8 – ADVENTURES

The previous chapters of this book have covered player characters and their abilities, combat and magic, gear and monsters. Now it is time to combine all these components and explain how they together form an exciting adventure!

This chapter contains rules and tools for handling journeys, managing non-player characters, and creating your own adventures. At the end of the chapter, you will find a complete introductory adventure – *The Castle of the Robber Knight*. The chapter is mainly written for the GM, which is who we mean by "you" in this chapter.

## JOURNEYS

As adventurers in the world of *Dragonbane*, the player characters must often journey through dark forests, rugged moors, and high mountains. A journey can be much more than just a way of getting somewhere – it can be a little adventure in itself. Who knows what lies just around the corner?

**Travel:** Journeys proceed in shifts. As mentioned in chapter 1, there are four shifts in a day: morning, day, evening, and night. As a rule, the player characters can hike roughly 15 kilometers per shift on foot or roughly 30 kilometers on horseback. Difficult terrain might slow them down. The player characters can normally walk or ride for a maximum of two shifts per day, including a few short breaks. If they make a long stop on the road, the distance they cover is reduced. They cannot march with weapons drawn.

**Forced March:** A player character can walk or ride for a third shift during the same day, but becomes Exhausted in the process. An already Exhausted character cannot travel a third shift, and no one can ever travel more than three shifts in a single day.

> **TRAVEL MAPS**
>
> Most official adventures for *Dragonbane* include a map of the area where the adventure takes place. You can use the adventure map to determine how far the player characters need to travel and keep track of their progress. The maps are not exact, so you will sometimes have to make a judgment call.

> **LONG JOURNEYS**
>
> The travel rules are designed for short treks within a limited area. If the player characters go on a long journey to another part of the world, there is no need to play it out in detail – the GM can just make a cut in the story and pick up again when they arrive at their destination.

### PATHFINDERS

Player characters who follow a road or clear path are not at risk of getting lost, but such luxuries are rarely available. When they are out in the wilderness, navigation is more difficult, and all sorts of mishaps can occur.

When traveling through pathless terrain, the player characters must appoint a *pathfinder* who leads the way for the group. This person must make a BUSHCRAFT roll every shift to find their way. If the pathfinder has no map, the roll is made with a bane. A spyglass gives a boon.

On a failure, the group suffers a mishap – roll on the table below. You can adjust the mishap to better fit the area.

**Rolling a Dragon:** A pathfinder who rolls a Dragon finds a shortcut – the distance covered this shift is doubled.

**Difficult Terrain:** If the terrain is particularly difficult, you can add a bane to the BUSHCRAFT roll.

> **RANDOM ENCOUNTERS**
>
> During a journey, the player characters can encounter the strangest creatures from far and near. Once per shift, or whenever you deem appropriate, you can roll on a table for random encounters. Random encounters can vary depending on the area you are in, which is why they are typically included in the adventure. You can also choose a suitable encounter instead of rolling randomly or make one up yourself. Some random encounters can even occur at night and when the player characters have made camp.

## MAKING CAMP

The player characters need to sleep at least one shift per day to avoid sleep deprivation (page 54). Out in the wilderness, each player character must make a BUSHCRAFT roll to find a suitable resting place. Anyone without a sleeping fur gets a bane on the roll. If it fails, the shift does not count as sleep, nor can it be used as a shift rest (page 52).

**Tent:** A tent grants a boon when making camp, and a successful roll allows multiple people to sleep in the tent without making their own BUSHCRAFT rolls. A small tent can accommodate two people and a large one has room for six. If the person setting up the tent fails, other player characters can roll separately for the same shift.

**Keeping Watch:** Since random encounters can occur at any time, it may be wise for the player characters to sleep during different shifts so there is always someone awake to keep watch.

**Dangerous Locations:** In certain locations, such as a dungeon with enemies all around, the player characters cannot make camp or take a shift rest. They must first leave the area and find a safer spot.

> **COLD**
>
> In cold weather, particularly at night, the player characters must make a BUSHCRAFT roll to withstand the cold (page 54). It takes a blanket to avoid a bane, while a fur grants a boon on the roll.

## FOOD IN THE WILDERNESS

Out in the wilds a player character can spend a shift hunting, fishing, or foraging for edible plants. The character cannot make any progress on the map during such a shift.

**Hunting:** Hunting requires a ranged weapon or a hunting trap. First, the hunter makes a HUNTING & FISHING roll to track down an animal. If it succeeds, roll on the table below to see what kind of animal it is. Killing it requires a second roll, this time for the hunter's weapon or HUNTING & FISHING again if a trap is used. Note that some animals cannot be caught with a trap. The table shows how many rations of food the animal yields.

**Fishing:** Fishing requires fishing gear and is handled with a HUNTING & FISHING roll. If it succeeds, the fisher catches D4 rations of food with a fishing rod or D6 rations with a fishing net.

**Foraging:** The player character makes a BUSHCRAFT roll – with a bane in winter and a boon in the fall. On a success, the forager finds D3 food rations worth of edible mushrooms, roots, and other plants.

**Cooking:** Anyone eating raw fish or meat is at risk of falling ill – roll against virulence 10. Plants can be eaten raw without risk, but it takes two rations of raw plants to cover the daily need of food. Cooking up to ten rations of food takes one shift and requires a successful BUSHCRAFT roll. On a failure, the food counts as uncooked. A field kitchen gives a boon to the roll. The same goes for a proper kitchen, and there you can also cook as much food as you want. See the Master Chef heroic ability.

## THE GAMEMASTER'S ROLE

As GM, you have an important role at the table. You describe what the player characters see, who they meet, and the challenges they face. Like the director of a film, you set the scene and are largely responsible for creating a good story. Being the GM is both challenging and rewarding. Feel free to let the role of GM rotate between all members of your group. Below are some simple tips on how you as GM can make your gaming experience even more entertaining.

### ENGAGE THE PLAYERS

As GM, make sure that all players feel included. If one player character has been getting lots of attention, give the others some spotlight as well. Also be mindful of the pacing. It is often a good idea to intersperse intense events with calmer scenes. But if you notice that the game is slowing down and the players are getting bored, let something exciting happen that forces the players to react.

### ROLL DICE OPENLY

The GM often handles the dice rolls for NPCs and monsters. You may be tempted to roll them secretly in order to control the story, but we recommend that you always roll dice openly. The benefit of open rolls is that it gets all players involved and increases the excitement around the table. After all, you are playing the game together.

### PLAY WITH THE PLAYERS

As GM it can be tempting to think you are playing *against* the players, as you control all the adversaries and dangers. But it is important to remember that you are all playing to have fun together. If the players come up with a clever plan and execute it successfully, let them feel good about themselves, and be content that you are creating stories and lasting memories together.

### BRING THE WORLD TO LIFE

As GM, you describe what the player characters experience. Try to paint a picture of a real world by describing details, sounds, and smells. Tell them about the owls hooting around the camp at night, the frogs croaking in the swamp, and the leaves rustling in the wind. A detail or two is often enough to make a scene or place come alive.

## NON-PLAYER CHARACTERS

You control everything in the world around the player characters, from dragons to demons, but your most important tool for creating drama is often your non-player characters. Stats for typical types of NPCs can be found on the next page.

**Random NPCs:** You can use the random tables on page 106 to create unique NPCs. Grab a die of each type from the box and roll them together.

### MANAGING NPCS

Mechanically, NPCs function like player characters. They move, perform actions, sustain damage, and use WP in the same way. But in practice, you should disregard all rules mechanics for NPCs if they do not directly affect a player character. Don't roll dice for actions performed by NPCs unless they are attacking or healing a player character. You *can* roll for NPCs in other situations if it enhances the drama, but usually there is no need for it. Remember, an NPC rolls against the default skill level of 5 if no other level is listed (page 33).

### MINIONS & BOSSES

NPCs can be divided into two categories: *minions* and *bosses*.

**Minions** are groups of less significant NPCs with identical stats. They are rarely described by name or personality. A minion who reaches zero HP is dead unless you want it to survive – perhaps to tell the player characters something important with its dying words. Minions almost never use WP and often act on the same turn in combat.

**Bosses** are different. They are often described with names and backgrounds, and usually play an important part in the adventure. A boss draws their own initiative card in combat. Bosses have WP and use innate and heroic abilities just like player characters. Particularly powerful bosses often have multiple heroic abilities – not least Robust and Focused several times over, which can give them far more HP and WP than the player characters.

If possible, try not to have your bosses die too early in the game. A boss who lives to fight another day is more fun than a dead one. Also make sure that the player characters cannot get to the boss too easily – put some minions in their way that need to be dealt with first. A boss encounter should be challenging!

> **ATTRIBUTES FOR NPCS**
>
> In adventures for *Dragonbane*, attribute scores for NPCs are not listed as they are very rarely used. If you at some point would need to roll against an exact attribute score for an NPC, use the guidelines below:
>

>
> **CON:** Roll against maximum HP, reduced by 2 for each level of the Robust heroic ability.
>
> **WIL:** Roll against maximum WP if this is listed, reduced by 2 for each level of the Focused heroic ability. If WP is not listed, roll against 10.
>
> **INT & CHA:** Roll against 10.

## CREATING ADVENTURES

Creating adventures for *Dragonbane* is easy. Preparing an adventure is often a fun and creative process. Here are some tips to get you started. At the end of this chapter, you will find a complete introductory adventure – *The Castle of the Robber Knight*.

**Random Generation:** With the tables on the following pages and a few dice rolls you can quickly and easily come up with the framework for a short adventure. Grab a die of each type from the box and roll each of them three times – this gives you a quest, a journey, and a site where the adventure reaches its climax. Keep in mind that the tables are meant as inspiration and that it is okay to make changes.

### 1. THE QUEST

To pull the player characters into an adventure, you need a "hook." It might be a quest given by an NPC, but it can also be a letter, a legend, or a map they find. The hook is what sets the adventure in motion and gives the players a goal. It is important to be clear when creating a hook. If you want your adventure to take place in an abandoned crypt, give the player characters a quest to find a lost treasure at the end of the crypt. Knowing what they are supposed to do makes it easier for the players to navigate the game.

### 2. THE JOURNEY

Traveling through the world of *Dragonbane* is an adventure in itself. Rules for journeys are found earlier in this chapter and official adventures have tables for random encounters. Playing the journey gives the player characters opportunities to use their skills to find the right path, gather food, and avoid dangers.

It can also be worthwhile to roleplay small talk around the campfire. Small details like these make the game world feel more alive. The journey is not mandatory however – sometimes you may want to skip it and let an adventure begin at the adventure site.

### 3. THE ADVENTURE SITE

The core of an adventure is an *adventure site*. It is the place where the adventure reaches its climax and where the player characters spend most of their time. You can easily create your own adventure sites by looking at *The Castle of the Robber Knight* at the end of this chapter and using the random table on page 111. Longer adventures can have multiple adventure sites.

**Map:** An adventure site is best illustrated using a map with a 2-meter grid. The map is a useful tool for you to describe the site to your players in a consistent manner. It can also be used for combat encounters (page 44). A simple sketch on squared paper may suffice, but you can also create beautiful and detailed maps using computer software.

**Challenges:** It would not be much of an adventure without dangers and challenges to face. Challenges often involve combat with enemies and monsters, but there are other variants that can come in the form of practical obstacles that must be overcome (a fallen tree blocking the road), social challenges (guards that need persuading), or traps (a treasure chest with a poison needle).

**Traps:** Traps are a good way to make the adventure site feel unpredictable and dangerous to your players. Use them sparingly in carefully selected locations. Traps can be detected with the SPOT HIDDEN skill or by the players describing that they are looking in the right place. For truly lethal traps, it is important to give the players a hint, so they get a chance to spot the trap. There could be a strange indentation in the stone floor, a mark on the wall, or something similar.

**Final Boss:** Most adventures end with the player characters facing a powerful adversary – a "boss." Make sure that you have prepared a final opponent and noted down its appearance, motivation, and tactics.

> **TYPICAL TRAPS**
>
> **Spike Trap:** Snaps shut on an unwary adventurer who tries to open a chest or door. Inflicts 2D6 piercing damage.
>
> **Trapdoor:** A hatch in the floor opens beneath a player character. The victim must make an EVADE roll with a bane and sustains 3D6 falling damage on a failure.
>
> **Poison Dart Trap:** Darts dipped in poison shoot out from the wall, hitting all nearby player characters who fail an AWARENESS roll. The GM decides the potency and whether it is a lethal, paralyzing, or sleeping poison (page 52).

> **TYPES OF TREASURE**
>
> There are two types of treasures in *Dragonbane* – *monetary treasures* and *gear*.
>
> ✦ **Monetary treasures** consist of coins (copper, silver, or gold) or valuables without practical use, such as jewels and jewelry. As a rule, valuables are simply treated as coins – let the players automatically know their worth (in gold, silver, or copper depending on the material) and note it on their character sheets. The item itself does not need to be written down, unless it is of particular importance.
>
> ✦ **Gear** has some practical use. It can be a weapon or some other item from chapter 6. It can also be a magical item (page 62).

> **TREASURE TABLES**
>
> At the end of this book, you will find tables used to quickly generate treasures in the game. The same treasures are available on playing cards in the boxed *Dragonbane Core Set*. Some adventures indicate when you should generate a random treasure, but you can also use the tables to create your own treasures.

> **LEAVING THE ADVENTURE SITE**
>
> Leaving a cave or other dangerous place full of enemies, perhaps to make camp and take a shift rest (page 52), can be risky. Roll on the table below if the player characters leave an adventure site for at least a shift. Disregard the result if it clearly makes no sense.

## CAMPAIGNS

*Dragonbane* is all about adventures. But playing individual adventures is just the beginning. By playing a series of adventures with the same player characters, your gaming group can experience a larger story that becomes more exciting and memorable. This is called playing a *campaign*.

*The Secret of the Dragon Emperor* in the boxed *Dragonbane Core Set* is one example of a campaign, but they can take many different forms. As GM you can have lots of fun planning and playing a campaign over time, but it is often good to start simple. You do not need a grand plan to begin a campaign. Just let the player characters go on adventures and find some way to link the adventures together as you go.

### THE GAME WORLD

The world of *Dragonbane* is full of misty forests, deep ravines, steep cliffs, and forgotten caves. It is a world of adventure and danger, of ancient beasts, hidden treasures, and dark cults working in the shadows.

The Misty Vale, which is described in the *Dragonbane Core Set*, is a good starting point for your adventures. The world of *Dragonbane* is meant to be discovered and experienced in-game. Who knows what lies beyond the mountains in the north? Perhaps you have an idea about a strange spider realm that would be perfect for the player characters to visit next. If so, run with that idea, or anything else you think might be appropriate.

In future modules we will explore the game world bit by bit, but in a way that gives you the freedom to create your own adventures.

> **DEAD PLAYER CHARACTERS**
>
> *Dragonbane* is set in a dangerous world where player characters will sometimes die. Having to part with a player character can be sad, but it is part of the game and something that opens up new possibilities.
>
> When a player character dies, let the player create a new character and join the adventure at the earliest opportunity. Grant the new player character an extra advancement roll for each session you have played so far and the same number of heroic abilities as the other player characters. The exact details are left to the GM.

## THE CASTLE OF THE ROBBER KNIGHT

*"For two days you have wandered, and now you have arrived at your destination. A grassy hill towers before you. On its crest rests a ruined fortress – the Castle of the Robber Knight. The warmth of the inn's fire in the village seems distant now, but you all remember the old woman's words. Long ago, a wicked robber knight named Rothgar Wolfsbane plagued this area, pillaging and plundering wherever he could. He fought against everyone – humans, dwarves, and elves – but it was the orcs who eventually brought about his downfall. An orc clan marched on Wolfsbane's castle and stormed it. Many lives were lost that day, but in the end Wolfsbane fell and his castle was ravaged by flames. Brave fortune hunters have felt compelled to explore the robber knight's castle in search of ancient treasures, but none have ever returned. Will you be the first?"*

This adventure is a simple introduction to *Dragonbane*. Many more adventures can be found in the game's core set. If you are a player, stop reading now. The rest of the text is for the GM only.

Adventures in *Dragonbane* have no predetermined plot but are often about letting the player characters explore an interesting place. In other words, there is no set ending to The Castle of the Robber Knight.

Start by showing the picture on the previous page to the players and reading the text above it. Draw the outline of the castle using the map on page 116 and fill it in as the player characters explore the different rooms of the castle. Best of luck!

### THE SITUATION

A long time ago a brutal robber knight ravaged the area in which this adventure takes place. His name was Rothgar Wolfsbane, and he fought everyone in his path – humans, dwarves, elves, and orcs. The sound of his dreaded war horn made the blood of his enemies run cold.

So loathed was Wolfsbane that the elder folk, who for ages had been sworn enemies, put aside their feuds and united to face their common foe. Eventually, a horde of orcs laid siege to the castle where Wolfsbane was entrenched. The battle for the castle was bloody and many orcs fell to the robber knight's blade, but in the end, they overpowered him – Rothgar Wolfsbane had his head cut off by an orc scimitar while his castle was engulfed in flames. The robber knight's reign of terror was over.

Now, many years later, the sounds of battle have faded but the ruins of the robber knight's castle still stand.

On moonlit nights, passing travelers have heard the blast of Wolfsbane's war horn and seen the headless robber knight appear in ghostly form. Rumors tell of caravans and adventurers disappearing near the ruin, and nowadays most people take detours around the hill.

It is true that the robber knight walks again in his castle. But what nobody knows is that a band of goblin bandits led by the cunning Jaldo are summoning the headless wight to scare people away from the ruin. They have made the castle their own and it is from there that they set out to raid the surrounding area.

As GM, you can also have the player characters run into goblins whenever you see fit.

#### 1. OLD ROAD

*Barely visible under brush and weeds, an old paved road winds its way up the hill. At its end looms a dark, dilapidated castle ruin. A raven caws somewhere nearby.*

✦ **The Shrubbery:** The road is partially overgrown and has not been used for a long time. The goblins prefer to travel along a narrow path that runs parallel to the road. The player characters discover it if they investigate the area.
✦ **The Helmet:** Halfway up the hill is an old helmet of charred metal, partially hidden in the undergrowth. Despite its age, it is perfectly usable (armor rating +1, bane on AWARENESS rolls). The helmet looks nasty with sharp spikes sticking out of the sides. Have the characters roll MYTHS & LEGENDS – on success, they realize that the helmet was forged by orcs. Anyone who wears it will get into trouble when they encounter the robber knight's wight later in the adventure.

#### 2. GATE HOUSE

*At the end of the road rises an old castle that must once have been an impressive sight. Now it is a run-down ruin with partially collapsed walls silhouetted against the gray sky. In front of you is a broken-down gate of charred, rotten wood. The wind wails faintly as it seeps into the courtyard.*

✦ **The Gate:** The double gate of charred wood hangs open at the entrance to the courtyard.
✦ *The Snare: If the players say they examine the gate, they can make a SPOT HIDDEN roll. Success reveals a thin trip line strung at knee height across the entrance. The trip line is connected to bells on the inside of the gate. If the player characters fail to detect the trap, it will trigger when they pass through the gate and the goblins will ambush them from the tree in the courtyard (location #3).*
✦ **The Hole:** There is a dark hole just inside the entrance. The ground has collapsed into the old cellar, leaving a four-meter drop onto cold, hard stone. The player characters must succeed with ACROBATICS to get across to the courtyard. On the other side of the hole is a long plank that can be used as a makeshift bridge – anyone using it gets a boon on the roll. On failure, the player character tumbles into the cellar (location #5) and takes 2D6 bludgeoning damage. Each player character can break the fall with another ACROBATICS roll, reducing the damage to D6.

#### 3. COURTYARD

*The broken walls of the castle rise up around you, framing a courtyard filled with rocks and rubble. A gnarled old oak tree reaches for the sky from the center of the courtyard. In the northeast corner is a stone staircase leading up to a rickety old tower. To the west, an open door gapes into a stone building by the western wall. To the east there is a wooden hatch in the ground.*

✦ **The Oak Tree:** The long-dead oak tree serves as the goblins' lookout tower and is where they ambush intruding adventurers. Its branches allow the goblins to move with ease between the castle walls and other locations without having to walk on the ground.
✦ *The Hiding Goblins: If the player characters triggered the trap in the gatehouse (location #2), the goblins have prepared an ambush. They wait until the characters have explored a location in the castle and come back out into the courtyard. Then they strike by hurling themselves from the branches. Each player character may then roll AWARENESS – those who fail count as surprised and act last in the first round of combat (see the combat rules). The goblins outnumber the player characters by one. They all act on the same turn and share an initiative card.*
✦ *Treasure: If the player characters climb the oak tree, they notice something glittering in a hole in the trunk. Here the goblins have stashed some loot for unexpected expenses. Roll once on the treasure tables on page 121 to see what it is.*
✦ *The Back of the Painting: If a player character examines the painting closely or looks around the room and succeeds with SPOT HIDDEN, they will find a note on the back of the canvas. It reads: "The scoundrel Rothgar Wolfsbane made me paint this portrait of him. If I do not make it out of here alive, I curse his name for all eternity! – Embarius."*
✦ *The Sleeping Goblin: The goblin chief Jaldo has made himself a little den among the rotten tablecloths and broken crockery in the corner cupboard. He is fast asleep after the night's festivities, with an empty wine bottle and an old, unusually large drinking horn in his arms (a successful CRAFTING roll reveals that it is actually a war horn). The horn may become important later in the adventure as it can be used to summon the undead robber knight. If the player characters make too much noise, Jaldo wakes up and sneaks out through a hole to warn his henchmen, who prepare an ambush in the oak tree in the courtyard.*

**Jaldo's Sob Story:** If the player characters surprise Jaldo and wake him up, he realizes that he is outnumbered and pretends to be a poor lonely goblin who got lost and sought shelter in the castle. He quickly concocts a rambling and ill-conceived story about a brutal orc clan taking over the castle and forcing him into the cupboard. Jaldo offers to show them where the orcs are hiding – he plans to lead them to the cellar, where the not-so-violent orc Grunta is confined, and lock them in.

Jaldo has the gift of the gab and has gathered a dozen goblin followers with promises of wine, silver, and a home of their own – promises which, to his great surprise, he actually managed to keep by finding the castle and quickly realizing that he could use the legend of the robber knight to his advantage.

#### 4. GREAT HALL

*What must once have been the great hall of the castle is now a sad mess of half-rotten rafters, broken dishes, and fallen mortar. The center of the room is dominated by a long oak table, and a large painting hangs askew on the north wall. You hear a faint gurgling sound coming from a cupboard in the corner.*

✦ **The Oak Table:** This is where the robber knight and his adjutants used to eat. Now the table is covered in debris – pottery shards, old tin cups, broken wine bottles, and moldy food scraps. The latter indicates that someone has been eating here recently. A successful CRAFTING roll reveals that the pottery shards and wine bottles are contemporary items of halfling origin.
✦ **The Portrait:** A large painting hangs askew on the north wall. It depicts a stern man with evil gray eyes, a wicked grin, and raven hair swept behind his head. He wears a full suit of armor and carries a horned great helm under his arm.
✦ *The Back of the Painting: If a player character examines the painting closely or looks around the room and succeeds with SPOT HIDDEN, they will find a note on the back of the canvas. It reads: "The scoundrel Rothgar Wolfsbane made me paint this portrait of him. If I do not make it out of here alive, I curse his name for all eternity! – Embarius."*

#### 5. CELLAR

*Below the hatch is a steep stone staircase leading underground. It is damp and smells of earth. The stairs end in a large space with stacks of barrels lining the eastern wall. The southern part of the room has several alcoves that the torchlight cannot reach. In a corner sits a large orc who turns to you in surprise.*

✦ **The Hatch:** The wooden hatch to the cellar is not locked when the player characters arrive, but it is bolted from the outside. The hatch has armor rating 8 but can be forced open with 20 points of damage.
✦ **Grunta:** In the darkness of the cellar lives the orc Grunta, cast out from her clan and used by Jaldo and his goblin bandits. Grunta's only job is to blow the robber knight's horn and summon his wight on Jaldo's command (the goblins' lungs are too weak, so there is little more than a pitiful squeak when they try to blow the horn). Grunta is deeply unhappy and wants to leave the castle but does not dare for fear of Jaldo. Her only joy in life is Merle the pig.
✦ *The Skeleton: If the player characters investigate the room and succeed with SPOT HIDDEN, they find a skeleton hidden under the rubble. Rusty chains reveal that the deceased was chained to a wall. Searching through the bones, they find a rolled-up piece of parchment with a sketched self-portrait of a young man, titled "The artist Embarius in his youth".*
✦ **The Goblin Den:** The alcoves along the southern wall are equipped with simple mattresses made of straw and sticks. Unless the alarm has been raised, there are goblins (one more than the player characters) resting here.
✦ **Treasures:** In one of the alcoves, the goblins keep some stolen goods of little value. Player characters who poke around a bit and succeed with SPOT HIDDEN can find something valuable – roll once on the treasure tables on page 121 to see what it is.

> **GRUNTA**
>
> Grunta is an orc without a home or purpose. Cast out of her clan, she wandered the lands in search of a new one. Her constant companion is Merle the pig, whom she rescued from a hungry wolfman. They have been inseparable ever since. When she met Jaldo and his goblins, Grunta was blinded by the promise of new friends and fell for his lies. Now she is stuck in the cellar because she is too afraid to leave – afraid of Jaldo and the robber knight's wight and that the goblins might eat her beloved pig. If there was a chance to escape with Merle, she would take it.

**THE ETERNAL WATCH OF ROTHGAR WOLFSBANE**

When Rothgar Wolfsbane fell during the orcs' siege of the castle many centuries ago, only his corporeal form died. Despite losing his head, Wolfsbane was so filled with hatred and cursed by so many that he was doomed to eternal undeath. Since then, he has been patrolling the castle as a headless wight, forever looking for his mortal enemies: orcs.

The robber knight can materialize at the GM's discretion, but the wight can also be summoned by someone with strong lungs (STR 14 or higher) blowing Jaldo's war horn (location #4). The wight attacks all living creatures except the person carrying the horn. Orcs or people wearing the orc helmet (location #1) are attacked first.

With a little bit of cunning, the player characters can convince the undead robber knight that the goblins are in fact tiny orcs (this wight is so blinded by its hatred of orcs that it can be PERSUADED with a successful skill roll). If they succeed, the characters can just sit back and enjoy their brilliant idea while the wight makes short work of the goblins.

There are three ways to overcome the robber knight:

✦ Defeating it in combat. However, the robber knight rises again after one shift (about six hours) and continues to haunt the castle.
✦ Crushing the robber knight's skull at the bottom of the tower (#6). If this happens, the wight runs amok for two rounds (its Ferocity increases to 3) before collapsing in a pile of bones. Then the entire castle begins to shake and collapse. All player characters must make an EVADE roll (not an action) every round they remain inside the castle walls. On the first failure they take D6 bludgeoning damage, the second failure inflicts 2D6 damage, and so on.
✦ Delivering the skull to the headless wight. If this happens, the wight stops and puts its head in place. Then it lets out a long sigh and collapses as described above. The castle crumbles in the same way.

#### 6. TOWER

*What must once have been a mighty stone tower is now just a hollow shell. The floors above have collapsed, and the roof is long gone. All that remains are fallen stones, mossy stone walls, and a stone staircase that winds its way down, partly covered in rubble and debris.*

✦ **The Ravens:** A flock of ravens has nested high up in the tower. Where the third floor once was there is now only a small stone ledge protruding from the wall. A player character who succeeds with an ACROBATICS roll can climb up to the ravens' nest. Roll once on the treasure tables on page 121 to see what's there.
✦ **The Stairs:** The way down is almost completely blocked by stones from the collapsed floors, but it is possible to get past the rubble by crouching and moving carefully.
✦ *Hoofprints: A successful SPOT HIDDEN roll reveals the muddy hoofprints of a pig leading down the stairs.*
✦ **Falling Boulder:** When the player characters are halfway down the stairs, a large stone further up comes loose and bounces towards them. Have everyone make an EVADE roll – those who fail are pushed forward and end up in the water at the end of the stairs, taking D8 bludgeoning damage.
✦ **The Pig:** The staircase disappears into black water at the bottom level of the tower. Decades of rainwater have turned what was once a wine cellar into a deep well. On a ledge by the water, Merle the pig has made himself a nest. If the player characters have not yet encountered Merle (see random encounter #2), he is resting in the nest and greets the adventurers with a surprised grunt.
✦ **The Helmet:** If the player characters peer down into the depths, they see something shimmering in the black water. The well is four meters deep and it takes a successful SWIMMING roll to reach the bottom and find the treasure. Roll twice on the treasure tables on page 121 to see what it is. There is also a grinning skull inside a rusty, horned helmet – this is the skull of Rothgar Wolfsbane, which can be used to banish the wight. If the player characters have seen the portrait in the great hall (#4), they immediately recognize the helmet that the robber knight carried under his arm. The helmet has armor rating +2 and a bane on AWARENESS rolls and ranged attacks.
