# Chapter 8: Adventures

## Journeys

Adventures often require characters to travel long distances through diverse terrain. This section covers movement, time, and hazards encountered during travel.

### Distance and Speed

Characters travel at different speeds depending on their mode of travel and terrain:
- **Walking** — 20 miles per day (normal terrain)
- **Mounted** — 30 miles per day (normal terrain)
- **Ship** — 50 miles per day (water)
- **Caravan** — 10 miles per day (overland with supplies)

### Terrain Effects

Different terrain types affect movement speed:
- **Forest** — 50% movement
- **Mountains** — 50% movement
- **Swamp** — 25% movement
- **Desert** — 75% movement
- **Road** — 100% movement

### Hazards on the Road

Travelers face various dangers including:
- Bandits and brigands
- Wild animals
- Extreme weather
- Treacherous terrain
- Magical obstacles

## The Gamemaster's Role

The Game Master (GM) serves as the referee, narrator, and controller of all non-player characters and monsters.

### Responsibilities

The GM is responsible for:
- Describing the environment and situation
- Adjudicating the rules
- Creating and controlling NPCs and monsters
- Tracking initiative and time
- Making rulings not covered by the rules
- Setting difficulty classes for skill checks

### Running Sessions

A typical session might last 2-4 hours and cover one or more encounters, journeys, or story beats.

### Adjusting Difficulty

The GM can adjust encounter difficulty by:
- Adding or removing enemies
- Changing enemy statistics
- Altering terrain or environment
- Providing additional resources to players

## Non-Player Characters

Non-player characters (NPCs) are individuals in the world controlled by the GM.

### Creating NPCs

A complete NPC typically has:
- Name and appearance
- Personality and motivations
- Skills and abilities
- Goals and secrets
- Connection to the party or adventure

### NPC Roles

NPCs serve various roles in the game:
- **Allies** — Characters who help the party
- **Rivals** — Characters who compete with the party
- **Enemies** — Characters who oppose the party
- **Patrons** — Characters who hire the party for quests
- **Questgivers** — Characters who provide hooks for adventures

### Reaction and Interaction

NPCs react to the party based on:
- How the party treats them
- Their personality
- Current circumstances
- Existing relationships

## Creating Adventures

Adventures are the stories and quests that the characters undertake.

### Adventure Structure

A typical adventure has:
- **Hook** — A reason for the party to get involved
- **Setup** — The initial situation or problem
- **Development** — Complications and obstacles
- **Climax** — The main challenge or confrontation
- **Resolution** — How the adventure concludes

### Types of Adventures

**Dungeon Crawl** — A confined area with multiple rooms and encounters  
**Wilderness Trek** — Travel through dangerous terrain  
**Urban Intrigue** — Political maneuvering and espionage in a city  
**Mystery** — Solving a puzzle or investigating a crime  
**Heist** — Planning and executing a theft or elaborate scheme  
**Rescue Mission** — Saving someone or something important

### Encounter Design

Encounters are individual challenges the party faces:
- **Combat Encounter** — Fighting enemies
- **Social Encounter** — Negotiation or roleplay
- **Exploration Encounter** — Discovering locations or items
- **Environmental Encounter** — Surviving natural hazards

### Difficulty Ratings

Encounters have difficulty ratings:
- **Easy** — Party has advantages or weak enemies
- **Medium** — Balanced encounter
- **Hard** — Strong enemies or disadvantages for the party
- **Deadly** — Potentially lethal encounter

## Campaigns

A campaign is a series of connected adventures forming a larger story.

### Campaign Structure

Campaigns often follow an arc:
1. **Opening** — Establishing the world and party
2. **Rising Action** — Complications and consequences
3. **Climax** — The main conflict comes to a head
4. **Resolution** — Wrapping up loose ends

### Long-Term Goals

Campaigns often feature:
- Overarching villains or threats
- Political intrigue and alliances
- Character development and personal quests
- World-changing events

### Tracking Progress

Use tools to track:
- Character development
- Relationship changes
- Recovered treasure and resources
- Completed quests and objectives
- World status and changes

## The Castle of the Robber Knight

This sample adventure serves as an introduction to running adventures and creating encounters.

### Adventure Overview

The party is hired to investigate and neutralize a bandit lord who has been terrorizing the local region from his fortress, the Castle of the Robber Knight.

### Setup

- The party is hired by the regional lord or town council
- They are given information about the bandit lord and his forces
- They may receive a reward for capturing or defeating the bandit lord
- They need to plan an approach to the castle

### The Castle

The Castle of the Robber Knight is a medium-sized fortress with:
- Outer walls and gatehouse
- Main castle structure with multiple towers
- Barracks for the bandit forces
- Treasury and storage
- Living quarters

### Inhabitants

The castle is home to:
- **The Robber Knight** — A powerful warrior with many mercenary allies
- **Mercenaries** — Trained soldiers loyal to the knight
- **Servants** — Non-combatants who maintain the castle
- **Prisoners** — Captured travelers and noble hostages

### Encounters

The party encounters various challenges:
- **Gate Guards** — Defending the castle entrance
- **Mercenary Patrols** — Armed soldiers
- **The Treasury** — Trapped and guarded
- **The Robber Knight** — The final confrontation
- **Escape** — Getting out with the treasure and/or prisoners

### Variations

GMs can modify the adventure by:
- Adding more or fewer guards
- Creating additional complications (allies of the knight arrive)
- Allowing alternative solutions (stealth, diplomacy, infiltration)
- Providing additional treasure or treasure
- Creating moral dilemmas for the party

### Rewards

Successful completion might reward:
- **Treasure** — Gold and magical items
- **Allies** — Freed prisoners or grateful nobles
- **Land or Title** — Recognition for services rendered
- **Information** — Clues to larger mysteries
- **Experience** — Advancement and new abilities
