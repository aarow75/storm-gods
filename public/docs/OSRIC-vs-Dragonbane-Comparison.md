# OSRIC vs. Dragonbane — Mechanics Comparison

*Comparing OSRIC (Old School Reference and Index Compilation, v2.2) against the Dragonbane Core Rulebook (Free League, 2023). Abbreviations: OS = OSRIC, DB = Dragonbane.*

---

## 1. Combat

### The Fundamental Difference

Both games use d20s and produce fast, dangerous combat — but they diverge on nearly every foundational design choice:

- **OS:** Traditional two-sided combat. Both the attacker and the defender are implicitly present in every exchange — monsters roll their attack dice against player AC; players roll to hit monster AC. Armor Class is an abstraction that represents how hard you are to damage; better armor makes you harder to hit outright.
- **DB:** Player-facing combat. Players always roll — both to attack and to defend. Monsters never roll to attack; instead, players roll their EVADE or weapon skill against a fixed difficulty (15 for standard monsters). Armor reduces damage after it lands rather than preventing hits.

This player-facing/traditional split produces a completely different table experience. OSRIC players can be passive during enemy turns (waiting to see if the monster hits). DB players are always active — they respond to every monster attack with a roll, creating more engagement and more tension around individual defenses.

Both games use d20, but in opposite directions: OSRIC rolls high (≥ AC target); DB rolls low (≤ skill).

### Combat Comparison Table

| Topic | OSRIC | Dragonbane |
|---|---|---|
| Core mechanic | d20 + to-hit bonus ≥ AC target (roll high) | d20 ≤ skill (roll-under) |
| Monster attacks | Monsters roll d20 normally; players are passive defenders | No monster attack roll — players roll EVADE or weapon vs. difficulty 15 |
| Armor abstraction | Sets AC (10 = unarmored; lower = better; plate = AC 3) | Flat AR subtracted from damage (leather = 1, plate = 6) |
| Hit locations | None — single HP pool per creature | None — single HP pool per creature |
| Shield | +1 to AC | +1 AR (or weapon parry — separate defense roll) |
| Critical hit | None in core rules | Dragon (1) → D6 on critical effects table |
| Fumble | None in core rules | Demon (20) → D6 on fumble effects table |
| Boon / Advantage | Situational bonuses to the d20 roll | Boon (roll 2d20 take best) / Bane (roll 2d20 take worst) |
| Initiative | Both sides roll d6; higher number acts first | Draw card 1–10; lower card acts first (granular, per character) |
| Round length | 1 minute (10 segments of 6 seconds) | 10 seconds |
| Actions per round | 1 attack (fighters gain extra attacks at levels 7+) | 1 action + 1 fast action (or 2 fast actions) |
| Dying | 0 HP = unconscious (lose 1 HP/round); −10 HP = dead | 0 HP = Broken; d20 death roll each round; ally can rally with HEALING |
| Instant death | Any damage while unconscious | Single hit ≥ maximum HP |
| Conditions | No formal condition track | 6 named conditions (Exhausted, Sickly, Dazed, Angry, Scared, Grievous Wound) |
| Two-weapon fighting | −2 primary / −4 off-hand; off-hand limited to dagger/hatchet | Any weapon can parry in DB; no specific two-weapon rule |

### Key Tactical Differences

**Who rolls for defense.** This is the most dramatic difference. In OSRIC, when a goblin attacks, the GM rolls d20 against the player's AC — the player takes the hit or doesn't. In DB, when a goblin attacks, the player rolls their EVADE skill — they are always in control of their own defense. DB players feel more agency; OSRIC players experience the authentic randomness of "the dice are against me" when an enemy roll goes well.

**Armor function.** OSRIC armor prevents hits entirely (lower AC = less likely to be hit at all). DB armor only reduces damage after a hit lands. A plate-armored OSRIC fighter rarely gets struck; a plate-armored DB fighter gets struck often but loses few HP when they are. The OSRIC model rewards heavy armor as a hit-prevention system; the DB model makes armor a survival buffer rather than an avoidance tool.

**Initiative granularity.** OSRIC's d6 per-side initiative means "my side acts, then your side acts" — 6 possible results, and ties are simultaneous. DB's card draw assigns each character a specific acting position (1–10), with lower cards acting first. In a crowded combat, OSRIC initiative is simple but flat; DB initiative creates meaningful order questions (does the wizard draw before or after the enemy archer?).

**Round length and its implications.** OSRIC's 1-minute round represents a prolonged series of feints, parries, and exchanges — "one attack roll" abstracts a full minute of melee. DB's 10-second round means each action is one concrete swing or spell. OSRIC magic can have casting times of 1–9 segments within the minute; DB spells fire as your action in a 10-second round with no segment subdivision.

**The 100% skill and skill ceiling.** DB skills max at 20 (effectively). High-skill DB characters are reliable but not categorically dominant. OSRIC fighters at level 13+ make 2 attacks per round — genuinely different in kind from level 1. There is no equivalent acceleration in DB.

## 2. Character Advancement

### Advancement Comparison Table

| Topic | OSRIC | Dragonbane |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Class/level (11 classes; racial level caps) | No classes; individual skill advancement |
| XP trigger | 1 GP recovered = 1 XP; monster XP by HD | Roll Dragon (1) on a trained skill during play |
| Advancement roll | None — accumulate XP to class threshold | End of session: roll d20 *higher* than current skill level → +1 |
| Training required | Yes: ~1,500 gp and 1d4 weeks per level | No training costs or time requirement |
| HP progression | Roll class HD + CON modifier each level (caps at level 9–11) | No HP advancement (HP = CON score, fixed) |
| Combat power curve | Steep: level 10 fighter is vastly more durable and effective than level 1 | Gradual: high skill is more reliable, not exponentially more powerful |
| Henchmen / followers | Cha governs max henchmen; rules for recruiting, paying, and motivating | No formal hireling or follower rules |
| Alignment tracking | Required; some class abilities depend on it | No alignment system |

### Rate of Advancement Compared

**OSRIC:** A starting fighter needs 2,000 XP for level 2. A dungeon haul of 1,000 GP yields 1,000 XP. With a party of 4, each member gets 250 XP from that haul. Add monster XP and the fighter might reach level 2 after 3–5 sessions. Training costs 1,500 gp per level — significant at low levels. Advancement is fairly rapid at early levels, then slows dramatically.

**DB:** A Swords skill at 10 improves when: (a) the character rolls a Dragon (1) on Swords during play (5% per roll), then (b) rolls above 10 on d20 at session end (50% chance). Combined: ~2.5% per session the skill sees use. Expected sessions to improve: ~40. DB advancement is extremely slow by comparison — meaningful increases take many sessions.

OSRIC creates tiers of power that make level matter enormously. DB creates gradual improvement where advancement is so slow it barely registers session-to-session.

## 3. Magic

### Magic System Comparison Table

| Topic | OSRIC (Arcane and Divine) | Dragonbane (Magic Schools) |
|---|---|---|
| Access | Class-gated: Magic User, Illusionist, Cleric, Druid, Paladin (9+), Ranger (8+) | Any character may learn any magic school skill |
| Resource | Spell slots per day (class + level) | Willpower Points (WP = WIL score) |
| Casting roll | None — spells never fail if cast without interruption | Yes: d20 ≤ magic school skill |
| Failure | Only if interrupted during casting | No effect; WP still spent |
| Critical/Fumble | Not applicable | Dragon (1) = maximum effect; Demon (20) = Magical Mishap table |
| Resource recovery | Full recovery after 8 hours sleep | d6 WP after 15 min rest; full WP after 6-hour shift rest |
| Memorization | Arcane: choose spells from spell book each morning; Clerics: automatically granted | No memorization; WP budget limits casting volume |
| New spells | Arcane: find scrolls/books; Int-based chance to comprehend; limited spells/level | Train magic school skill (Dragon + session roll, same as all skills) |
| Spell levels | Arcane: 9 levels; Cleric: 7 levels | Power Levels 1–3 (cost 2/4/6 WP) |
| Armor restriction | No armor restriction for clerics; arcane: no armor allowed for Magic Users/Illusionists | Metal armor gives bane on all magic school rolls |
| Counter-magic | *Dispel Magic* spell; *Protection from Evil*; *Counterspell* | *Dispel* (General Magic) |
| Divine vs. arcane | Two separate systems (divine spells require deity faith; arcane require study) | No divine/arcane distinction; all schools treated equally |

### Key Practical Differences

**Reliability vs. risk.** OSRIC magic is reliable: choose spell, memorize it, cast it, it works (unless interrupted). DB magic carries real risk: the caster rolls their school skill; rolling a Demon (20) triggers a Mishap that could backfire catastrophically. DB mages are gambling every time they cast; OSRIC mages are executing a prepared resource.

**WP vs. spell slots.** DB WP is flexible — any combination of Power Level 1, 2, and 3 spells up to the WP total. OSRIC spell slots are by level — a 3rd-level Magic User with two 2nd-level slots and one 1st-level slot can only cast specific spells in those slots. DB gives more casting flexibility within its daily limit; OSRIC requires more strategic preparation.

**Recovery timing.** DB WP recovers after 15 minutes of rest (d6 WP) and fully after a night's sleep (~6 hours). OSRIC spells recover only after a full 8 hours of sleep. For a DB mage, a short break might restore 3–4 WP; for an OSRIC mage, a short rest restores nothing. DB magic is far more available per adventure day.

**The memorize-and-forget structure** (OSRIC only) means OSRIC mages must predict what spells they'll need. A Magic User who memorized *Sleep* instead of *Fireball* against a fire-immune enemy has no recourse until the next day. DB mages choose spells freely from their known list at casting time, limited only by WP.

## 4. Coinage, Encumbrance, and Movement

### Coinage

| System | Denominations | Base unit |
|---|---|---|
| OSRIC | CP, SP, EP, GP, PP (5 denominations) | Gold Piece (GP); 1 GP = 1 XP |
| Dragonbane | CP, SP, GP (3 denominations) | Silver Piece; 10 CP = 1 SP, 10 SP = 1 GP |

Both systems use GP/SP/CP, but OSRIC also has Electrum Pieces (EP) and Platinum Pieces (PP). The exchange rates are compatible:
- OSRIC: 1 GP = 10 SP = 100 CP; 1 PP = 5 GP
- DB: 1 GP = 10 SP = 100 CP (same structure)

The key difference is OSRIC's GP = XP mechanic. In DB, gold has no XP value — characters don't "level up" from wealth.

**Price comparison:**

| Item | OSRIC price | DB price |
|---|---|---|
| Dagger | 2 gp | 5–10 SP |
| Long sword | 15 gp | 50 SP |
| Chain mail | 75 gp | 80 SP |
| Plate armor | 400 gp | (No equivalent in DB core; Full Plate ≈ 200–400 SP) |
| War horse | 200–500+ gp | 500 SP |

The sword comparison gives **1 OSRIC GP ≈ 3–4 DB SP** for standard equipment. Chainmail prices diverge slightly (75 gp vs. 80 SP), nearly 1:1 in this case. Use **1 OSRIC GP ≈ 3–4 DB SP** as a baseline.

### Encumbrance

| Topic | OSRIC | Dragonbane |
|---|---|---|
| Unit | Pounds | Item count (each = 1 slot) |
| Maximum | 150 lbs + Str bonus; armor independently limits move | STR ÷ 2 items (rounded up) |
| Penalty type | Stepped: weight determines movement rate (120/90/60/30 ft) | Binary: over limit → bane on all physical rolls |
| Armor movement cap | Yes: e.g., chainmail limits max move to 90 ft regardless of weight | No; only item count matters |

### Movement

| Topic | OSRIC | Dragonbane |
|---|---|---|
| Base movement (human) | 120 ft/round (combat) | 10m per fast action; 20m on a full Dash |
| Scale | Feet (absolute) | Meters (literal on a map) |
| Mounted (combat) | War horse: 240 ft/round | Requires RIDING check in combat; no defined rate |
| Long distance | Not detailed in core | Not defined (GM-implied) |
| Small race penalty | Dwarves/gnomes/halflings: −30 ft from all rates | AGL modifier adjusts movement ±4m |

At combat scale, converting OSRIC feet to DB meters: 120 ft ≈ 36 m ≈ 3–4 DB movement actions per round. The games assume similar-speed human combatants; the difference is OSRIC's granular foot-measurement vs. DB's fast-action movement mechanics.

## 5. Conversion Guidelines

Use these when porting creatures, characters, or encounters between systems.

### Attack Conversion

OSRIC to-hit requirements translate to DB skill as follows:

| OSRIC Roll Needed | Success % | ≈ DB Skill Equivalent |
|-------------------|-----------|----------------------|
| 20 (exactly) | 5% | 1 |
| 17+ | 20% | 4 |
| 14+ | 35% | 7 |
| 11+ | 50% | 10 |
| 8+ | 65% | 13 |
| 5+ | 80% | 16 |
| 2+ | 95% | 19 |

*Multiply success% by 20 to get approximate DB skill. Reverse by dividing DB skill by 20 to get success%, then find the matching d20 roll needed.*

### Armor Conversion

OSRIC AC and DB AR work in opposite directions:

| OSRIC Armor | AC | DB Equivalent | AR |
|-------------|-----|---------------|-----|
| No armor | 10 | No armor | 0 |
| Leather | 8 | Leather | 1 |
| Studded leather | 7 | Studded Leather | 2 |
| Chain mail | 5 | Chain Mail | 3 |
| Plate mail | 3 | Plate Armor | 4 |
| Full plate + shield | 1 | Full Plate + Shield | 6+1 |

*Approximate formula: DB AR ≈ (10 − OSRIC AC) ÷ 2. Round to nearest whole.*

### HP Conversion

| Level | OSRIC HP range | DB HP range |
|-------|---------------|-------------|
| Level 1 / Starting | 4–10 | 10–14 (CON score) |
| Level 5 | 15–40 | 10–14 (unchanged) |
| Level 10 | 40–80+ | 10–14 (unchanged) |

DB HP does not scale with experience. When converting OSRIC creatures to DB:
- **OSRIC level 1–3:** Use HP directly (ranges overlap)
- **OSRIC level 4–7:** Halve the OSRIC HP for DB
- **OSRIC level 8+:** Use ¼ OSRIC HP, or treat as a boss creature with extra conditions

### Monster Conversion

**OSRIC → DB:**
1. HD × 4.5 = average HP; use as DB HP pool
2. Convert THAC0 to DB skill using the attack table above (assign to EVADE difficulty for player rolls)
3. Convert AC to DB AR using the armor table
4. Replace any OSRIC saving throw with a DB attribute roll (STR check for physical, INT for mental, etc.)

**DB → OSRIC:**
1. Use DB HP as level 1–2 OSRIC HP
2. Convert DB EVADE skill ÷ 5 to THAC0 (20 − result = target roll needed)
3. Convert DB AR × 2 = OSRIC equivalent AC reduction from 10

### Magic Conversion

| OSRIC Spell Level | ≈ DB Equivalent |
|------------------|-----------------|
| 1st level | Power Level 1 (2 WP) |
| 2nd level | Power Level 1–2 (2–4 WP) |
| 3rd level | Power Level 2 (4 WP) |
| 4th–5th level | Power Level 3 (6 WP) |
| 6th–9th level | Power Level 3 (6 WP); treat as high-impact scene-enders |

**Resource note:** A DB character with WP 12 can cast ~6 Power Level 1 spells per day. An OSRIC Magic User at level 5 has roughly 4 × 1st-level, 2 × 2nd-level, and 1 × 3rd-level slot. The total castings are similar; DB's flexibility vs. OSRIC's strategic preparation is the key difference.

### Conditions → OSRIC Wound Penalties

When running DB's condition system in OSRIC (e.g., for players who prefer conditions over hit-point damage):

| DB Condition | Linked Attribute | OSRIC Equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Exhausted | AGL | −10% to movement; −2 to all Dex-based rolls |
| Sickly | CON | −2 to HP/level rolls; −2 to Con checks |
| Dazed | INT | −2 to all Intelligence checks; disoriented |
| Angry | CHA | −2 to reaction rolls; must attack most threatening foe |
| Scared | WIL | −2 to saves vs. fear; cannot willingly approach the source |
| Grievous Wound | STR | −4 to hit; treat as if Strength reduced by 4 |

| 1 Silver Piece | 3–4 Silver Pieces |
| 1 Gold Piece | 3–4 Gold Pieces (or 30–40 Silver Pieces) |
