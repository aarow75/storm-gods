# The Grand Duchy of Karameikos

*An OSRIC Campaign Setting*

---

## The Land

Karameikos is a hard-won wilderness on the southeastern coast of the Known World — a vast, forested frontier wedged between the warm waters of the Sea of Dread to the south and the jagged peaks of the Altan Tepes mountains to the north and east. It is a place of ancient ruins half-swallowed by forest, river roads connecting scattered towns, and wild country where humanoid raiders and worse prey on anyone foolish or brave enough to leave the main roads.

The duchy's terrain is defined by three features. **The great forest** covers more than half the land — the Dymrak in the east, the Radlebb in the west, and the unnamed stands of old-growth between. These forests are not merely inconvenient; they are actively hostile. The trees are old enough to have watched empires rise and fall. The things living among them have done the same. **The rivers** are the duchy's real roads: the Highreach running south from the mountains through Threshold to the capital, the Windrush cutting west across the forest heart. Where the rivers go, settlers follow. Where the rivers do not go, only rangers and fools venture. **The mountains** seal the north and east, the Altan Tepes forming a natural wall broken only by the Black Eagle Pass and the Castellan Road. The mountains are not empty. They have never been empty.

The climate is mild near the southern coast, turning cold and wet as elevation rises. Specularum, the capital, enjoys Mediterranean weather nine months of the year. Threshold, a week's ride north, experiences harsh winters and spring flooding when the Highreach swells with snowmelt. The mountain foothills see snow from October through April.

### The Two Peoples

Karameikos is two civilizations occupying the same ground, uneasily.

The **Traladarans** are the original people of this land — dark-haired, olive-skinned, passionate and proud. They have lived in the forests and river valleys for over a thousand years, since the collapse of their ancient kingdom of Traldar. Traladaran culture prizes oral tradition, communal loyalty, and the memory of old grievances. They worship the Traladaran Three — the divine heroes Halav, Petra, and Zirchev, who in legend saved early Traldar from the gnoll hordes of King Wogar. The Traladarans never forgot what it cost to survive. They have not forgotten, either, that when Duke Stefan Karameikos arrived fifty years ago and declared their homeland a duchy of the Thyatian Empire, no one asked their opinion.

The **Thyatians** are colonists from the dominant power to the east — fair to olive complexioned, militarily disciplined, administratively sophisticated. Stefan Karameikos brought Thyatian nobles, soldiers, and merchants with him when he purchased the deed to this territory from the Emperor. They hold the government positions, run the major mercantile houses, and staff the Ducal Guard. Many are second-generation settlers, born in Karameikos, who know no other home. They consider themselves Karameikans. The Traladarans do not always agree.

Between these two peoples runs a current of low-grade tension. Intermarriage is increasingly common among younger generations. The merchant class is heavily mixed. But property disputes, religious friction, and competition for the Duke's favor keep old resentments alive. Player characters dropped into this conflict will find no clean sides — only people trying to live well under difficult circumstances.

## The Church

Religion in Karameikos is a political fact as much as a spiritual one.

### The Church of Karameikos

The Thyatian faith, now official religion of the duchy. Its temples are well-built stone structures in every town. Its priests are organized, educated, and connected to the ducal government. The Church venerates the Immortals — cosmic powers above the gods, who have ascended beyond mortality to guide the affairs of the world. Principal Immortals include Halav (justice and warfare — the Thyatians absorbed the Traladaran divine hero into their own pantheon, which the Traladarans find deeply offensive), Petra (protection and community), and Zirchev (nature and hunting).

The Church of Karameikos maintains hospitals, schools, and orphanages in every major settlement. Its healing priests are genuinely indispensable. This institutional good makes the Church difficult to dismiss even for those who find its politics distasteful.

**Key figure: Patriarch Olliver Jowett.** A large, genial man of seventy who has served in Karameikos for forty years. Jowett is not a politician's priest — he genuinely believes in reconciliation between Thyatian and Traladaran communities. His moderate position infuriates hardliners on both sides.

### The Church of Traladara

The old faith, maintained by Traladaran communities across the duchy. Its theology centers on the Three — Halav, Petra, and Zirchev as mortal heroes who became divine through sacrifice. Traladaran temples are smaller, less formal structures, often located in community halls or converted houses. The clergy are village-rooted rather than hierarchically organized.

The Church of Traladara exists in a legal grey zone. Duke Stefan has not banned it, but he has not granted it official status. Thyatian nobles sometimes pressure local sheriffs to restrict Traladaran religious gatherings. In the countryside, the old faith is simply the faith; in the cities, Traladaran worshippers sometimes meet quietly.

**Key figure: Magda Marilenev.** Matriarch of the Marilenev Traladaran noble family, informal leader of Traladaran community institutions in Specularum. A woman of considerable intelligence and controlled fury who has spent thirty years playing the Thyatian political game without surrendering an inch of what she believes belongs to her people.

## Factions

### The Ducal Government

Duke Stefan Aleksander Karameikos III is, by any objective measure, a remarkable man. He traded his family's entire Thyatian estate — wealth accumulated over generations — for the deed to this wilderness. He has spent the fifty years since building it into a functioning state. He is genuinely idealistic about creating a new nation where Thyatian and Traladaran peoples live as equals under a fair law. He is also a Thyatian aristocrat of the old school who is temperamentally inclined to solve problems through force and has a blind spot a mile wide about the perceptions of people who did not choose to be governed by him.

The Ducal Council handles day-to-day governance. The **Ducal Guard** provides military and constabulary functions in the towns. The **Duke's Road Wardens** patrol the King's Road and Castellan Road — the two maintained highways connecting major settlements. Outside these corridors, order is aspirational.

**What the Duke wants:** A stable, prosperous duchy. Recognition from the Emperor that Karameikos is a real nation, not a colonial holding. His children — Adriana, Justin, and Stephan the younger — to have something worth inheriting.

**What threatens the Duke:** The Iron Ring (embarrasses him internationally), the Black Eagle Barony (his treacherous cousin), Traladaran separatist sentiment, and the constant drain of frontier warfare against humanoid raiders.

### The Black Eagle Barony

The western frontier, theoretically under the Duke's authority, is in practice controlled by Baron Ludwig von Hendriks — the Duke's cousin, a man of profound and cheerful cruelty. Fort Doom, the barony's capital, is an accurate name. Hendriks taxes his peasant population into starvation, uses captured Traladarans as slave labor, and cooperates actively with the Iron Ring. His Guard is better-equipped than the Duke's.

The Duke knows. His position is genuinely trapped: moving openly against his own cousin would fracture the Thyatian noble coalition that makes the duchy governable. Waiting allows Ludwig's abuses to continue. The Duke has settled for a policy of quiet non-acknowledgment that satisfies nobody and produces results ranging from grim to catastrophic for those living under Hendriks' rule.

**What Ludwig wants:** Wealth, power, and entertainment. He collects rare weapons, exotic animals, and other people's suffering.

**What Ludwig fears:** Proof that reaches the Emperor directly. A unified Traladaran uprising. His former captain Bargle the Infamous doing something that implicates him at an inopportune moment.

**Adventure use:** The Black Eagle Barony is a reliable source of villains, rescue missions, and moral complexity. Ludwig is not stupid — he knows how close the line is and mostly stays behind it. His henchmen do not always exercise the same care.

### The Iron Ring

A criminal organization operating throughout the Known World, with particular density in Karameikos. The Iron Ring's primary business is slavery: abducting Traladarans from frontier villages, marching them through the Dymrak under armed guard, and selling them to buyers in Thyatis and beyond.

The Iron Ring is not a monolith. It is a franchise of loosely affiliated operators held together by mutual profit and a shared marking system — a stylized chain link branded or tattooed on agents and property alike. Local cells operate with considerable autonomy. The organization has corrupted officials in at least three towns, maintains safehouses throughout the duchy, and uses caves in the Altan Tepes as transit points.

**What the Iron Ring wants:** Profit. Invisibility. The continued absence of anyone willing to seriously investigate their Specularum connections.

**Adventure use:** The Iron Ring provides a recurring villainous infrastructure. A rescued slave leads to a safehouse. A safehouse reveals a corrupt magistrate. A corrupt magistrate connects to a shipping house. The chain is long and goes somewhere uncomfortable.

### The Veiled Society

Specularum's answer to organized crime. Unlike the Iron Ring (which is external and extractive), the Veiled Society is local and embedded — it grew out of Traladaran community networks that provided mutual aid when Thyatian law was unreliable. Over generations it calcified into a protection racket, debt enforcement operation, and occasional murder-for-hire service. The current leadership has Traladaran patriot sympathies layered over mundane criminality.

The Veiled Society and the Iron Ring are competitors who occasionally cooperate when neither can avoid it. The Veiled Society does not deal in slaves; this is their one firm principle, and the Iron Ring resents it as lost revenue.

**What the Veiled Society wants:** Control of Specularum's lower city. Political protection for Traladaran communities. Eventually — though this goal is kept quiet — to make the Marilenev family the ruling power in a Traladaran-majority city.

### The Thyatian Merchant Houses

Three major Thyatian merchant families — the Radu, the Torenescu, and the Vorloi — control the bulk of Specularum's trade. They are in constant competition with each other and in cautious cooperation against any threat to the overall system that benefits them. Each has its own relationship with the Veiled Society (the Radu pay them; the Torenescu fight them; the Vorloi pretend they don't exist). Each has a representative on the Duke's Trade Council.

**Adventure use:** Merchant house disputes are an endless source of courier missions, warehouse investigations, suspicious cargoes, and "find out what the other house is planning" espionage work.

## Key Locations

### Specularum (The Capital)

A port city of roughly 50,000 people, built where the Highreach meets the Sea of Dread. Specularum is cosmopolitan, bustling, and stratified. The **Merchant Quarter** crowds the harbor with warehouses, exchange houses, and taverns catering to sailors from a dozen nations. The **Old Quarter** — the original Traladaran settlement — is dense, labyrinthine, and Veiled Society territory. The **Stronghold** sits on high ground above the harbor, housing the Duke's Court, the Ducal Guard barracks, and the official residences of the major noble families.

Specularum is the best place in Karameikos to find specialists: sages, healers, weapon-smiths who know what they're doing, merchants who can source unusual materials. It is also the best place to make enemies worth having.

**Notable locations within Specularum:**
- **The Merchant's Rest** — an upscale inn in the Merchant Quarter, where ship captains and factors stay; information hub
- **The Black Jug** — a rough tavern in the Old Quarter where Veiled Society recruiters are always watching
- **The Temple of Halav** — the Church of Karameikos's main Specularum facility; Patriarch Jowett holds services here
- **The Traladaran Community Hall** — nominally a social club; actually where Magda Marilenev holds court

### Threshold

A frontier town of around 5,000 at the north end of the navigable Highreach, below where the river turns to rapids. Threshold is the last real town before the wilderness. It has a wall (timber and earthwork, patched regularly after goblin raids), a Duke's Warden garrison, two inns, and a prosperous river trade in lumber and furs. It also has three separate dungeon complexes within a day's ride, a ruined wizard's tower on the hill above town, and a mountain pass that every humanoid tribe in the Altan Tepes apparently considers a traditional raiding route.

Threshold is the classic starting town. It has everything adventurers need (equipment, rumor, employment) and immediate proximity to everything adventurers want to fight.

**Notable figures in Threshold:**
- **Castellan Halaran** — the Duke's appointed governor, a cleric of middle years who genuinely cares about the town's welfare and is running on insufficient sleep and inadequate garrison numbers
- **Prestelle** — runs the Merchant's Guild office; knows where every rumor in the Highreach valley came from
- **Aleena** — a cleric of the Church of Karameikos, young, formidably capable, who conducts healing services and has better dungeon experience than anyone will admit

### The Castellan Keep

A stone fortress on the eastern pass road, at the edge of the Altan Tepes. The Keep guards the primary land route into Karameikos from the east. Its permanent garrison holds the pass against humanoid incursion; a rotating duty assignment, not a popular posting. The surrounding wilderness is dense with goblin, hobgoblin, orc, and gnoll activity. The famous **Caves of Chaos** lie less than a day's march from the Keep's gates — a ravine honeycombed with interconnected tribal warrens, where five or six humanoid species maintain an uneasy coexistence organized around raiding the road.

**Adventure use:** The Keep provides a safe base, an employer (the Castellan needs the Caves cleared, has needed it for twenty years), and a well-populated wilderness for extended campaign play.

### The Dymrak Forest (Eastern Wilderness)

The ancient forest east of the Highreach, stretching from Threshold south toward the coast. The Dymrak is old in a way that makes the skin prickle. Its trees are enormous. Its trails are uncertain — roads that existed last year may not exist this year. The forest is home to a Traladaran community of woodspeople who have maintained their ways despite everything, to goblin tribes of unusual organization, and to things older than either.

The **Hutaaka** — a jackal-headed humanoid civilization whose ruins litter the deepest parts of the forest — have not entirely vanished. Whether the ruins are empty is a question best answered carefully.

### Fort Doom (Black Eagle Barony)

Ludwig von Hendriks's seat of power on the western coast. Fort Doom is a real fort — well-maintained walls, a garrison of mercenaries and former Thyatian soldiers, a dungeon that sees regular use. The town around it is called Luln on old maps. The current inhabitants do not call it that. They do not call it much of anything; they keep their heads down.

Getting into Fort Doom to accomplish something is a classic high-stakes problem: the walls are real, the guards are competent, and Ludwig keeps good records of who he's had reason to be suspicious of.

### Koriszegy Keep (Ruined)

On an island in Lake Windrush, reachable by boat. Koriszegy Keep has been abandoned for three hundred years, since its last lord went mad and his family fled. Local Traladaran tradition holds that the lord's curse still walks the keep. Thyatian settlers largely think this is superstition until they send someone to investigate and he does not come back. The keep is on no official maps as a problem site; it is on every local's mental map as somewhere you do not go after dark, or before dark, or at any hour that ends in you going near it.

**Adventure use:** Haunted ruins with genuine supernatural threat. The Traladaran stories are not wrong, but they are incomplete.

## Campaign Arcs

### Arc 1: The Frontier Road

The characters arrive in Threshold, broke, carrying a letter of introduction to Castellan Halaran, and in need of work. The Warden needs the eastern road cleared of a goblin band that has been raiding supply caravans. A merchant needs an escort to a lumber camp a day north. A worried father needs someone to check on his son at a homestead that's missed its last two message drops.

These early missions build a map in the players' heads. By the end of the arc, they know Threshold, the surrounding terrain, and at least two factions with ongoing interest in hiring them.

### Arc 2: The Iron Chain

A village north of Threshold is raided during the night. No bodies — just empty houses and a dropped torch. Someone was taken. The Iron Ring safehouse is three days east, in the caves below a Thyatian logging operation whose foreman has been asking no questions about his security subcontractors.

This arc introduces the Iron Ring, the political complication that the Duke cannot visibly help with, and the first hint that the Ring has friends in Specularum. Rescuing slaves is viscerally satisfying; discovering that the buyer's name is in the Ducal Guard's officer rolls is not.

### Arc 3: The Two Churches

A Traladaran elder in Threshold has died and left property. The Church of Karameikos has filed a legal claim (the elder registered with them for hospital services). The Church of Traladara holds his spiritual testament, witnessed by community elders. The Castellan wants the dispute resolved without bloodshed; the Ducal lawyer says the law is clear; the Traladaran community says the law doesn't apply; the Church of Karameikos priest says he's just following procedure.

No one is the villain. The dispute is about power, precedent, and who gets to be legitimate. The characters can mediate, choose a side, or find a technical solution that satisfies neither party but prevents violence.

### Arc 4: The Eagle's Shadow

The characters are hired (by whom depends on previous arcs) to find proof of Ludwig von Hendriks's Iron Ring connection. The evidence trail leads to Fort Doom, requires someone to go inside, and ultimately produces documentation that the Duke could act on — if he chose to. Whether he does depends on what the players have built in terms of relationships and reputation. A Duke who trusts them may move quietly. A Duke who doesn't may burn the documents and thank them for their service.

### Arc 5: The Bones of Traldar

In the Dymrak Forest, someone has found an ancient Traldar site — pre-human in origin, connected to the Hutaaka. The finding draws three parties simultaneously: a Thyatian academic expedition, a Traladaran community elder who says the site is sacred and closed, and something from inside the ruins that has begun emerging at night. The characters arrive as the situation deteriorates.

This arc deals with the setting's ancient history, the tension between scholarship and sacredness, and introduces the Hutaaka as a potential threat or ally depending on player choices.

### Arc 6: The Succession

Duke Stefan has been gravely wounded in an assassination attempt. He lives, but will not be capable for months. His three children — each with a different vision of Karameikos's future — begin positioning. The Ducal Council is divided. The major factions see opportunity. The characters, who by now know everyone and owe favors in three directions, are approached by multiple parties.

This arc is pure political play. There are no battles unless the players make catastrophic choices; it is entirely about relationships, information, and deciding what Karameikos should be.

### Arc 7: King Halav's Return

A Traladaran prophecy holds that King Halav — the divine hero who defeated the gnoll King Wogar — will return when Traldar needs him most. A charismatic Traladaran leader has begun preaching that the time has come and he is the vessel. His following is growing fast, cutting across class lines. The Church of Traladara is split between those who believe him and those who are suspicious. The Thyatian establishment is alarmed. The man himself may be sincere, mad, manipulated, or exactly what he says.

### Arc 8: The Altan Tepes War

A hobgoblin war chief has united the tribes of the eastern mountains under a single banner — the first time in living memory. His army is organized, drilled, and moving toward the Castellan Road. The Duke is calling every available sword. The characters know the terrain and the people on both sides. The hobgoblin chief has reasons for his war that are not entirely wrong. The Duke's general wants a decisive battle; the Castellan wants to negotiate; the characters are in the middle.

## Bestiary

### Goblin (Wolfskin Raiders)

The most common humanoid threat in Karameikos. Wolfskin Raiders are the eastern goblin tribes of the Altan Tepes, distinguished by their practice of wearing wolf pelts as cloaks and their use of trained wolves as hunting and battle companions. They raid rather than invade — quick strikes against farms and caravans, then withdrawal into the forest before a response can organize.

**In combat:** Small, quick, prefer spears and short bows. Fight in loose groups, never standing when they can flee. Will sacrifice a rear guard without hesitation. Dangerous in packs of fifteen or more; manageable in smaller groups if the wolves are dealt with first.

**Their wolves:** Half-trained, half-feral. They obey a handler's commands but have minds of their own. If the handler is killed, the wolves may continue attacking or may scatter — roll for each wolf individually.

**What they want:** Meat, metal, and to not be killed. Goblin raiders are not fanatics; a sufficiently painful defense will often convince them to target easier villages.

**Encounter sizes:** 2d6 scouts, 3d10 raiding party, 6d10 tribal gathering (unusual outside their cave complexes).

### Hobgoblin (Mountain Legions)

Larger, more disciplined humanoids of the deep Altan Tepes. The hobgoblin tribes have a military culture — they drill, they use formations, they maintain supply lines. Individual hobgoblins fight with spear and shield in ranks; officers carry swords and give orders that get followed.

**In combat:** They do not flee easily. A hobgoblin unit will hold a position until a withdrawal is ordered. They are not brave — they are disciplined, which is more dangerous. Individual specimens are formidable; a unit of ten is a genuine tactical problem.

**Their organization:** Hobgoblin society is militaristic and hierarchical. A chief who shows consistent victory accumulates followers rapidly. A chief who loses becomes a former chief. The current unification under Vlack of the Iron Fist represents an unprecedented consolidation of power — something has motivated him beyond normal raiding economics.

**What they want:** Tribute from the lowland humans, recognition as the legitimate rulers of the mountain territories, and the Castellan Road closed to Thyatian traffic.

### Gnoll

Hyena-headed humanoids of the deep forest and southern approaches. The gnolls of Karameikos are connected in Traladaran mythology to King Wogar's ancient horde — the gnoll-lords whose invasion nearly destroyed Traldar civilization a thousand years ago. Modern Karameikian gnolls are not organized at that level, but they are fierce, strong, and crueler in their habits than either goblins or hobgoblins.

**In combat:** They prefer to strike from ambush, and they prefer targets that cannot fight back. Against prepared defenders they are aggressive but not suicidal. A gnoll in melee is strong enough to be genuinely dangerous to any armored fighter.

**Their habits:** Gnolls are slavers by inclination. Captives are worked to death or ransomed. They occasionally cooperate with the Iron Ring, an arrangement both parties find mutually useful and personally distasteful.

### Bugbear

Massive, stealthy humanoids that appear sporadically throughout the Altan Tepes and occasionally in the deeper Dymrak. A bugbear's defining trait is that it moves in near-silence despite its size — experienced wilderness travellers have been killed by bugbears they never heard coming. They do not form tribes of any size but operate as small family groups or solo hunters.

**In combat:** They have surprise in almost any terrain outside open field. When surprised, they are still formidable; when the surprise is theirs, they are lethal. Bugbears fight with large clubs and crude swords, focusing on incapacitating targets quickly.

**Their value as information:** Bugbears know the territory they inhabit better than any humanoid and better than most humans. Getting information from one requires either captures, which is dangerous, or negotiation, which requires something they want.

### Wight (Undead)

The ruins of Traldar civilization are old enough to have produced their share of unquiet dead. Wights are the most common undead threat in the abandoned keeps and barrow mounds scattered through the Dymrak and the Altan Tepes foothills. They were once warriors or leaders; their burial sites were disturbed; they did not stay buried.

**In combat:** Wights are immune to non-magical weapons and drain life energy with their touch — each successful hit reduces the target's experience level. This drain is permanent without magical restoration. A party without magical weapons has no business engaging wights directly.

**Their territories:** Wights do not wander far from their burial sites. They can be avoided by knowing where the old mounds are — which Traladaran communities know, and Thyatian settlers do not. The Traladaran practice of leaving offerings at known barrow sites is not superstition; it is a working détente.

### The Iron Ring Slaver

Not a monster but a human threat that functions similarly to one. Iron Ring operatives travel in groups of six to twenty, armed with swords and crossbows, with chains and manacles for cargo. They are not soldiers but they are not amateurs — a slaving crew has conducted violence before and knows how to minimize their own losses.

**In combat:** They prefer capture to killing (dead slaves have no value) and will attempt to subdue with nets, bolas, and sleep-inducing substances before drawing blades. Against serious armed resistance they will fight, but they will also retreat if the exchange rate goes against them.

**Identifying them:** Iron Ring agents in Karameikos avoid visible branding, but maintain identifying marks in their equipment — a particular knot in their rope work, a chain-link stamp on the inside of a buckle. Characters who know what to look for can identify Ring operatives before trouble starts.

### Bargle the Infamous

A Thyatian wizard in Ludwig von Hendriks's employ — well-dressed, cheerful, and among the most dangerous men in Karameikos. Bargle is not evil in any theatrical sense; he is simply a man who decided a long time ago that power matters more than principle and has been consistently correct in the short term. He serves Ludwig because Ludwig pays well and asks few questions about methodology.

**In combat:** Bargle is a high-level magic-user who has had the good sense to survive. He prepares for likely threats and has access to resources most wizards don't. He will not fight fair.

**His usefulness:** Bargle is not loyal to Ludwig — he is employed by Ludwig. Information the characters possess, or access they can provide, or a better offer from somewhere, are all possible leverage points. He has also kept records. Bargle never stops being a threat, but he is a threat with negotiable edges.

## Running Karameikos

**The frontier as pressure cooker.** Karameikos works best when the wilderness is genuinely threatening and the safety of towns is genuinely limited. Resources are scarce. Healing is available but expensive. The humanoid tribes are a continuous threat, not a one-time problem. Towns are islands of order in a sea of danger, and the sea is large.

**Political tension without politics paralysis.** The Thyatian/Traladaran conflict should create texture, not a wall. Characters with Traladaran heritage face different assumptions than Thyatian ones; characters with no stake in the old-world conflict can navigate between communities in ways that locals cannot. The tension creates roleplaying material; it should never prevent play.

**Duke Stefan as a good man with a flawed position.** He is not a villain, and he is not a perfect ruler. He is doing something genuinely hard (building a just state on someone else's land) and making consistent mistakes about which voices he listens to. Respecting him while disagreeing with him is a valid and interesting player position.

**The Iron Ring is always operating.** Even when the characters are doing something else entirely, the Ring is moving people. A session dealing with Church disputes may end with the party discovering that the wagon they passed on the road contained something that wasn't lumber. This is not a gotcha; it is a world that continues.

**Threshold as home base.** The classic Karameikos campaign starts and returns to Threshold. It is small enough to know personally, large enough to have everything useful, and positioned perfectly for expansion in every direction. The characters should know the innkeeper's name, the Castellan's history, and which priest to wake up at midnight when something goes wrong.
