# Apple Lane
The hamlet of Apple Lane is a small market settlement with approximately 20 adult inhabitants. It sits astride a well-used trade road on the borderlands between two tribes, some 32 kilometers from the nearest city, Jonstown. Travelers, from whom the settlement gets the larger share of its trade, are happy to stop at its inn and pray at the Uleria Temple. Farmers and shepherds from the surrounding hills enjoy its urbane comforts. Apple orchards, from which the hamlet draws its name, surround it on every side.

Apple Lane is near the boundary between the Colymar and Malani tribes. These tribes have old rivalries and disagreements that often result in raids and boundary disputes; occasionally in outright war. Both tribes follow Orlanth the Storm God. Most of the local population are members of the Hiording or Varmandi clans of the Colymar.

Apple Lane is part of the Kingdom of Sartar. Boldhome, capital city of Sartar, lies some 65 kilometers away by secondary and primary roads.

The map on the facing page shows the village buildings.
The descriptions of the individual buildings below contain notes about important people living there, and their statistics.
There are no stats for children: this is intentional and reflects the general practice among most Sartarites whereby the strong protect the weak.

Unless otherwise noted, all buildings are of stone and have terracotta roof tiles.

## Short Background
To help unite the tribes of the Quivin hills, Prince Sartar had a temple to Uleria built here. Several generations later, a companion of Prince Tarkalor, Gringle Goodsale, received the rights to establish a nearby market. A caravanserai called the Tin Inn was built alongside Gringle’s trade house, popularly called “Gringle’s Pawnshop,” and the hamlet took shape. Masons from Boldhome built many of the buildings.
The Colymar appointed a thane to protect the hamlet.
Travelers could pass through these lands unmolested and Apple Lane became a popular way station for traders.

The Lunar Occupation did not initially have much impact on Apple Lane and for a decade life continued as it did under the Princes of Sartar. After Starbrow’s Rebellion, Gringle found himself deeply in debt to the victorious Fazzur Wideread and received the dubious honor of the right to collect Lunar taxes from the Colymar and Malani tribes. In 1621, Lunars seeking Gringle’s treasures killed the Thane of Apple Lane, burned down the Pawnshop and gave the hamlet to a retired Lunar officer, Percippus.

The hamlet would have died but for the efforts of Brightflower the Brewer, Aileena the Priestess, and Hendroste Horsemaster, a new arrival. They kept the hamlet alive by encouraging travelers to come to Apple Lane for drinks, sex, and trade. They paid protection money to Percippus, who recruited several soldiers to help defend the hamlet.

After the Dragonrise of 1625, Lunar rule collapsed.
Percippus and his household fled, and Leika became Queen of the Colymar. Now the hamlet is free, but without protection.

## Uleria’s Temple and the Ladies Quarters
Uleria is the Goddess of Love, and this shrine is dedicated to her. The Uleria Temple is the central building in the hamlet and predates the rest of the village. It is a two-story stone building, its rectangular terracotta roof held up by columns carved and painted as coupled caryatids.

The temple has a different ornately carved door on each side; one entry is restricted to use by the priestess and her ladies and is covered with warnings to stay away. The other entries are dedicated to one of the goddess’ aspects, so that the priestess will know what duty to perform for each person who enters. One entry opens on a sacred bordello, where spells and rites are performed; one may give 5 L and couple with one of the initiates or 15 L and couple with the priestess.
A second door opens on a comfortable dining room where for 2 L each, strangers may converse, drink, and eat together.
The last door leads to the steam room, where for 1 L a guest may have a hot bath with water and steam. The internal walls have been whitewashed and painted with frescoes depicting a wide variety of erotic scenes. The second story holds the women’s living quarters.

One priestess and two younger ones live here, all tending the needs of their cult. The priestess Aileena is a strikingly attractive woman in her mid-30s. She has been with this temple for nearly 20 years and is devoted to the arts of Love. Janeera and Thrilla are younger women. Thrilla only recently came to the temple; she is a courtesan from the Lunar Empire that fled Boldhome after the Dragonrise and received Aileena’s protection. The temple performs fertility rituals, provides convivial company, and administers the arts of love as needed.

## The Tin Inn

![](/docs/runequest/adventures/05-tin-inn.png){#map-tin-inn}

The Tin Inn is a well-established and fortified roadhouse on a frequently travelled route, and almost always has visitors. It is the only tavern for a day’s journey beyond; outlying farmers normally congregate at the inn. The inn has two stories and it, along with its outbuildings, wall, and courtyard are tin-roofed. The inn has a large common room on the ground floor; upstairs are eight rooms (although only five are available to rent). Paying guests may sleep in the common room or rent out the upstairs rooms (which are equipped with a bed, a washing basin, and a chamber pot). Prices for drinks, food, and accommodations are provided on page 413 of the RuneQuest core rules.

Brightflower, the daughter of the previous owner, runs the inn. Gooseberry Sprig works as the cook and dogsbody.

Postal and Mineera live in a small building within the walls but outside the inn and work there as well. Postal is the stable hand as well as being noted for his skill at carpentry.
Mineera gardens and tends the animals. Their daughters Jeena and Vareena work in the inn as waitresses and general helpers. Jeena is married to Hendroste the Horsemaster and resides with him.

Pramble, an elderly poet, lives permanently in the Tin Inn, as he has for the last 20 years. He has a hunched posture and personality, and spends most of his meager income on vellum and ink. Occasionally he works as an extra for the inn when he needs money and when trade allows.

Squinch, the local scholar, also lives at the Tin Inn. See Storeroom (page 69) for more about him).

## Temple To All The Deities

This is a two-story building with a full basement. An additional two-story tall tower surmounts it.

Of the main building, the first floor is one large room, while the upper floor is divided into several smaller rooms.
These rooms are decorated with frescoes showing as many deities as the painters could imagine. In the basement are rooms for storing food and equipment, and the caretaker’s rooms.

The first and second floors of the temple, as well as the two tower rooms, are rented for celebrations or sacrifices.
Locals use them for their seasonal farming ceremonies, and for weekly prayers to less popular deities. Only cults of Chaos and evil have ever been denied rental here.

No one has ever dared to try to rob the place, though rumor has it that several guardian spirits occupy the building.
Even during the depths of the Lunar Occupation, this building was unspoiled.

The caretaker is an old woman, Kareena. Kareena still mourns for her ward, a simple girl who died in the Great Winter.

## Stable
The Horsemasters’ stable has 80 stalls. In addition to training horses, Hendroste also breeds and sells them. He has 2–12 horses available, as well as a few mules and donkeys (never more than ten at a time).

Half of the stable has a second story, used for storage.

## Storeroom
This one-story building has a unique (and very secure) pyramid shaped roof made of stone. The walls and foundations are also of stone. A single brassbound and magically-locked door (locked with a Lock spell backed up with 10 magic points) faces the Tin Inn. The storeroom has never been robbed.

The storeroom is used as a regional granary. It also functions as the hamlet’s general store and is run by Squinch.
Within the granary are foodstuffs, grain, apples, wine, seeds, cloth, blank parchment, and just about any other common trade good that Squinch thinks he can sell to the villagers, travelers, or farmers.

Though he is aloof and artificially worldly, he never intentionally cheats a customer. Squinch’s air of snobbery comes from his position as the local sage and merchant. The farmers and most travelers go to him for their needs, and he also acts as the buyer of surplus farm crops.

Squinch lives in the Tin Inn, where usually he can be found.

### Gringle’s Pawnshop
This is the burnt husk of a two-story stone building. The roof is gone (having fallen into the building), and one secondfloor wall has collapsed. This austere two-story stone building has only one window (on the second story), and only two doors. It is called Gringle’s Pawnshop after its original owner, a merchant priest who ran the pawnshop for nearly 50 years.

### Smithy and Armory
This building is empty and abandoned. The double doors of this building face the road. There also is a door in the rear.

### Ruined Hut
This is little more than a campsite, surrounded by a broken fence.

### Issaries Shrine
This is a stone that has been carved with a crude image of Issaries and his runes placed upon it. Travelers pay their respect to the God of Travelers by offering it a libation of wine or placing food at the base of the stone.

### Surrounding Area
Orchards, mainly the apples from which the village takes its name, surround the community for several kilometers, interspersed with farmhouses and pasturage. Tenants from the nearby Varmandi and Hiording clans work the orchards.
Migrants and fugitives sometimes camp in the trees.

The orchards surrounding Apple Lane produce the equivalent of 25 hides of food, primarily apples. The orchard rights are distributed as follows: five hides for the support of the Uleria Temple, five hides for the support of the Horsemasters’ Guild, and five for the support of the Temple to All the Deities. Another five hides are customarily allocated to the thane and five more hides to the Weaponsmasters’ Guild, but as that office is vacant, the produce is currently sold by Squinch, and given to the tribe.
