# Other Places of Interest
## Alebard’s Tower
This hollow-walled drystone tower is about 20 meters in diameter and stands about 12 meters tall. Its walls are thick at the base and taper at the top. It serves as a shrine to Humakt the War God. Prior to Starbrow’s Rebellion, this was the headquarters of the Humakti Temple of the Wooden Sword.

## Apple Lane
This market hamlet is in territory disputed by the Colymar and Malani tribes. It is noteworthy for its temple to Uleria, the goddess of Love. The hamlet also has a fortified roadhouse and caravanserai—the Tin Inn—on the trade road between Runegate and Jonstown and was the home of the noted Issaries Priest Gringle Goodsale until 1621 when Lunar soldiers chased him out of Sartar and burned down his pawnshop. See Apple Lane chapter.

## Black Altar
This large black stone is used by Darkness cults for sacrifice.
It has a groove carved in it to drain blood into a stone receptacle. Trolls and other Darkness worshipers can often be found here.

## Birne’s Squeeze
The trade road between Apple Lane and Jonstown runs through the Thunder Hills by way of this narrow, winding, and rocky ravine. Travelers are often ambushed here; as a result, the phrase “Birne’s Squeeze” is used by the Colymar as a euphemism for highway robbery.

## Boulder Ruins
These ruins atop Stael’s Hills were once a complex of temples and ruins but are now little more than foundations and walls made of huge polygonal-shaped stones. The vaulted roofs of the complex collapsed centuries ago and the stones were long ago quarried. Here the Varmandi found the goddess who now resides in the fine temple at Greenstone.

## Broken Tower
This ruined tower and surrounding ruins is avoided by the local herders. See the RuneQuest Quickstart Adventure “The Broken Tower” for more information.

## Chaos Ground
This tainted place at the edge of Stael’s Hills was created when the Crimson Bat bled here in 1602. It is a place of power for Chaos-worshiping cults, and Chaotic things can occasionally be found here.

## Cinder Pits
In a Gods War battle pitting the storm gods against the sky gods, this was the place the dead gods were cast down and burned. It is fuming bowl of ash and smoke. Loud noises can sometimes be heard coming from within.

## Dragonewt Plinths
There are four sets of dragonewt plinths in the Colymar tribal lands, forming a straight line over many kilometers. Each set consists of a pair of standing stones which appear to be a crude and stylized representation of dragons, wyrms, and an otherwise unknown thing vulgarly called a “frogosaurus.” The plinths are part of a functioning dragonewt road between the dragonewt city deep in the Quivin Mountains called Highwyrm and Stones-over-Souls, a dragonewt city near Dunstop in Tarsh. ## Dragonewt Band
This band of dragonewts is currently in the Colymar lands, keeping an eye on the activities of Yerezum Storn, a recurring dream dragon that has recently reappeared in the area around Apple Lane (see __The Dragon of the Thunder Hills__, page 104).

The dragonewts will not fight unless they are attacked. As far as dragonewts go, this group will be remarkably friendly as they regularly speak to humans, looking for the humans that will perform utuma for Yerezum Storn. Once they determine that someone is not likely to perform the rite, they lose all interest in them.

To the human eye, all dragonewts of a given type look alike, even to the extent of possessing individual personalities.
If they do possess individuality, dragonewts do not display it in the presence of non-dragonewts. In dealings with others, the dragonewts follow a strict code. They will not speak to a non-dragonewt unless spoken to first. Only the minimum required answer will be given. Only Sarna Ya’qal speaks to outsiders, although Slave to Lies translates for it.

The dragonewts are not easily coerced by force. They do not fear physical death. Only spiritual destruction holds any terror for them.

#### Sarna Ya’qal (Tailed Priest)
Sarna fluctuates between extremes of honorable and dishonorable, greedy and generous, impatient and patient, and extrovert and introvert. It has been given the task of finding the correct humans to perform utuma for Yerezum Storn and is willing to make great sacrifices (including entanglements in the material world) to carry out its task.

## Dragonewt Weapons, Skills, and Magic
> Dragonewts are complex and ancient creatures, with unusual weapons specicic to their race, as well as unique practices,
particularly skills and magic. All are described at length in the Glorantha Bestiary and are summarized here for the purposes of this adventure.

### Dragonewt Weapons

Dragonewt weapons are fashioned of obsidian, stone, wood, dragonbone, or leather, and among dragonewts they are given or made, never purchased. Base chance for a human using any dragonewt weapon is 05%.

- Klanth: A weapon made with obsidian blades set into a wooden haft and grip. (Slashing)
- Korff: A slightly-curved dragonbone sword that appears to be made of ivory. Dragonewts will go to any length to recover a lost korff. (Cutting)
- Utuma: A ceremonial stone dagger used in ritual suicide. (Impaling)
- Gami: A three-pronged dragonbone sword-catcher, primary used as a parrying weapon. On a special success parry, the attacker is disarmed. On a critical success parry, the attacker’s weapon is broken, regardless of its hit points. (Cutting)
- Long Bow: A large bone bow. (Impaling)
- Sling: As per normal slings, though the pocket and loops are often made of human, troll, or dragonewt skin. (Crushing)
- Samarin and Chokin: These are small throwing weapons that can be easily concealed in the hand. Up to five can be thrown at one time, but each thrown after the first subtracts –10% from each attack. (Impaling)

### Dragonewt Skills

- Don Armor (05): This is a dragonewt-only Manipulation skill: the base 05% chance comes through a successful mystic heroquest. Successful use of the skill allows the dragonewt to don its ornamental dragonbone armor in 1D10+10 strike ranks. When the armor is put on, the gamemaster should roll D100. Each full 05% rolled (round down) gives the armor 1 point of damage absorption, to the limit of the user’s Don Armor skill. Dragonewts use this skill before important journeys or battles, and the armor is left on until the mission is accomplished.
- Quickdraw (05): This Manipulation skill lets the user draw their sword from the scabbard and attack in one smooth, uninterrupted motion. If the Quickdraw skill is used successfully, the user needn’t wait the additional 5 strike ranks before attacking once their weapon is drawn. If the skill fails, the user will be the last to attack in that round but can still parry. If the skill roll is fumbled, the user has lost their weapon and thrown it 1D6 meters in a random direction, in addition to a roll on the Fumble table.
- Worship (utuma ritual) (00): This mysterious dragonewts spiritual ritual utilizes the utuma and is never taught to outsiders. Its secrets will not be revealed here.

### Dragonewt Magic

Dragonewt magic (sometimes called “dragon magic”) is learned through esoteric contemplation and is different from Rune magic, spirit magic, or sorcery. When the dragonewt wills an effect, it takes place on SR 1. Effects are not spells—a dragonewt simply knows or does not know an effect.
- Attune Landscape: The dragonewt achieve oneness with its surroundings, becoming nearly impossible to see so long as it does not move (once it moves, the effect ends). Unless a viewer receives a special success in the appropriate Perception skill, it cannot be detected. This even applies to spells such as Second
Sight or Detect Enemies. If the dragonewt succeeds in using its Hide skill when invoking this effect, any potential enemy must be aware of the presence or possible presence of the dragonewt to even attempt using a Perception skill (and the viewer still needs a special success to spot the dragonewt).
- Soul-blast: The POW of the dragonewt is matched against that of its target via a blast of green energy.
If the dragonewt succeeds, the target loses 2D6 magic points.

### Gejay Hills
These hills are also called the Oakland Back and separate the Colymar from the Malani tribe. They are a mix of oak woodland and grassland. Herders and bandits populate the hills.

### Hound Knob
This stone formation is atop a rounded hill that rises above Tarndisi’s Grove. Local hunters avoid the area as a monstrous hound called Black Growler haunts it.

#### Black Growler

This demonic hound originated in the Underworld. It escaped when the Sun stopped in 373 and was bound here by an ancient curse. The Black Growler must return to the Hound Knob each dawn, reappearing each dusk to hunt. It avoids the Aldryami of Tarndisi’s Grove and the dragonewts but considers humans and other mortals to be prey.

### Kjartan’s Lake
This large alpine lake is situated in a boggy vale, rich with berries. To the east are the forested foothills of the Quivin Mountains.

### Larnste’s Table
See the description under __Temples__ (page 14).

### Lunar Manors

These are the ruins of fortified slave farms built by the Lunars after Starbrow’s Rebellion, and where the local clans paid their annual tribute to the Lunar Empire. The farms, their records, and their shrines were all destroyed after the Dragonrise and the overseers killed, although most of the slaves escaped. They now stand empty and ruined.

### Nymie Vale
This is a cultivated valley of small farms, vineyards, and pastures for cattle and sheep. Ruins of older civilizations are scattered around the vale.

### Odd Tower

During Starbrow’s Rebellion a part of the Lunar army made a feint across the River at Two Sisters and constructed several observation towers by the Upland Marsh. This tower is the only one to have survived from that time. It is still used as an observation post.

### Old Grave

![](/docs/runequest/adventures/04-grave.png){#kings-grave}

This is the burial mound of King Berevenenos, a longforgotten Second Age hero who died fighting against the dragons. His mound is six meters high and has a diameter of nearly 20 meters. On the south side of the mound, a hole big enough for a man to crawl has been dug into the mound. A careful look with a torch will show that the hole penetrates the center of the mound. A heavy slab of rock has been removed, permitting entrance into the burial chamber.
Within is an oblong stone chamber divided into two rooms:

Outer Chamber: The adventurers enter here. On the floor are bronze and ceramic dishes that centuries ago held food and drink. There is also a desiccated corpse holding a silver goblet—the Lunar grave robber who dug the hole and tried to rob the grave goods. The bronze and ceramic dishes might be worth a grand total of 5 L if cleaned and a willing buyer is found, and the silver goblet alone is worth an additional 5 L.

Inner Chamber: On a raised stone dais is the decorated burial urn of Berevenenos. Surrounding the urn are his grave goods (see __Grave Goods__, below). Noticeable among these items are the ornate silver helmet, iron cuirass, great shield, weapons, and jewelry.

#### Ghost of King Berevenenos

If anyone enters the inner chamber, Berevenenos will manifest with righteous anger and indignation—he rages that the people of this land have forgotten him, and his resting place violated by would-be thieves. He attacks intruders unless they placate him with offers of annual gifts of food and drink. If an adventurer makes such an offer, but later fails to provide the offerings, Berevenenos will curse their clan, giving them a –25% to the Sacred Time Harvest rolls until his curse is lifted.

Berevenenos hates dragons and dragonkind, and he is willing to give his weapons and armor to someone that impresses upon him their desire to fight against dragons without compromise. If an adventurer who takes Berevenenos’ weapons or armor later compromises with the dragons, Berevenenos will curse their clan until the weapons and armor are returned.

### Pig Hollow

### Quackford
This market settlement is where the royal road crosses The Stream over Sartar’s Bridge, a stone bridge built by Prince Sartar. At 400 people, the village is the largest settlement of the Arnoring clan and includes a small community of ducks led by Fethry Hookbill and Mularda Bronzewings.
The village has a weekly Issaries market and boats take goods from here all the way to Esrolia.

### Queen’s Tomb
This tomb-sanctuary in the Thunder Hills is the burial place of an ancient demi-god ruler. It is covered by a huge mound and has a long entrance passageway. Huge statues of gods and goddesses are seated above the entrance. The gods and goddesses are seated, but the heads of the statues were at some point removed and they are now scattered throughout the area. Local herders avoid the place, claiming it is haunted.

The Queen’s Tomb is the resting place of Orgorvale Summer, the demi-goddess daughter of King Vingkot and granddaughter of Orlanth. She was the God Time ruler of these lands. Her cult died out in the Second Age, after the dream dragon Yerezum Storn was summoned here by draconic magic. Yerezum Storn has recently reappeared and resides within the Queen’s Tomb. See __The Dragon of the Thunder Hills__ (page 104).

### Quivin Mountains

This small mountain range consists of thirteen steep-sided peaks above 2,000 meters and incised by deep valleys. The highest peak is Quivin Mountain at 3,120 meters, and sacred to the Orlanth cult. Nearer to the Colymar lands are Wild Mountain (2,210 meters) and Eagles Mountain (2,560 meters). Above 2,500 meters or so there is usually snow and ice year-round. The tree line is around 1,750 meters.
The city of Boldhome is in a deep valley in the Quivin Mountains. The largest city in Sartar (and one of the largest in Dragon Pass), Boldhome is the traditional seat of the Princes of Sartar.

### Rainbow Mounds

Beneath these hills is a notorious system of caves, with walls colored in all the hues of the rainbow. The entrance is easily located because of an enormous plinth called the Giant’s Table, beneath which lies the entrance to the limestone caves.
The Giant’s Table is a double “T” formation, and the cave mouth lies between its legs. The plinth legs are 10 meters high and the stone topping them is about 12 meters long.

The caves beneath the Rainbow Mounds are home to a colony of newtlings and many rock lizards. From time to time, others have made their homes in the extensive caves; about a decade ago, a feared troll bandit gang used the caves as a hideaway.

### Runegate

![](/docs/runequest/adventures/04-map-of-runegate.png){#map-runegate}

The small walled town of Runegate squats atop a low rocky rise on the northern bank of the Swan River. It dates from before the Dragonkill War. Runegate was resettled by horse-worshipers who fled from the Holy Country in 1325, dragging their sacred lintel stone. They called themselves “Hyalorings” and claimed descent from the ancient hero Hyalor Horsebreaker. The Hyalorings formed a triaty of clans (the Enhyli, the Lonisi, and the Narri) with Runegate as their tribal center, and each clan swore to take wives only from a specific clan. However, around 1400, to make peace with the Colymar, the Narri took wives from them. This so-called “Fish Marriage” badly weakened the triaty so that the Lonisi were devastated in the subsequent Taral War. The remnants of the triaty eventually joined the Colymar tribe and the Taraling clan was founded on the ruins of the Lonisi.
The Taraling clan currently dominates the town.

Runegate was the site of a major battle when the Red Emperor invaded Sartar in 1602. The town survived an assault of undead from the Upland Marsh only to be destroyed by the Crimson Bat, only the lintel stone surviving.
It was subsequently rebuilt; as a result, most of the buildings are less than a generation old and most are built out of wood and lime plastered adobe. The town has a population of about 900 people, with a major temple to Elmal the Sun God and shrines to Humakt the War God and the Storm Bull. Queen Leika asserts lordship of Runegate; however, the chief of the Runegate town council, Verinos the Old, is generally seen as the master of the town.

To the north of Runegate is Horse Town, where the local clans breed, raise, and sell horses.

### Seven Falls
The Stream narrows here as it flows through a series of tiered cascades. None of the steps are much more than 4 meters, and salmon make the run up the cascades to Kjartan’s Lake every Earth Season.

### Stael’s Hills
This range of hills overlooks The Creek and separates the Colymar from the Malani tribe. The Boulder Ruins atop the hills form the traditional boundary between the Colymar and Malani tribes.

### Starfire Ridges
This rugged range of hills separates the Colymar from the Lismelder tribe. It is divided into the Little, Big, and Upper Starfire Ridges. It is a badland littered with rocks, ruins, caves, and strange obsidian formations; herders, hunters, and bandits are the main inhabitants.

### Stream Vale

This valley is densely settled with farms. The hills on the north side of the valley have many vineyards. Ducks populate the lower part of the valley. Centuries ago, the Colymar tried to seize control of the entire valley down to the Upland Marsh in the Beast Men Wars. Although the Colymar were initially successful, removing the ducks resulted in the Zombie War, which ended only once the Colymar recognized the duck’s claims to the lower part of the valley.

### Tarkalor Keep
This stone complex in Stael’s Hills consists of a tall central tower of some 25 meters high with four floors, and three additional towers of two floors. From atop the central tower, a watch can be kept on both the Ormthane and Swan valleys.
The tower was built to keep peace between the Colymar and Malani tribes, and the princes of Sartar often used the keep as a hunting lodge. The keep was abandoned during the Lunar Occupation and has served as a refuge for bandits, outlaws, and rebels.

### Tarndisi’s Grove
This ancient mixed forest of oak, beech, pine, and firs is sacred to the goddess Aldrya and inhabited by a small community of Aldryami, brown and green elves. Tarndisi the Dryad, the Chief Priestess of Aldrya, rules the grove. She has friendly relations with the Clearwine Earth Temple and accepts offerings from the Ernalda cult. The Shanassee Tree is an ancient sentient redwood tree that dates to the Dawn.

## Neighbors
### Balmyr

The Balmyr tribe is a confederation of eight clans numbering about 7,600 people. They settled the southern reaches of Kjartan’s Lake and the broad valley to the south called Chormsland. The Balmyr are a member of the Wilmskirk confederation of tribes.

### Lismelder
The Lismelder are a small tribe located between the Upland Marsh and the Starfire Ridges. They number about 4,000 people (down almost 1,000 from the Great Winter) and consist of eight small clans. They were originally part of the Malani tribe but broke away in 1356. The Lismelder are noted friends of the ducks of Beast Valley and are their allies against the undead hosts of Delecti the Necromancer.

- Bostrop: This clan is famed for their dwarf-made water mill, which grinds grain into flour. Other communities bring their grain to them to be ground, making the Bostrop the richest clan of the Lismelder.
- Greydog: This small clan is best known for the Greydog Inn. They have long fought a losing battle with the Orlmarth clan over pasture rights on the Starfire Ridges.
- Goodsword: This clan controls the Six Stones, a minor temple to Humakt, also known as Indrodar’s Necklace.
- Goodweaver: At 800 people, this is the largest clan in the tribe. They are closely tied to the nearby duck communities and are known as peacemakers.
- Hillhaven: The smallest clan of the tribe, the Hillhaven number little more than 300 people. They have a long tradition of powerful Kolatings and has made many pacts with powerful local spirits.
- Lonendi: This clan is located near the Upland Marsh and is often troubled by the forces of Delecti the Necromancer.
- Marshedge: This clan is located on the edge of the Upland Marsh and has close links with the local ducks.
- Poss: This clan has an ancient rivalry with the Greydog clan. They prospered during the Lunar Occupation thanks to their co-operation with the Lunar Army.

### Locaem
This confederation of seven clans numbers about 7,000 people, and includes all the lands surrounding the Pegasus Plateau. The tribe is centered on the hill fort of Famegrave and is a member of the Wilmskirk confederation of tribes.
After the failure of Starbrow’s Rebellion, the Lunar Empire transferred the Zethnoring clan from the Colymar tribe to the Locaem tribe. The pro-Lunar king of the Locaem was devoured in the Dragonrise, and the tribe has been unable to agree upon a new king.

The Three-Emerald Temple is within the Locaem tribal lands. This major Earth Temple goes deep into the earth, where worshipers may offer sacrifices directly to the Three Green Goddesses.

### Malani
The Malani tribe is a confederation of six clans numbering about 6,000 people and is centered on Two Ridge Fort.
The Malani are a member of the Jonstown confederation of tribes. They have been ruled by a dynasty of Humaktworshiping kings; the current king is Amalda, the daughter of the previous king, Edruf Strongbreath.

The six clans are:

- Blue Fox Clan: This small clan occupies the area between Stael’s Hills and The Creek. The Blue Fox have friendly relations with the Narri clan.
- Desuring Clan: This small clan farms between Larnste’s Table and The Creek.
- Isolting Clan: The largest clan of the tribe, the Isolting is traditionally (although not always) the royal clan. They reside between Two Ridge Fort and The Creek. They are noted for their worship of Humakt the War God.
- Lysang Clan: One of the original Tree Triaty clans, the Lysang clan settled in the Arfritha Vale in the “Second Wave” of settlement. Their land-claims were disputed by the Colymar tribe, which was the cause of raids for many years. After the Zarran War, they acknowledged the Colymar tribe’s authority over the entire Arfritha Vale. In 1613, Lunar imperial authorities ordered the Lysang clan severed from the Colymar tribe and joined them to the Malani tribe.
- Namolding Clan: One of the original Tree Triaty clans, the Namolding clan settled in the Arfritha Vale in the “Second Wave” of settlement. They supported the Lysang clan in their dispute with the Colymar tribe. After the Zarran War, the Colymar were recognized as the rulers of the entire Arfritha Vale. The upper Arfritha Vale were given to the Antorling, who joined the Colymar tribe. The Namoldings gladly joined the Colymar tribe soon after. In 1613, Lunar imperial authorities ordered the Namolding clan severed from the Colymar tribe and joined them to the Malani tribe.
- Orleving Clan: This influential clan occupies the Ormsthane Vale. Ernalda is their patron deity and the major temple to the Earth Goddess at Greenstone is within their lands. They have feuded with the Varmandi clan for centuries.

### Sun Dome Temple
The Sun Dome Temple typically holds itself aloof from the Orlanthi-worshiping tribes of Sartar. The cults sometimes challenge each other ritually to commemorate the rivalry of Air and Sun and hold each other with distrust. The Runegate clans consider the cultists of the Sun Dome Temple to be traitors, despite (or perhaps because of ) the similarity of their cults.

### Upland Marsh
The Upland Marsh is a large valley bog, with a collection of murky islands, rocky outcroppings, and groves of cypress and blackthorn trees standing out of the dead plant material and black water. Several of the islands are the ruins of ancient cities and buildings. A maze of drainage channels crisscross the marsh, like a fine network of scars. Dead corpses animated by the evil magic of Delecti the Necromancer haunt the bog; some are little more than skeletons, the black bog water has preserved others with hair, organs, and skin intact despite the passage of centuries.

In 1380 the Lismelder and Colymar tribes attempted to seize the rich farmlands along the under-populated stretch of The Stream which is called Duck Vale. The only occupants were various Beast Men, especially the humanoid ducks. The attack was successful.

However, three years later came an invasion from the nearby Upland Marsh of walking corpses. Some were very old.
The Lismelder tribe called together its warriors and militia, but they were tricked and defeated in battle. The undead began burning all their houses and fields. An expedition led by a famous warrior went into the marsh. He was Kurash Varn, of the Sambari tribe. But he was ambushed by ducks, and the undead things just kept coming out. Divination revealed that the ducks were necessary to suppress the undead from the marsh.

At last a treaty was made, and the Colymar abandoned all the parts of Duck Vale which they had gone to, and paid compensation to the families of the dead.
