# Clearwine Fort
Clearwine Fort is the political and religious center of the Colymar tribe. It is in an agriculturally rich area, with many vineyards to the north and east. The town has a weekly market and temples and shrines to the tribal gods. The tribal assembly meets here; outside the fort is a major Earth Temple.

## History
Founded in 1325, this fortified town is the oldest human settlement in Sartar. Legends hold that Orgorvale, daughter of the demigod Vingkot, built its cyclopean walls in the Lesser Darkness to house her treasures. The Orgorvaltes were destroyed near the end of the First Age.

Under the Empire of the Wyrms Friends, it became the administrative center for the district of Storn and was ruled by its priests. Its inhabitants all died during the Dragonkill of 1120 and it was left empty of humankind.

Colymar originally built his settlement a few kilometers upstream, but after this settlement was burned by a later band of settlers in 1325, Colymar called his people together, and they entered and cleared all draconic malignance out of the ancient hill fort that loomed over the valley. They took it as their home and called it Brondagal.

Since then, Clearwine Fort has been the most important settlement of the Colymar tribe. Despite reversals in war, the tribe could always take refuge behind its ancient walls.

## Population
The population of Clearwine Fort is approximately 1,800 people, of which 1,000 are adults and 850 are children.
About 1,000 people are members of the Ernaldoring clan; another 500 from the other clans, and 300 are outsiders and foreigners.

The adult population includes: 80 nobles (king, chieftain, thanes, and Rune Masters); 100 full-time warriors; 25 priests and God-talkers; ten scribes; 250 free farmers; 300 semi-free tenants, assistants, herders, and hunters; 100 free crafters; 100 slaves; and 35 itinerants.

## Persons of Prominence
### Leika Black Spear
Queen of the Colymar tribe. High Priest of Orlanth Rex, Wind Lord of Vinga.

Female, age 37. Taraling clan.

Leika is Queen of the Colymar by right and by conquest.
She is a Wind Lord of Vinga, and the high priest of Orlanth Rex for the Colymar tribe. She earned the name “Ballista” when she accompanied the Wooden Sword into the caves of Chaos deep within Snakepipe Hollow.

When her kinsman and king of the Colymar, Kallai Rockbuster, was exiled by the Lunar Empire following the defeat of Starbrow’s Rebellion, Leika asserted her claim to the tribal kingship by undertaking a second harrowing raid deep into the Caves of Chaos in Snakepipe Hollow. She came out with the legendary harp called Jeweled Notes, which played magical songs of great strength, and was led back to Clearwine by a great black bull that appeared atop the Starfire Ridges. She was acclaimed Queen by her joyous people and priests, without any preliminary legal preparations, and despite the presence of other rivals.

Leika was a popular queen, but the Lunar Empire conspired with her rivals and in 1615 her own tribe exiled her. The Lunars recognized her kinsman Kangharl “Blackmoor” as the new king. Kangharl was a skilled warrior who fought valiantly against the Lunar Empire several times yet joined the Lunar religion to gain support for the kingship. In exile, Leika took the Black Spear and served King Broyan of Whitewall as one of his captains.
She maintained her position as high priest of Orlanth Rex and offered sacrifices on behalf of the tribe. During the siege of Whitewall, she helped slay the Crimson Bat. She led a band of Colymar exiles at the battles of Auroch Hills, Nochet, and Pennel Ford, and was one of the chiefs who acclaimed Broyan King of Kethaela.

In Fire Season 1625, Leika survived the magical assault from the Lunar College of Magic that killed King Broyan and much of his army. Leika and her followers returned to Sartar to foment rebellion. But only a season later, the Dragonrise devoured the Provincial Army, and most of the key Lunar supporters in Sartar, including Kangharl. Shortly after the Dragonrise, Leika and her Colymar supporters returned to Clearwine Fort and proved herself Queen with the backing of the tribal priests and the militia. Leika acknowledges Kallyr Starbrow as the Prince of Sartar.

Leika is a proud, stubborn woman; she is undaunted despite suffering numerous defeats and reversals that would have crushed the spirit of someone lesser. She is very loyal and generous to her supporters, even to many of those who acquiesced to her exile.

High Priest of Orlanth Thunderous.
Male, age 35. Hiording clan.

Asborn is a Storm Voice of Orlanth who refuses to stay dead. The son of a Hiording chieftain, Asborn is a boon companion of Queen Leika and twice accompanied her into Snake Pipe Hollow. He is often called Demonslayer for all the Chaos he killed. Twice he has died and twice Orlanth has returned him from the dead. He has come back to life so many times that it’s easier to come back than stay dead.

Asborn was once a close friend of Kangharl Kallaisson before he became king of the Colymar. The two fought side-by-side in many adventures and battles, including one of the seven skirmishes against the Lunars atop the Hill of Orlanth Victorious that helped spark Starbrow’s Rebellion.
When Kangharl betrayed Queen Leika in 1615, Asborn returned to his fortified farmhouse at the base of the Starfire Ridges and openly defied Blackmoor. When Leika returned to Sartar in 1625, Asborn returned to her service. He was instrumental in having her declared tribal Queen and now serves as her Chief Priest.

### Dunorl Brandgorsson
An old man in his mid-70s, Dunorl is the Storm Voice of Orlanth at Clearwine Fort’s Lightbringer Temple. He is one of the last surviving remnants of the time before the Lunar Occupation. The younger brother of two kings, Dunorl was a counselor to Prince Terasarin and Prince Salinarg, and a staunch supporter of the House of Sartar. Dunorl’s reputation was enough to survive both the Lunar Occupation and Blackmoor’s reign.

Dunorl is a quiet, patient man, who has outlasted and outlived most of his enemies. He cares little for clan and tribal rivalry, placing himself beyond petty squabbling.
Most of Dunorl’s time is taken up with leading worship and teaching spells. His son Baranthos is chief of the Ernaldoring clan; Dunorl’s sister Yanioth is the Asrelia Priestess at the Earth Temple.

### Ereneva Chan
High Priestess of Ernalda.

Female, age 29. Ernaldoring clan.

The High Priestess of Ernalda is a daughter of Beneva Chan (a previous High Priestess) and Kallai Rockbuster (king of the Colymar from 1603 to 1613). Ereneva fled to Esrolia in 1613 after her parents were exiled in the wake of Starbrow’s Rebellion and she became a Priestess of Ernalda in Nochet.
Her lover (with whom she had a daughter) died at the Battle of Pennel Ford in 1624. Following his death, Ereneva returned to Sartar and rejoined the Colymar Earth Temple.
She was surprised when the aged High Priestess took up the veil of Asrelia and abdicated, and Ereneva was named as the new High Priestess.

Ereneva is devoted to her goddess and has strong connections to Esrolia and the Shaker Temple. She favors peace except when the welfare of her temple or tribe is threatened. In clan and tribal affairs, she relies on the counsel of Baranthos and his father Dunorl. She dislikes Kallyr Starbrow and blames her for the exile of her parents.

## Points and Places

### 1. Assembly Hill
About a kilometer north of Clearwine Fort is a rocky hill overlooking the surrounding countryside. Here the free men and women of the Colymar assemble each Movement Week of Storm Season to deliberate affairs, resolve disputes between the clans, and to offer sacrifices to Orlanth Rex. The Rex statue is brought from the royal palace to Assembly Hill and sacrifices are offered for peace and victory. Several thousand people gather for the assembly. People stay with kinfolk, in inns in Clearwine Fort, or set up tents around the hill.

The assembly grounds are used throughout the year by entertainers visiting Clearwine Fort.

### 2. Small Walls
These walls were built around two centuries ago. They are only two-to-four meters high and are made of fieldstone.
Within the walls are the sacred vineyards of the Earth Temple.
Here Hareva was given the clearwine grape by the goddess.

### 3. Earth Temple and Sacred Fields
This temple is located outside of the walls of Clearwine Fort.
A major temple to Ernalda, with shrines to Asrelia, Babeester Gor, and Maran Gor, this is one of the most important Earth Temples in Dragon Pass. The temple has a shrine to Flamal as a husband-protector. It is a square building, with the inner sanctum underground. The temple’s interior is decorated with stone carvings and paintings of deities. The high priestess is always of the Ernaldoring clan, either by birth, marriage, or adoption.

The vineyards within the small walls belong to the Earth Temple and are sacred to the Ernalda cult. The rich farmlands between Clearwine Fort and The Stream also belong to the temple; the tenant farmers grow grain and wine for the cult.
Collectively, these lands are called the Sacred Fields.

### 4. Blue Mound
The decorative blue glazed tiles that once covered the stone walls of Clearwine Fort were removed as part of the ritual performed by Chief Colymar to clear out the draconic malignance. The tiles, as well as other draconic artifacts found within the fort, were broken and piled in a great burial mound. Called the Blue Mound, this is a place strong in draconic magic. Ever since the Dragonewts Dream started in 1539, dragonewts occasionally visit without comment or explanation.

### 5. City Walls
The walls of Clearwine Fort are made of massive blocks of stone and range between 4 and 12 meters in height. There is a walkway atop the walls, protected by a narrow battlement on the outer edge resembling the teeth of dragons.

### 6. Dragon’s Gate
This is the main approach into the Royal Citadel. The gate consists of two great monoliths capped with a huge lintel; the opening is roughly 3 meters tall and 3 meters wide. Above the lintel have been carved two dragons flanking a pillar (thought by some to represent Kero Fin or even the Spike).
The gate can be sealed with a pair of massive wooden doors.

### 7. Royal Citadel
The Royal Citadel is the oldest part of Clearwine Fort and its foundations date to before the Dawn. It is located on the highest part of the hill and the walls here are also the highest—ranging from 8 to 12 meters high. The citadel can be accessed from the Upper City by a ramp that leads through the King’s Gate. The royal palace and the necropolis occupy most of the interior of the citadel.

### 8. Palace of the Kings
This complex is built around the king’s audience chamber.
This rectangular hall has an open hearth that is vented through an oculus in the roof above it and surrounded by four columns. The king sits atop the magical Ivory Throne to meet with councilors, petitioners, and envoys in this chamber, and hold feasts for tribal thanes and warriors. A great bronze cauldron, a gift from Palashee Longaxe to the Colymar and large enough to hold over 400 liters of wine, is kept here for ritual celebrations.

Surrounding the audience chamber is a system of interconnected courtyards, archive rooms, workshops, living quarters, religious shrines, armories, and storerooms. The Rex Statue of the Orlanth Rex cult is kept most days in a room near the audience chamber.

The king’s household and retinue have living quarters within the palace. Most of the Royal Bodyguard reside here.

### 9. Necropolis
This is the burial ground of dead Colymar kings and their families. The dead are placed on pyres and burnt. Their cremated bones and effects are put in urns and placed underground in stone chambers, often accompanied by weapons, jewelry, and other valuables, and covered with a cairn or barrow.

The tombs have not been looted, due to their proximity to the royal palace. The necropolis is surrounded by a low stone wall.

### 10. Thunder Tower
This great round tower is made of huge blocks of stone.
It overlooks the entrance to the citadel and a secondary entrance to the Lower City. It is 25 meters tall and 20 meters in diameter. The tower is self-sufficient and may be sealed off from the rest of the city—it contains sufficient food to feed a score of people for four weeks. The tower serves as a minor temple to Orlanth Thunderous and contains barracks for twenty warriors, maintained by the king.

It is connected to the citadel by a single huge slab of stone that bridges the Main Gate into the Upper City.

### 11. Hero’s Trail
This steep and well-hidden trail leads to a sally port on the north wall. It pierces the wall at an oblique angle via a long, narrow, and unobtrusive passage through its thickness. At both ends, the passage is guarded by reinforced wooden doors, which, when opened, allow the defenders to slide through singly if the need arises.

### 12. Upper City
The buildings here are built of stone, with tiled roofs made of timber, slate, or baked clay. Buildings typically adjoin one another and open towards an inner circular street that winds its way from the Lower City to the Citadel. Most houses are the residences of wealthy families, although the district includes two inns, stables, a red-smith, and several temples (including the Lightbringers Temple). The Lightbringers Temple and the weekly market are the heart of the district and the center of city life.

### 13. Market
This plaza is the location of the weekly market. A towering, bronze statue of Issaries, the god of communication and trade, is on the south side. On the north side is the Lightbringers Temple. Jaranstand White Grape is the market priest, and he resolves disputes between merchants.

### 14. Lightbringers Temple
This building across from the market consists of a central courtyard open to the sky, surrounded by stone corridors.
There are altars for offering sacrifices to each of the gods of the temple. A chamber along the rear of the temple serves as the sanctuary where cult images and sacred regalia are kept.
Portions of the building double as a hospital.

The temple serves as a major temple to Orlanth Adventurous, with minor temples to Orlanth Thunderous and Rex, shrines to all the Lightbringers, and an associated shrine to Ernalda. Nearby are the homes of the tribal priests.

### 15. White Grape Inn
A freshly painted sign of a cluster of white grapes swings in the breeze outside this inn, one of two in Clearwine Fort.
Sora Goodseller, an Issaries priest from the Holy Country, recently purchased this inn. Travelers, merchants, and foreigners patronize the inn. This large stone building has two floors, a cellar, an attached warehouse, and stables. The main floor of the building is the tavern and common room for meals and entertainment. The upper floor is sleeping rooms for rent. The cellar contains foodstuffs and wine.
There is always at least one mercenary guard here. Prices for food, drink, and rooms are as per the RuneQuest rules, page 413.

### 16. Old Gate and Ramp
This is the original entrance to the Upper City, dating back to the First Age. It is accessed by a ramp paved with stones.
The gate can be sealed with heavy wooden doors, although they have not been for more than a century.

### 17. Lower City
This area is settled by farmers, crafters, and foreigners.
Buildings typically adjoin one another and open towards an inner circular street that winds its way to the Upper City.
There are many workshops here.

The Lower City is separated from the Upper City by a steep cliff and by the old walls of the Upper City. There are two gates leading out of the Lower City into the Sacred Fields. These gates can be closed with wooden doors.

### 18. Sartar’s Tower
King Sartar built this tower in 1500. It is of formidable craftsmanship, 31 meters tall and 15 meters wide at the base.
The tower is used as a watchtower and as a signaling beacon and manned by several archers of the king.

### 19. Temple of the Tribe
This open-air altar is where black bulls are sacrificed by tribal leaders to the wyter of the Colymar tribe, as part of the Orlanth Rex cult.

### 20. The Ram’s Head
A skeletal ram’s head with immense horns hangs above the entry to this three-story stone and timber building. Hareva Weaver, Issaries priest and counselor of the king operates the inn. The ground floor is the tavern and common room for meals and entertainment. The upper floors have both private rooms and dormitory style barracks for visitors. Prices for food, drink, and rooms as per the RuneQuest rules, page 413

### 21. Ernaldoring Hall
This is the home of the Ernaldoring chieftain and the center of his household. It is a large, square, two-story building, built last century in the Esrolian style, ordered around a large central courtyard.
