 The Dragon of Thunder Hills
This scenario has the adventurers confront one of the most fearsome monsters of Glorantha—the dream dragon. A clever party has plenty of opportunities to arm themselves with weapons and magic that will give them an edge against the dream dragon; however, a reckless or foolhardy party will likely meet their demise at the claws and breath of Yerezum Storn. Success against the dragon marks the adventurers as truly heroic material and dramatically enhances their wealth and Reputation.

This dream dragon was worshiped as an incarnation of a regional goddess by the previous inhabitants during the era of the Third Council (also called the Empire of the Wyrms Friends) but vanished nearly six centuries ago.
She is one of the many draconic entities awakened because of the Dragonrise and emerged from the Queen’s Tomb not long ago.

## Current Events
This scenario is assumed to take place in Fertility Week of Storm Season 1625, although as always gamemasters are welcome to set the scenario whenever works best for their campaign. This is the snowiest time of the year, and there might be as much as 2D20 cm of snow on the ground. The temperature is hovering around freezing, and the snow can quickly turn into rain and then back again to snow. Winter thunderstorms are not uncommon.

The situation in Dragon Pass has stabilized somewhat.
The Lunar Army has retreated to its winter quarters in AldaChur. In Boldhome, Kallyr Starbrow has been recognized as Prince of Sartar by all the tribes, including even Queen Leika (whose acknowledgement of Kallyr was notably reluctant and guarded).

## Dragon Raid!
The adventurers are traveling south, through the snow along the herder’s path between Apple Lane and the Nymie Vale.
They are headed to Clearwine Fort. To the east, the thick woods of Tarndisi’s Grove can be seen; to the west looms the high cliffs of the Starfire Ridges.

The gamemaster should encourage the players to discuss why their adventurers are traveling to Clearwine Fort.
Perhaps they are headed there for seasonal worship or to bring some matter up with the tribal leaders.

Once the players have eased into their roles, the gamemaster should ask for Listen skill rolls. Those adventurers who succeed hear a loud roar off in the distance.

The gamemaster should then ask for Scan rolls.
Adventurers who heard the roar get a +25% bonus to their Scan skill. Those who succeed see the ominous outline of a winged serpent with four limbs rising high into the air above the nearby Nymie Vale, a cultivated area of small farms and vineyards.

If they all fail their rolls, they are surprised as the dragon flies directly over them, terrifying their mounts (mounted characters should make a Ride roll to avoid being thrown).
The dragon spirals around, then flies towards the Thunder Hills.

The adventurers should be encouraged to check out where the dragon came from—the dragon may have preyed on a local settlement or stead! The adventurers may check against their Loyalty (Colymar Tribe) or Honor Passion to be motivated to check out what happened.

### Jenstal’s Stead

![](/docs/runequest/adventures/09-map-of-jenstal's-stead.png){#map-jenstals-stead}

A short run towards the valley brings the adventurers to Jenstal’s Stead, a small collection of farmhouses and other buildings. Fields and vineyards surround the buildings.
Jenstal is a member of the Anmangarn clan. If any of the adventurers are of the Colymar tribe, Jenstal is related to them by marriage (the gamemaster should just come up with whatever works best if there is more than one adventurer clan).

- Animal Corral: Low stonewalls surround this corral. Scattered within are the corpses of six partially eaten cattle and the mangled corpses of two humans. Here Jenstal and Hulik died trying to defend the livestock from the dragon.
- Barn: The corpse of a mangled ox and a human (Berke) partially block the entrance to the barn. Within the barn are the intact and undamaged corpses of two cows and one ox—Berke managed to get these animals into the barn before they succumbed to the dragon’s poisonous breath.
- Inner Hearth: In the inner hearth are the surviving members of Jenstal’s family: Arndala, Korostal, Penene the Younger, Arnstal, and Eona. The corpse of Penene the Elder (Jenstal’s mother) is here as well (she earlier succumbed to the dragon’s poisonous breath).

When the adventurers arrive, Penene and Eona are tending to Arndala and Korostal, both of whom are badly wounded. Arnstal huddles in a corner. Penene pleads, “Can you help my mother and uncle? They are badly hurt and not moving, although I do not think they are dead. Where is father and Hulik? Did they escape? Please, help us!” If the adventurers tell of the fate of Jenstal and Hulik, Penene and Arnstal break down in tears. Arndala weakly says, “There will be time enough for vengeance later. The tribe must be told. Please, take my children to Clearwine Fort and tell the queen what happened here!”

## Clearwine
The audience chamber and great hall of the Colymar kings is a rectangular stone building, fronted by an open, columned portion, and roofed with terracotta tiles. Within is an open hearth surrounded by four columns; smoke from the hearth is vented through an opening in the roof. Frescoes depicting divinities and heroes decorate the white walls. On one side of the hearth, sits Queen Leika Black Spear atop the Ivory Throne of the Colymar kings, a sacred relic that was stolen from the old kings of Tarsh. Consulting with Queen Leika are several other tribal notables: Dunorl the Old, the Chief Priest of Orlanth Thunderous; Asborn Thriceborn, Storm Voice and confidant of Queen Leika; and Hastur the Lawspeaker, Chief Priest of Lhankor Mhy.
With news of the dragon raid, the adventurers get an immediate audience with the tribal queen and her counselors.
If the adventurers wish to impress the tribal leaders, they can make an Orate or a Loyalty (Colymar Tribe) roll, or a Loyalty (Queen Leika) roll.
Queen Leika is very troubled by the news. She asks that servants take care of the survivors from Jenstal’s Stead and then ask her advisors what they think.

### Dunorl the Storm Voice

Q: Where did the dragon come from?

A: “It seems likely that the dragon came soon after the Dragonrise. Perhaps that woke up all the old draconic things in Dragon Pass and we are only now noticing it?”

Q: How can one fight a dragon?
A: “If you try to fight the dragon using nothing but brute force, you will die. But if you are clever and know the secrets of the gods, you may succeed.

“From the beginning of time, Orlanth fought the dragons to make the world a place for gods and mortals. Orlanth slew Aroka and Sh’harkazeel, and many that were lesser. Heroes like Alakoring Dragonbreaker have repeated Orlanth’s deeds and broken dragons.
“Orlanth used cleverness and not mere brute strength to fight the dragons. For example, Orlanth used his winds to blow away Aroka’s poison breath*. In some stories, Orlanth used his Sandals of Darkness** to sneak up upon Aroka; in others he blinded the dragon with his weapons† or was protected by the Shield of Aran‡. Orlanth found his enemy’s secret weakness and used magical weapons, like his Flint Sword. Such cleverness is why Orlanth is King of the Gods.”

* This might be a poetic description of the Increase/Decrease Wind spell or an air elemental.
** This might be a reference to the Dark Walk spell.
† This might be a reference to the Lightning or Thunderbolt spells.
‡ This is another name for the Earth Shield spell.

## Hunt for the Dragon
After listening to her counselors (and questioning the adventurers) Queen Leika decides that she and her companions will search the Starfire Ridges for signs of the dragon’s lair. She asks the adventurers to go to Tarndisi’s Grove and ask the Dryad about the dragon. The queen further asks the adventurers to investigate the Thunder Hills for signs of the dragon.
If the adventurers agree, they get an experience check on their Loyalty (Colymar Tribe), Loyalty (Queen Leika), or Honor Passion (player’s choice) and Dunorl provides them with a Bag of Winds. “I’ve captured a wind in this bag and if you release it, it will carry out one task for you—unless you can bind the winds to your will” (a reference to the Orlanth cult’s Command Cult Spirit spell).
If the adventurers refuse, then go on to the If the Adventurers Refuse the Adventure sidebar below.

## Tarndisi the Dryad

The forest called Tarndisi’s Grove is about ten kilometers from Clearwine Fort. It is a thickly wooded area of oak, pine, and fir, with irregular labyrinthine hollows up to several meters deep. The terrain is too rugged for wheeled vehicles and mounts must be led through the tangle of roots, branches, and hollows. It is forbidden to cut or burn the trees of the grove.

### If the Adventurers Refuse the Adventure
Sometimes adventurers refuse the call to adventure. If the adventurers refuse to hunt the dragon and insist on having Queen Leika solve the problem, the gamemaster has options:

- Provide the adventurers with additional motivation. Perhaps the dragon raids Apple Lane or the dragon devours a herd belonging to an adventurer. Or maybe the dragon kills a member of an adventurer’s family or clan. Or perhaps omens and nightmares haunt the dreams of one of more adventurers— something that they are Devoted to or Love is destroyed, or their Honor is disparaged. It could be also that they require financial incentives, such as a fine riding horse (worth 150 L) and a bronze torc (worth 25 L) emblazoned with symbols showing the friendship of the Colymar clan. Unfortunately, such a negotiation will earn the adventurers +1d3% Reputation as mercenaries, rather than acting nobly or out of loyalty to the clan.
- Let Queen Leika attack the dragon instead of the adventurers. The gamemaster can decide if she is successful. If she is, the adventurers get no further reward, but their Reputation as cowards grows within the clan and tribe. Alternately, the gamemaster can decide Leika’s raid is a failure, resulting in several deaths and a badly wounded queen, followed by a rampage by the dragon.

It is easy to get lost in the grove—the adventurers must succeed with a Track or Search roll or be hopelessly lost.
A small elf patrol (see page 52) shadows the adventurers as soon as they enter the grove. The elves are using their Hide skill to avoid detection. If the adventurers can make their way through the grove, the elves merely follow them from a short distance.
If, however, the adventurers get lost or their party includes noted elf foes (such as dwarves or trolls), the elf patrol confronts the trespassers. They demand (in Tradetalk and in Earthtongue) that the adventurers lay down their weapons and come with them—if the adventurers refuse, the elves attack with their bows.
Otherwise the elves only make themselves known once the adventurers find their way to the actual “grove”—a small open stand of about a dozen redwood trees. The central redwood tree is the tallest, some 80 meters high. There are numerous smaller oak trees growing around the stand (these are the War Trees).
If they haven’t already made themselves noticed, the elf patrol now appears amongst the trees. They warn (in Tradetalk and in Earthtongue) the adventurers that this is a sacred place and that weapons are forbidden within. If the adventurers wish to enter the grove and speak with the dryad, they are to place their weapons on the ground. The elves get noticeably agitated if any iron weapons are revealed, but take no additional actions—however, they will treat the adventurers as hostile in the future.
If the adventurers do not comply, the elves repeat their demand. A second refusal results in attack by the elves and 1D6+3 War Trees. They will continue fighting until the adventurers surrender or are slain, or they themselves are defeated.
If the adventurers comply, Tarndisi emerges from the base of the central redwood tree. She has copper skin and green hair.
Unless the adventurers are trolls or dwarves, carry iron, or belong to elf-hating cults (such as Zorak Zoran), Tarndisi is friendly. She is willing to talk freely with members of associated cults; otherwise she demands that the adventurers exchange a favor for a favor—in this case, they must bring her the tusks of Redeye the Boar (see Pig Hollow, page 78).
She directs the elf patrol to escort the adventurers to Pig Hollow. If the adventurers are successful, they are to escort them back to the grove. If the adventurers have already slain Redeye, they need only present her with one of the tusks.
Assuming the adventurers prove their friendship one way or another, Tarndisi is willing to answer their questions. The dryad predates the Dawn and has known many heroes and outlasted even more kingdoms and empires. Tarndisi does not volunteer information, but answers questions as best she can. Some examples:

> **The Obsidian Sword**
> This broadsword is made of a single, impossibly long, piece of obsidian. It does 1D8+2 damage plus damage bonus and has 10 hit points. It is otherwise equivalent to a broadsword. The blade is especially deadly against dragons and dragonkind: damage by the sword ignores all draconic armor including dragon scales, dragonbone armor, and even draconic spells as if the armor did not exist. It can be repaired with magic but cannot be repaired through conventional means by any other than secrets held by dragonewt craftsmen.

Q: What do you know about Dragons?

A: “The True Dragons have always been here—Dragon Pass is their nest. They are both benevolent and malevolent, no different from fire, storm, or water. They are beyond this world and rarely involve themselves of it, except in their dreams. Our kind does not disturb them, nor tries to propitiate them, but your people have awakened them again. The last time did not turn out well for any mortal race, especially your own, but perhaps that is the nature of your kind.”

Q: What do you know about the Dragon that has recently been attacking these lands?

A: “Yerezum Storn has awakened again. I remember her from the third and final Unity Council. She dreamt she was the ancestor of the people here, so she might communicate with your kind. Perhaps she does again, but no longer recognizes you. Or worse, perhaps she does.” (The Unity Council is a reference to the Empire of the Wyrms Friends)

Q: Where is the Dragon?

A: “Yerezum Storn was worshiped by your people as the manifestation of the sleeping queen of these lands. Your people used to speak to her at the tomb where the queen still sleeps. Perhaps she awaits you there?” (A successful Homeland Lore roll reveals this is a reference to the Queen’s Tomb.)

Q: What does it want?

A: “Like all dreams, Yerezum Storn wants you to join in her dream—she wants your kind to worship her, listen to her, let her be queen of these lands again. True Dragons care nothing about such things, but they dream of things that they ignore, reject, and refuse. Either join in her dream or dispel it.”

If the adventurers plan to immediately return to Clearwine Fort after speaking with Tarndisi, the gamemaster should go straight to Spot the Dragon, page 111.

## Investigating the Thunder Hills
The Thunder Hills are rugged hills; their lower elevations and valleys filled with oak and pine groves, and their higher elevations covered with grass and stone. There is little farming here, although herding and hunting is common.
The hills are littered with tors and outcrops that are difficult to distinguish from the many stone ruins.
The Old Grave and the Queen’s Tomb are at opposite ends of the Thunder Hills. There is a dragonewt plinth in the central hills, right near Pig Hollow. At some point, the gamemaster should have the adventurers encounter the scenes described in Meet the Dragonewts and Spot the Dragon (below). These scenes can take place wherever and in whatever order the gamemaster desires.

### Spot the Dragon
At some point the gamemaster should ask the adventurers to check their Listen skill. Those who succeed hear the now-familiar echoing roar of a dragon in the distance.
Afterwards, the gamemaster should ask them to check their Scan skill (with a +25% bonus to adventurers who made their Listen skill).
Those who succeed with their Scan skill see the sinuous shape of a winged serpent flying towards the area of the Queen’s Tomb. The dragon spirals above the hill for a while and then descends to the hilltop, now out of sight.

### Meet the Dragonewts
At some point prior to coming to the Queen’s Tomb, the adventurers should encounter Sarna Ya’qal’s dragonewt band.
An ideal place to stage this is near one of the dragonewt plinths, although it can be staged anywhere.
As they travel, the adventurers see a band of eight dragonewts—three (the tailed priest and two beaked dragonewts) are mounted on demi-birds and the rest (all crested dragonewts) are on foot. The dragonewts make no effort to be hidden and head directly towards the adventurers. If the adventurers flee, the dragonewts pursue.
The dragonewts retreat if attacked.
The dragonewts stop a little more than 20 meters from the adventurers. One of the crested dragonewts (Slave to Lies) comes to the adventurers with its head near the ground.
The dragonewt’s jaws and tongue have been surgically altered.
It greets the adventurers with the plea “I beg to speak with you for my master” in several different languages until they acknowledge it. The dragonewt tries first in Tradetalk; if there is no reaction it tries again in Heortling, then Tarshite, New Pelorian, and then in Pure Horse Tongue, before restarting (much slower) in Tradetalk. Whichever language the adventurers react to is the language Slave to Lies uses with them.
Once acknowledged, Slave to Lies says: “I am the voice of Sarna Ya’qal, who knows that you seek the wandering dream. Sarna Ya’qal asks you to liberate the dream from its prison and offers you the tool of its liberation. You must cut off the head of the dream. When the task is done, bring the tool and the dream’s head to the nearest dragonewt plinth and place both at the base of the plinth. If you do this, Sarna Ya’qal shall be indebted to you for as long as you live.” If the adventurers have any questions, Slave to Lies scurries back to Sarna Ya’qal to repeat the question in Auld Wyrmish, receives an answer in Auld Wyrmish, and then scurries back to the adventurers to give the answer. Sarna Ya’qal provides no information as to why it wants to “liberate” Yerezum Storn or about the nature of the dragon but is willing to provide the location of the dragon’s lair and useful information such as the form of its breath attacks.
If the adventurers agree, then Slave to Lies scurries back again to Sarna Ya’qal and returns with the Obsidian Sword and offers it to the adventurers. The dragonewts watch to see if the adventurers take the sword. Regardless of what the adventurers do, the dragonewts then depart to the nearest dragonewt plinth and travel away on the dragonewt road.

## The Queen’s Tomb

![](/docs/runequest/adventures/09-map-of-queen's-tomb.png){#map-queens-tomb}

This tomb-sanctuary in the Thunder Hills is the resting place of Orgorvale Summer, the demi-goddess daughter of King Vingkot and granddaughter of Orlanth. Her cult died out in the Second Age, after the dream dragon Yerezum Storn was summoned here by draconic magic. Yerezum Storn has recently reappeared and resides within the Queen’s Tomb.
The Queen’s Tomb is well known to the locals, but widely avoided by local herders as haunted. It is located atop a stony hill and covered with a huge mound of earth and stone some 15 meters tall and 50 meters in diameter. The Queen’s Tomb itself is carved into the solid rock.
A local legend holds that the mound was raised in the Lesser Darkness by the gods.

### 1. Entrance
The entrance is on the west side of the mound with a stone gallery leading below. Above the entrance is a terrace with ten huge statues of five gods and five goddesses seated atop thrones. The heads of the statues were long ago removed from their bodies and now litter the ground near the entrance. Each head is about a meter tall and most have been defaced.

The entrance passage leads down a series of steps to the Antechamber.

### 2. Antechamber
This stone chamber is barren. Spiraling Air Runes, Man Runes with horned headdresses and lightning, snakes, and other human figures have been painted on the walls with red ochre.

### 3. Central Chamber
This chamber has four passages, each with a trilithon entryway. Thirty-two niches have been carved into the stone. Each niche is about 70 cm high, 50 cm wide, and 50 cm deep. Each once held an elaborately decorated cremation urn, accompanied by a libation cup and food plate; however, many of the urns have been broken or stolen.

Second Sight reveals that this room has numerous discorporate spirits. Touching or in any other way disturbing the contents of the tomb or the urns by anyone not a member of the cult of Orgorvale is the equivalent of challenging one of the spirits to spirit combat. The spirits are normal random spirits (CHA 3D6, POW 3D6+6, INT 2D6+6). If they get the chance, they will attempt to possess the body of their opponent (Spirit Combat 60%).

**Treasures:** Twenty-three of the libation cups are still intact, and 11 of the plates. Each would be worth 50 L if a buyer can be found who is not squeamish about purchasing grave goods.

### 4. Speaking Room
One hundred twenty funerary niches have been carved in the stone walls of this room. Most still contain cremation urns, libation cups, and food plates.

The room has a constant low murmuring sound. If anyone makes a Listen roll, they can hear whispers from the urns. The whispers are in Spiritspeech. Many of the whispers are unintelligible, but those that can be heard are complaining about hunger and neglect.

Second Sight reveals that this room is swarming with discorporate spirits. Touching or in any other way disturbing the urns by anyone not a member of the cult of Orgorvale is the equivalent of challenging one of the spirits to spirit combat. The spirits are normal random spirits (CHA 3D6, POW 3D6+6, INT 2D6+6). If they get the chance, they will attempt to possess the body of their opponent (Spirit Combat 65%).

### 5. Painted Room
The walls and ceilings of this room have been covered in colorful and ornate murals depicting events from the Lesser Darkness, particularly the lives of the daughters and sons of the demigod Vingkot (the Vingkotlings) and the deeds of Orlanth. The pillars leading to the Main Temple have been carved with figures of Orlanth and Ernalda. The style of both is naturalistic and flowing.

A careful study of the frescoes adds +1D6% to Cult Lore (Orlanth).

### 6. Snake Pit
This room contains a circular pit carved into the stone nearly two meters wide and seven meters deep. A small faience statue (approximately 20 cm long) of a woman with a crown of five serpents riding atop a draconic serpent has been placed in a wall niche. The serpent is modeled on Yerezum Storn’s draconic form; the woman is modeled on her human form.

**Treasures:** Within the pit are several centuries of sacrifices to Yerezum Storn. The bulk of the offerings are nearly 2,000 terracotta snakes (some painted and some plain), but there are also cattle and sheep bones. A careful Search also reveals three *klanths*, one *utuma*, nine ruined and bent bronze broadswords, 753 silver coins from the Empire of the Wyrms Friends (each weighing roughly the same as a Lunar but potentially worth more to scholars), and 25 gold Wheels displaying the Sun Dragon instead of the Emperor.

### 7. Sleeping Goddess Chamber
This chamber has been hewn out of solid rock. It has been colorfully painted with murals like in the Painted Room, showing numerous gods and goddesses. In the center of the room is a rectangular cuboid, one meter wide, one meter deep, and two meters long. Atop it is a greenstone statue of a goddess wearing a skirt made of strips of lapis lazuli. She has anklets, bracelets, and a pectoral cape made of copper. The goddess is reclining as if sleeping.

Wound around the base of the cuboid is all 15 meters of Yerezum Storn (see The Dragon—Yerezum Storn, following).

**Treasure:** Around the edges of the room are scores of votive images, depicting worshipers and offerings. Most are made of terracotta, but many are made of bronze. There is about 70 L (10 ENC) worth of bronze figurines. A niche in the rear wall holds several more treasures: a pair of carved jade horses—one bearing a crowned woman and one a man wielding a spear (600 L); a fine bronze torc and matching bracelets which look like twining serpents (1,250 L); and a copper mirror polished on both sides and giving a fine reflection (500 L).

## The Dragon — Yerezum Storn
Yerezum Storn is the free-roaming thought of a True Dragon, ensnared in the material world. She dreams herself as Orgorvale Summer, the Gods Age ruler of these lands and ancient founder. She craves worship, offerings, and experiences in the material world. Her efforts to fulfill these cravings never satisfy her and are a source of endless suffering and rage. Once she was largely benevolent towards mortals but her continued ensnarement in the material world has made her cruel and malevolent. She is greedy, vain, and arrogant, but is bound by her word.
The Third Council ordered the local population to worship Yerezum Storn as the incarnation of Orgorvale Summer. For more than two centuries, she resided here, receiving worship. Her cult died when the dragonewts betrayed the Third Council, and for many centuries she has been absent. She has returned because of the Dragonrise.
In her normal draconic form, Yerezum Storn is some 15 meters long and weighs over 1,300 kg. She is long and serpentine; she has an elongated head with a horned frill and her legs are used primarily for gripping, not movement. Her scales are blackish purple in color.
Yerezum Storn can take human form. She wears a dark purple skirt and a crown of five serpents.
Yerezum Storn should initially look like a dragon, circled around the Sleeping Lady in the Main Temple. Unless the adventurers immediately attack, she will transform herself into human form to communicate with them. She announces herself (in archaic Theyalan) as “Yerezum Storn, your goddess once, and your goddess to be.”

### Bargaining with Yerezum Storn
Yerezum Storn wants reverence, homage, supplication, and tribute; to get it, she is willing to kill as many people as it takes. However, she would prefer to get what she wants without any more effort—thus she is perfectly willing to bargain with the adventurers to begin her cult.

Yerezum Storn demands that everyone present sacrifice at least 3 magic points to her and that she be offered an immediate sacrifice of food and tribute. Going forward she wants a regular (weekly) offering of magic points, food, and tribute. Yerezum Storn appoints one of the adventurers her first “priest” to organize her “cult”. She will demand ever-increasing amounts of offerings from her worshipers.
Yerezum Storn offers no magic, no divine intervention, and none of the usual benefits of a cult. She is willing to teach Auld Wyrmish to her priest. The main thing she provides her worshipers is protection (primarily from herself ). Failure to provide her with the worship she demands is punished by Yerezum Storn with fire and poison.

### Fighting Yerezum Storn
In combat, Yerezum Storn relies on her bite and breath attacks. Outside of the Queen’s Tomb, she typically attacks using her Fire Breath and then closes in. Inside the Queen’s Tomb, she is far more likely to use her poisonous breath (as she does not want to damage the temple).
If the adventurers start to get the upper hand, Yerezum Storn will try to flee outside where she can fly and use her Fire Breath to best advantage. If she cannot flee, Yerezum Storn likely takes human form and tries to bargain with them.

### The Head of Yerezum Storn
If the adventurers kill Yerezum Storn and cut off her head with the Obsidian Sword, the head opens its eyes and speaks in archaic Theyalan:

“Do not destroy what is left of me or give my remains to the traitors. Let me serve you in exchange for letting me continue to exist. What power and knowledge that remains with me is at your command.” If the adventurers accept the offer, the head dutifully serves whoever carries the head. It functions much like a bound spirit, allowing its wielder access to its magic points.
It can cast spells and answer questions as commanded by its wielder.

### Awakening Orgorvale Summer
If Yerezum Storn is defeated, the spirit of Orgorvale Summer is freed from the Empire of the Wyrms Friends’ magic that has imprisoned her. The Sleeping Lady sits upright on her dais. Her divine presence fills the temple-tomb and she wishes to be worshiped again. Her Runes are Man and Earth—she is the granddaughter of Orlanth as well—and adventurers connected to those Runes or to the Orlanth cult are invited to create her cult by sacrificing at least 1 point of POW to gain an equal number of Rune points with Orgorvale Summer.
Orgorvale Summer provides all Common Rune spells as well as the special Rune spells Summon Ancestor, Summon Spirit Teacher, and Summon Specific Ancestor. Her magic is geographically restricted to the lands roughly between the River and Prax—outside of there, she has no power.
Orgorvale Summer requires 2 magic points be sacrificed to her on her seasonal Holy Days (determined by her cult founders, although her High Holy Day will be the anniversary of Yerezum Storn’s defeat). She is associated with both Orlanth (her grandfather) and Ernalda (her grandmother), and friendly towards the deities of Earth and Air. She does not hate or fear Darkness or Fire and is friendly towards both Argan Argar and Yelmalio (and Elmal).

Orgorvale Summer hates Chaos and is hostile towards any self-proclaimed heirs of the Golden Empire (Dara Happa, the Lunar Empire, etc.). She favors the skills of Ride and Herd and suggests to her worshipers that they reduce the cost to her worshipers, and that they sell the following spells to their fellow worshipers: Heal 1, Strength (2 pt.), Vigor (2 pt.).
Queen’s Tomb begins as Orgorvale Summer’s only holy place but provides all her special Rune spells. Any ancestor summoned within the Queen’s Tomb is Friendly or Neutral.
At this point, it is up to the gamemaster and the adventurers to develop the cult. Her cult may become a local cult of Orlanth or Ernalda, part of the Colymar tribal cult, or an independent spirit cult.

## Afterwards
After they have confronted Yerezum Storn, the adventurers need to decide if they fulfill their end of the bargain with the dragonewts.

### Fulfilling the Bargain
If the adventurers bring the Obsidian Sword and the Head of Yerezum Storn to the Dragonewt Plinth, a delegation of dragonewts led by Sarna Ya’qal arrive along the dragonewt road. Sarna Ya’qal takes the Obsidian Sword and the Head, and Slave to Lies says to whichever adventurer carried the Head, “My master is in your debt. Until you die, you may call upon him to fulfill that debt. Bring purple nardus flowers to this plinth, and my master shall come.”

It is the gamemaster’s decision whether the debt is discharged by a single request or whether the characters can ask for Sarna Ya’qal’s aid until the adventurer who carried the Head dies.

### Betraying the Dragonewts

The adventurers may decide to betray the dragonewts and keep the Obsidian Sword and/or the Head of Yerezum Storn.
If they do not present both at the Dragonewt Plinth by Sacred Time, Sarns Ya’qal and the dragonewts shall hunt down whoever possesses either item with the intent of killing them and retaking the items. Sarna Ya’qal does not care how many times it must die to reclaim the items—it cannot ascend to the next stage of dragonewt consciousness until it has completed its mission.

### Other Rewards

For defeating Yerezum Storn, Queen Leika and the other tribal leaders assign the group the rights to four hides of land scattered throughout the Nymie Vale, which also gives the adventurers a check on their Loyalty (Colymar Tribe)
Passion or start that Passion at 60%. The land granted to the adventurers includes Jenstal’s Stead and the survivors could make excellent tenants. The gamemaster and players are encouraged to develop other tenants.
Additionally, Queen Leika gives the adventurers a herd of 20 cattle from the royal herd, which gives the adventurers a check on their Loyalty (Queen Leika) Passion or lets them start that Passion at 60%.
If none of the adventurers hold the rank of thane (e.g., Thane of Apple Lane), the queen gives one of the adventurers the rank of tribal thane.

## Rewards and Consequences
Stopping Yerezum Storn is a heroic deed and marks the adventurers as notable individuals throughout Sartar.

### Reputation
Depending on their actions, the adventurers get one or more of the following increases to their Reputation:

- Defeating Yerezum Storn is worth +2D6% to an adventurer’s Reputation and raises the adventurers’ CHA by +1 (if they helped in the fight).
- Keeping the head of Yerezum Storn (and betraying the dragonewts) is worth an additional +1D6% to the wielder’s Reputation. The possessor of the head may add +1 to CHA.
- Calling Sarna Ya’qal to fulfill its debt is worth +1D6% to an adventurer’s Reputation.
- Founding the cult of Orgorvale is worth an additional +1D6% to the founders’ Reputation and may add +1 to CHA.
- Founding the cult of Yerezum Storn is worth +1D6% to the founders’ Reputation and may add +1 to CHA.

The maximum CHA bonus is +3. Ambitious or clever adventurers will hire minstrels or scribes to record their deeds for an additional +1D3% Reputation each, up to the total possible reward for all deeds combined.
