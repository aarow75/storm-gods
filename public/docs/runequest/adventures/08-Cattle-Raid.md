# Cattle Raid
In this scenario, the adventurers are recruited to stop a pair of saber-toothed cats that have been threatening the sacred cattle of a local clan. While defending the herd, they come into confrontation with warriors from a rival clan who threaten to take the cattle. How the adventurers handle this confrontation determines whether there will be a bloody feud between two clans or even tribal war.

## Background
The Varmandi are a small clan of the Colymar tribe of around 450 members. Their lands are near Apple Lane.
Their main settlement is Oakton, a small walled village surrounding the Oak of Vengeance (which is the clan’s cult sanctuary). They have a long history of bloody feuds, especially with the Orleving clan of the Malani tribe. The Varmandi are notorious for having started several tribal wars with their feuds.

The clan has comparatively little arable land—only about 60 hides worth. Most of the clan’s wealth is in the form of their livestock. Some of the animals are needed for plowing and daily support, but the Varmandi keep about 400 cattle and 1200 sheep in the hills. Of these, some 100 cattle belong to the tribal Orlanth temple (whose high priest is the tribal king) and are sacred to the local Orlanth cult. The livestock graze primarily in the Gejay Hills, which often puts them in conflict with the neighboring Malani tribe, specifically the Orleving clan.

The Varmandi suffered terribly during the Lunar Occupation, and after the previous chieftain was killed in Starbrow’s Rebellion, the women of the Varmandi chose Gentle Vastyr to lead the clan away from war. Vastyr died during the Great Winter, and in 1622, the Wind Lord Korol Serpent-Tongue became clan chief. Since then, the clan has taken increasingly violent actions to aggressively defend their interests.

### Current Events
This scenario is assumed to take place in early Dark Season 1625, less than a season after __Defending Apple Lane__, although gamemasters are free to set it at whatever date works best for their campaign. Kallyr Starbrow has captured the city of Jonstown and repelled a last-ditch Lunar counterattack at the Battle of Dangerford. The Colymar did not participate in either of those events.

The situation in Dragon Pass is still very tumultuous but Sartar is fully freed from the Lunar Empire. Old rivalries between clans and tribes are quickly reemerging to the fore now that the Lunar threat has receded.

Early Dark Season is the onset of winter and the first smattering of snow has just fallen. This is when the fabled clearwine grapes are harvested near Clearwine Fort to make ice wine. Livestock are taken from the hilltops into the valleys and ravines to get fodder.

## Starting the Adventure

Several ways to start the adventure are presented below. If one of the adventurers is the Thane of Apple Lane, then looking after the sacred herd is simply one of the duties of the office. If the adventurers are members of a local clan or are members of the Colymar tribe, they might just be told to defend the sacred cattle by the appropriate authorities.

Alternately, the adventurers might simply be hired to defend the herd by the owner of the Tin Inn in Apple Lane.

### Notes on livestock
In the average Heortling clan of 1200 people, there are about 200 hides worth of productive wealth (both land and livestock). In Sartar, about two-thirds of that wealth is agricultural, and about one-third is livestock beyond that necessary to work the land.

The average clan owns about 1000 cattle, with some 250 being kept on the individual farmsteads year-round (to help with plowing, milk, etc.), and about 750 being herded into the hills each spring, only to return with the coming of winter. There are also about 2000 sheep, which are driven many kilometers (often more than a day away from the clan’s lands) in search of pasture. Not all these cattle belong to the clan; most clans also are responsible for a herd of cattle belonging to the tribal Orlanth temple (whose high priest is the tribal king).

On average, one herder, assisted by a trained shadowcat, can drive 20 cattle or 50 sheep. Pasturing herds are usually about 100 animals.

These large pastoral herds are owned principally by temples, particularly by the local Orlanth temple. Cattle are primarily consumed in cult sacrificial feasts. The tribal Orlanth temple maintains the king’s household and cult activities with their own herds that are then cared for by the clans of the tribe. The tribal herds range in size from just a few hundred cattle (in the case of a small tribe like the Amad or Tovtaros) to more than 2000 cattle (such as the Colymar). Each clan is responsible for some part of the tribal livestock and receives much of the benefit of the herd (primarily milk and some of the offspring). However, they must provide the temple with renders of dairy products, as well as provide cattle for cult sacrifice.

### Option One: The Thane of Apple Lane

The Thane of Apple Lane is the representative of the tribal king in the region and can be expected to defend the sacred herd. The tribal queen summons the adventurer to feast at Clearwine Fort and holds an audience with the adventurer at the Palace of the Kings– a sign of respect, honor, and loyalty!
The adventurer eats good roasted meat, drinks clearwine out of a communal kylix, and is even given a silver spiral bracelet worth 50 L. Afterwards, the queen tells the adventurers that a pair of monsters have been preying on the sacred cattle herd cared for by the Varmandi clan. She asks them to travel to the Varmandi lands and kill the monsters. She then summons a young Varmandi herder named Heortarl to act as their guide.

### Option Two: For Clan, Cult, or Tribe
Adventurers from the Colymar tribe may be asked to defend the sacred herd by a tribal, cult, or clan leader, especially if they have a Passion of 70% or more with an appropriate leader or community. Some options based on the highest appropriate Passion possessed by one or more adventurers: Loyalty (Colymar Tribe) or Loyalty (Queen Leika): Like the Thane of Apple Lane option, the tribal queen summons the adventurer to feast at Clearwine Fort and holds an audience with the adventurer at the Palace of the Kings—a sign of respect, honor, and loyalty! The adventurer eats good roasted meat, drinks clearwine out of a communal kylix, and is even given a silver spiral bracelet worth 50 L. Afterwards, the queen tells the adventurer that a pair of monsters have been preying on the sacred cattle herd cared for by the Varmandi clan. She asks them to travel to the Varmandi lands and kill the monsters. She then summons a young Varmandi herder named Heortarl to act as their guide.

- Loyalty (clan): Adventurers with a high Loyalty
Passion to one of the clans of the Colymar tribe are asked by their chief to aid the Varmandi clan by killing the monsters that threaten the sacred cattle herd. The chief emphasizes ties of marriage and/or cult with the Varmandi, as well as the opportunity to demonstrate the superiority of the adventurers’ clan over the others. The chief arranges for a young Varmandi herder named Heortarl to act as their guide.
- Loyalty (Orlanth or Ernalda temple):
Adventurers with a high Loyalty Passion to their Orlanth or Ernalda temple are told by their priests (Asborn Thriceborn for Orlanth; Ereneva Chan for Ernalda) that a pair of monsters has been preying on the sacred cattle herd cared for by the Varmandi clan. The adventurers are each offered 1 point of spirit magic from their temple if they complete the task. The priests have arranged for a young Varmandi herder named Heortarl to act as their guide.
- Loyalty (Sartar): Dunorl the Old of Clearwine
Fort asks to meet with adventurers with a high Loyalty Sartar. Dunorl warns them that despite being free from the Lunar Occupation, Sartar is in a more precarious position than it has been since Starbrow’s Rebellion. The tribes are fighting amongst each other and it will take very little to start a tribal war that might quickly end the kingdom’s hard-won independence before Prince Kallyr has time to put Sartar on a more secure foundation. Dunorl is concerned that the monsters preying on the sacred cattle herd cared for by the Varmandi clan is just the sort of problem that might trigger a war between the Colymar and Malani tribes that would destroy Sartar. He asks them to kill the monsters that threaten the sacred cattle herd and has arranged for a young Varmandi herder named Heortarl to act as their guide.

### Option Three: Monster-Hunters for hire

At the Tin Inn, Brightflower is looking for adventurers to hunt a pair of monsters that have been preying on the Varmandi herds. She is particularly concerned about the threat to the “sacred herd” belonging to the tribal Orlanth temple. Brightflower offers to pay 50 L per monster killed and reminds the adventurers that friendship with the Varmandi clan is quite a reward on its own. She can be bargained up to 100 L per monster (success = 60 L, special success = 75 L, critical success = 100 L).

### Questions and Answers
Regardless of how the adventurers are recruited, intelligent adventurers should have some questions. Here are some answers to some likely questions.

#### Q: What kind of monsters are these?
A: “Terrible giant cats with long, deadly fangs called sakkars.
They are said to be incarnations of Death. They feed on anything from cattle to dinosaurs. These monsters are deadly.
They have already killed four cattle and seriously wounded one of the local herders.”

#### Q: Where were the monsters last seen?
A: “The better part of a day north of here, in the Gejay Hills.
Heortarl can take you there—he knows all the herder trails.”

#### Q: Are there any other monsters that might be causing trouble?
A: “There’s plenty of bandits in these hills. Tusk Riders, Orlevings, Lunar deserters... there was even a troll gang here a decade ago. But bandits we can kill—few are willing to hunt down a sakkar.”

#### Q: Why can’t the Varmandi deal with these monsters?
A: “The Varmandi are a small clan and all their warriors are required to protect them from bandits and to keep the Malani and others at bay. They don’t possess spare warriors to hunt down sakkars as well!

#### Q: Who are the Orlevings?
A: “The Orlevings are part of the Malani tribe of Two-Ridge Farm. They have a long history of feuds and raids with the Varmandi clan and there is no love lost between the two.” Once the adventurers are satisfied, a young Varmandi herder named Heortarl is summoned to guide the adventurers into the Gejay Hills to find the monsters and protect the cattle.

## Into the Gejay Hills
Heortarl brings the adventurers to the pastures where the monsters were last spotted. Heortarl takes the adventurers along herders’ trails through the rocky hills and mixed woods of pine, oak, and grassland. At this time of year, there is likely some snow on the ground, and there is more snow expected in later weeks. Heortarl wears a winter cloak made of antelope hide.

The gamemaster should strive to make Heortarl helpful and likeable to the adventurers. That shouldn’t be hard: Heortarl is enthusiastic and good-hearted; he is full of questions about the adventurers and their deeds. Heortarl wishes he could be an adventurer, fighting battles and going to far-away lands, instead of just herding someone else’s cows.

Old ruins are scattered about in the hills. Sheep graze alongside the remnants of the stone foundations of a building, a campfire is made behind a broken wall, a pile of ancient masonry, and so on. If the adventurers ask Heortarl about them, he doesn’t know much but he is happy to talk about them or anything else, despite his general lack of knowledge.

#### Q: What are these ruins?
Heortarl: “I dunno. There’s plenty of them around here.
The elders say that the Wyrmsfriends used to live here. The ruins probably belonged to them.”

#### Q: What do you know about sakkars?
Heortarl: “They are like monstrous shadowcats with swords for fangs. They hunt cows, sheep—even people!”

#### Q: What do you know about these hills?
Heortarl: “We’ve been in these hills since Time began. I’ve seen the Rainbow Mounds and the Giant’s Table. I heard that a terrible troll outlaw once lived there, preying on folk!
I’ve even seen the Old Grave where some dead god slumbers.
There’s lots of good pasture, cool streams with shady trees, and friendly spirits.”

If the adventurers ask Heortarl about bandits, the young man warns them about the perfidious Orleving clan.
“They are all thieves and murdering bandits! Did you know that Orlev Eye-spear stole the goddess from Iskalli son of Varmand, back when our ancestors first came to these lands?” If asked, Heortarl admits he doesn’t know what that really means, but he assures the adventurers that whatever it means, it can’t be a good thing.

## The Herders
After about six hours of travel, the adventurers can see a herd of about 100 black cattle being herded by five herders (Vargast, Jareena, Varlanth, Hengall, and Cara). They are rounding up the cattle for the evening. All carry slings and javelins; none wear armor beyond thick vests. The cattle are marked with the Black Spear of Colymar– this is the sacred herd of the Orlanth temple. A campfire provides welcome warmth.

The herders welcome Heortarl and the adventurers with some food (flat bread, cheese, and dried meat) and some wine that has been watered down (unless one of the adventurers manages to befriend Vargast, who has a skin of unadulterated clearwine). The herders are glad that the adventurers are here, as the clan is responsible for the sacred herd. If any of these cattle are killed or stolen, not only does the clan lose those resources but they must also make up the loss to the tribe or run the risk of angering the tribal gods!

The herders are concerned about the sakkars that have been preying on their herds, but also worried about the presence of Orleving bandits. The gamemaster should use this as an opportunity for the Varmandi to emphasize how much they hate the Orleving. The herders swap tales of betrayal, thievery, and murder—the cause of all attributed to the Orlevings.

During the night, the herders take shifts watching: adventurers who take a shift are accepted as fast friends; those who don’t are considered mere mercenaries.

Nothing happens during the night, although the gamemaster may introduce some suspense by letting adventurers who succeed with a Listen roll hear loud roars (like those of a lion) some distance away (perhaps a few kilometers).

### Varmandi Herders
These herders are typical for Dragon Pass. These herders are herding cattle, but they could just as easily be herding sheep. They attack as skirmishers using their slings. They fight in melee only if they cannot retreat. They all wear quilted linen jerkins, calf-hide trews, and fur-lined hooded cow-hide cloaks as protection against the cold.

## Monsters!
The next day, the herders round up the cattle to move them to new pastures. The herd travels slowly over a couple of hills, grazing contentedly.
Ask the players if their adventurers are wearing armor as they travel. Assuming the adventure takes place in Dark Season, emphasize how cold and uncomfortable it is. The gamemaster may wish to impose penalties on Perception skills for adventurers who insist on wearing bronze or iron armor everywhere, anywhere ranging from –10% for composite helms to –35% for full helms. The penalties to Move Quietly skills are described on the Armor chart on page 215 of the RuneQuest core rules.
Jareena warns the adventurers, “Be vigilant here. We are near Orleving lands.” With a successful Homeland Lore roll, the adventurers know that they are already on lands generally recognized as belonging to the Orleving. Alternately, a successful Scan roll spots a boundary stone.
If confronted, the herders respond proudly, “We don’t recognize those claims. This is Varmandi land.” Really attentive adventurers may try to Search or Track for signs of the sakkars. On a special or critical success with Search or a successful Track, they can see relatively recent signs of sakkars in the vicinity. However, there is not enough spoor to follow the sakkar—just enough to know that have recently been in the area.
By mid-afternoon, they arrive in a narrow lightly wooded valley cut by a small stream. There are plenty of ponds to water the animals. An adventurer who says they are trying to scout may make an opposed roll of their Scan against the Hide skill of the saber-toothed cats (80%). If the adventurer wins the contest, they get one melee round warning before the saber-tooth cats pounce!

The cats attack the herd by charging out of their concealed position and each springing at one of the cattle, stabbing with its bite and then digging in with its massive claws. By the end of the round, two cattle are down on the ground— either dead or bleeding to death.
The rest of the herd scatters and stampede in several directions. Adventurers who want to engage in melee with the saber-toothed cats need to succeed on a DEX×5 roll or else they get charged by a stampeding animal. A stampeding animal will attack and then continue running away.
The saber-toothed cats try to flee if attacked. They attack humans only if they are cornered, badly wounded, or otherwise desperate. They can be tracked to where they retreat to—a rocky outcrop. If followed there and attacked, the cats will fight to the death.

### Saber-toothed Cats
This is a mated pair of saber-toothed cats. The male is a massive creature, larger than average. The female is slightly smaller than average. They do not hunt as some lions do, where the male allows the female to do most of the dirty work. Here, each cat pulls its own weight.
Saber-toothed cats are particularly vicious in combat.
Each cat gets two claw attacks and one bite attack per round, all against the same opponent. If the cat hits a victim with both front paws, it will continue to hold on, ripping with its hind legs from that point on, and continuing to bite until the victim dies.

## Raiders
The herd of some 50 cattle has come to stop near where the narrow valley opens to join the broad Ormthane Vale.
A successful Scan or Listen roll reveals that an armed band of a half-dozen warriors are nearby—a failure results in the adventurer noticing the band only once they have come within javelin range. Two of the warriors are mounted, the rest are on foot. The warriors are marked as members of the Orleving clan.
The exact number depends on the number of adventurers:

If there are only three or fewer adventurers, then there should only be Deseros, Erlanda, and Alantos. If there are four adventurers, then add Arene. If there are more than four adventurers, add Urandorl and Intamorl.

The gamemaster should feel free to tailor the number of warriors to the capabilities of the party. The warriors should be approximately an equal match for the adventurers. One of the mounted warriors, an armored thane named Deseros Bloodsword, demands that the Varmandi run away and leave the cattle here. Deseros warns that if they try to stay or try to take the cattle, "I will take you in fetters and ransom you back to your kin."

Ask the players what they plan to do. Unless they act quickly and decisively, events will overtake them. Some options they might choose:

### Option One: Negotiate With the Orlevings
The adventurers may try to negotiate with Deseros. If the adventurers offer him 25 or more cattle, he will be satisfied and will let the herders take the cattle back to Oakton. The adventurers can try to Bargain with Deseros. With a successful Bargain, Deseros will accept twenty cattle. With a special success in Bargain, he will accept only fifteen cattle. On a critical success, he is willing to take only ten cattle. On a failure, he will demand thirty cattle. On a fumble, he will demand the entire herd. If the adventurers agree to give cattle to Deseros, Heortarl will be outraged and demand that they revoke the deal. "You cannot give Orlanth's cattle to those thieves and betrayers!"

The adventurers must overcome Heortarl's Hate (Orleving Clan) Passion using an appropriate Rune or skill (such as Orate). If they fail, Heortarl shouts abuse at the Orleving, triggering the same result as if they did nothing (see Option Three: Ignore Deseros). If the adventurers manage to overcome Heortarl's Hate (Orleving Clan) Passion, they may return to Oakton with whatever cattle they don't give to Deseros. However, upon their return, they must justify their decision to the Varmandi clan leaders and may find themselves on the hook for repaying the lost cattle to the tribal king.

### Option Two: Attack
The adventurers may decide to attack the Orleving warriors. Heortarl and Varlanth will aid the adventurers. The Orleving are unwilling to fight to the death over cattle. If any take serious wounds (such as reducing any hit location to negative hit points or reducing total hit points to three or less), they will retreat to the Orleving lands to seek reinforcements. By evening, an Orleving war party numbering a dozen mounted and armored warriors will show up. The following day, they make a search through the Gejay Hills—how determined their search is depends on whether any of the Orleving were killed in the attack.

### Option Three: Ignore Deseros
If the adventurers ignore Deseros and try to drive the cattle back, Deseros demands that they stop and gathers his warriors into a skirmish line. Deseros then threatens them unless they stop; however, he takes no action except to follow them. His followers bang their weapons on shields, and shout threats, but also take no action. Heortarl says to the adventurers, "They are afraid of you!" He then mocks the Orlevings as cowards and says, "We are taking our cattle home and there's nothing you impotent whoresons can do about it!" At this point, Alantos the Huge loses his temper and throws a javelin at Heortarl, doing 9 points of damage to his abdomen, incapacitating (and nearly killing) Heortarl. The next round Deseros leads an attack on the adventurers, hoping to chase them off. As above, they are unwilling to fight to the death over cattle.

These warriors are led by Deseros Bloodsword, a Malani thane, and are competent warriors. He is careful and cautious, if not particularly innovative, in his tactics. He is reluctant to start a fight with a comparable-strength group of adventurers and prefers negotiation; once combat is engaged, however, he and his followers fight like wildcats.

## Reward and Reputation
The adventurers get +1D3 points of Reputation each for rescuing the sacred herd; +1D3 Reputation each if they chased off the Orleving warriors (regardless of whether they started a feud with the Orleving clan in the process). If the adventurers manage to return to Oakton with the sacred herd largely intact and the Varmandi herders alive, they prove their friendship with the Varmandi clan. Chief Korol presents them with a small herd of ten cows from the clan's own herd and swears friendship with the adventurers. If the adventurers return with a much-diminished sacred herd and two or more Varmandi herders dead, they will meet a cool but civil reception. It should become obvious to them that the chief suspects that their efforts were halfhearted or even deliberately ineffectual, and he will begrudgingly reward them a single cow as a token gesture.

## Revenge and Retaliation
If the adventurers kill any of the Orleving, the Orleving demand compensation or seek vengeance upon the Varmandi and adventurers. The Orleving demand that full ransom price for all dead Orleving and half the ransom price for all wounded Orleving be paid by the adventurers and/or the Varmandi clan. Soon after, when the adventurers are in Apple Lane, an Issaries Herald named Yandara Truespeaker arrives, prominently displaying her Herald's Staff (which indicates she is under the protection of Issaries and the Lightbringers). Yandara has been paid by the Orleving to deliver their demands for compensation for the killed and injured kin. The adventurers are given a season to pay up or the Orleving shall seek revenge against them and their kin. The extent of the Orleving retaliation is up to the gamemaster, but it should initially be roughly proportionate to the ransoms of the Orlevings injured or killed by the adventurers. Orleving warriors might start by attacking tenants or other low-status associates of the adventurers and escalate based on the adventurers' actions. A feud can make an excellent component in an ongoing campaign, with tit for tat potentially escalating into war.

## Return to Oakton
If the adventurers negotiate with Deseros and manage to overcome Heortarl's Hate (Orleving Clan) Passion, they can return to Oakton with whatever cattle they don't give to Deseros. However, upon their return, they must justify their decision to the Varmandi clan leaders (requiring successful use of appropriate Communication skills) or may find themselves on the hook for repaying the lost cattle to the tribal king.
