# Pellucidar — The World at the Earth's Core

*A RuneQuest Campaign Setting inspired by the Pellucidar series of Edgar Rice Burroughs*

---

## The World

Pellucidar is a hollow earth — a vast prehistoric world contained within the shell of our own planet, lit by a tiny, eternal inner sun suspended at the precise center of the sphere. The inner sun never sets. It never rises. It hangs motionless at the zenith of every point in Pellucidar, casting a perpetual noon that bleaches the sky and casts no meaningful shadow. There is no night. There are no seasons. There is no concept of time as surface-dwellers understand it.

**The great consequence of the eternal sun** is that every creature in Pellucidar lives by sleep rather than darkness. Gilaks and beasts alike rest when tired and wake when rested. "Yesterday" and "tomorrow" are slippery concepts. Two tribes may meet and each believe they left their village only recently, but they may have been travelling for what a surface-world clock would measure as days apart.

**The problem of navigation** haunts every surface-worlder who descends. Without stars, without a moving sun, without a compass that works reliably so deep inside the earth, there are no cardinal directions. A traveller in Pellucidar navigates by landmarks alone. Every experienced guide maintains a mental map of peaks, rivers, and distinctive terrain. The inner surface curves upward in every direction; the horizon is replaced by a gentle upward curve that eventually disappears into haze. On a clear day, a man with good eyes standing in open country can see terrain that is, by surface reckoning, many miles "above" him.

### The Ecology of Pellucidar

Pellucidar is a Mesozoic and Cenozoic menagerie existing simultaneously. Dinosaurs graze alongside mammoths. Saber-tooths hunt in the same territory as giant hyena-dogs. The evolutionary pressures that drive surface-world extinction simply have not operated here. Everything large, everything ancient, everything lethal from across geological time has found a niche and flourished.

The dominant creatures of the open Lidi Plains are the **lidi** themselves — great sauropod grazers that move in herds of dozens. The **tandor** (mammoths) occupy the more temperate zones. Apex predators — the **tarag** (saber-tooths), **jaloks** (hyena-dogs), and **thipdars** (giant pterosaurs) — hunt across every terrain type. In the seas, things worse than any of these prowl the depths of the Sojar Az.

Above it all, controlling the most intelligent tier of the ecosystem, are the **Mahars**.

## Factions

### The Mahars

The Mahars are the dominant civilization of Pellucidar — eyeless, telepathic pterosaurs of great cunning and immense antiquity. They cannot speak any vocalized language; they communicate mind-to-mind with crystalline clarity across distances of hundreds of feet. Their civilization predates humanity in Pellucidar by geological ages. They have cities, rituals, records, and a sophisticated understanding of biology — all of which they have used primarily to systematize their exploitation of humans.

The Mahars view Gilak humans as a useful species: strong enough to dig, carry, and fight under direction; simple enough to control. They use Sagoth gorilla-men as their military and police force. Gilaks are taken in slave raids, marched to Mahar cities, and set to labor in mines and building projects. Periodically, Mahars conduct **feeding rituals** in which human captives are consumed — not for sustenance alone, but as part of ancient rites whose precise meaning has been lost even to the Mahars themselves.

**What the Mahars want:** Stability. The continuation of their civilization. The suppression of David Innes and the Federated Kingdoms. The recovery of the Sacred Formula (see below).

**What the Mahars fear:** The surface world's weapons. The possibility of true human unity. Sunlight, in concentrated form, disrupts their senses.

**Key Mahar city:** **Phutra**, a vast underground city of carved stone tunnels and great vaulted chambers, deep below the Pellucidaran surface. The Mahars do not need light; Phutra is lightless and labyrinthine.

### The Federated Kingdoms of Pellucidar

Founded by David Innes after his descent via the Iron Mole, the Federated Kingdoms are an alliance of formerly isolated Gilak tribes united by the radical idea that humans do not have to be slaves. The capital is **Sari**, the first city David Innes helped build — the first permanent human settlement with organized defenses.

The Federated Kingdoms are perpetually young and perpetually embattled. Every alliance is fragile. Every tribe's loyalty must be earned fresh. Feuds between tribal chieftains constantly threaten the coalition. David Innes and his wife Dian spend much of their time traveling between settlements, mediating disputes, training warriors, and organizing defenses against Sagoth raids.

**What the Federated Kingdoms need:** More member tribes. Weapons. Training. The Sacred Formula. A coherent means of measuring and coordinating time across settlements.

**Key figures:** David Innes (Emperor), Dian the Beautiful (his wife and political anchor among coastal tribes), Ghak the Hairy One (King of Sari, loyal warrior-king), Ja of the Mezops (Admiral of the inland sea fleet).

### The Mezops

The Mezops are island-dwelling warriors of the Sojar Az — skilled sailors and bowmen who have maintained independence from the Mahars by virtue of their control of the inland sea. They fish, trade, and occasionally fight with extraordinary effectiveness at range. Once David Innes won the trust of Ja, the Mezop chief, they became the primary naval arm of the Federated Kingdoms.

**What the Mezops want:** To be respected as equals. Fair trade. Security for their island homes. The freedom to sail without Mahar interference.

**Naval strength:** Mezop outrigger canoes are fast and maneuverable. A fleet of Mezops can intercept and outrun almost any threat on the Sojar Az.

### The Horibs

The snake-men of Pellucidar's jungle interiors. The Horibs are isolationist and deeply hostile to all warm-blooded creatures. They do not seek conquest; they seek territory and prey. Attempts at diplomacy invariably fail — the Horibs regard any approach that doesn't end in combat as an invitation to set a more elaborate ambush.

**Where they live:** Dense jungle and swamp, especially in regions where cave systems surface close to jungle floor — they move between above and below ground with ease.

**Threat level:** Individually beatable; in ambushes, lethal. They have no interest in alliance, negotiation, or mercy.

### The Korsars

Corsairs descended from surface-world pirates who found their way into Pellucidar centuries ago. They've gone native in the worst sense: raiding settlements along the Korsar Az sea's shores, taking slaves, and treating both Gilak and Mahar as targets of opportunity. Unlike the Mahars, they don't have a stable civilization — they have a culture of raiding and a fleet of shallow-draught warships.

**What the Korsars want:** Plunder. Slaves they can sell or put to work. The subjugation of the Mezops, who have consistently defeated them at sea.

**Political complexity:** The Korsars are human. David Innes's diplomats have occasionally attempted to bring them into the Federated Kingdoms. These attempts have all failed, but the possibility remains open.

### Independent Gilak Tribes

Most of the human population of Pellucidar lives in scattered tribes that have had no contact with the Federated Kingdoms. They speak dozens of mutually unintelligible dialects. They range from peaceful foragers to aggressive warrior cultures. They are, collectively, the primary recruiting ground for the Federated Kingdoms — and the primary source of Sagoth slave-raid targets.

**Key fact for GMs:** A "neutral" Gilak tribe is not a blank slate. They have histories, feuds, territorial claims, religious practices (usually animist, focused on the great beasts), and reasons to trust or distrust any approaching stranger. The first impression matters enormously.

## Locations

### Sari

The first city of the Federated Kingdoms. Built on a defensible hilltop by David Innes and Ghak the Hairy One, Sari is a permanent settlement with stone walls, organized food storage, and something approaching a garrison. It is the political heart of the young federation.

**Population:** Several hundred Gilaks, growing slowly as other tribes send representatives or refugees.

**Defenses:** Stone walls on the accessible approaches, armed gate guards (trained by David Innes in organized ranks rather than mob attacks), a signal system of fire beacons.

**GM notes:** Sari is the logical base of operations for a player group allied with the Federated Kingdoms. It has resources, information, and a community of NPCs. It is also a target — Sagoth raids have been beaten off, but a serious Mahar-directed assault would be a major campaign event.

### Phutra

The great underground city of the Mahars. Phutra is carved from living rock deep beneath the Pellucidaran surface — a vast complex of tunnels, vaulted chambers, feeding pools, and slave pens. It is lightless. The Mahars navigate by heat sense and echolocation; the Sagoths move by memory and touch.

**Access:** A series of guarded ramp-tunnels descend from surface openings concealed in rocky outcrops on the Mahar Plains.

**The slave pens:** Large cavern cells where Gilak captives are held before assignment to labor details or the feeding ritual. Escape is theoretically possible through the tunnel system but near-impossible without a guide.

**The ritual chamber:** A vast domed space at Phutra's heart where the Mahars perform their periodic feeding ceremonies. The architecture suggests these rituals predate even Mahar civilization as it currently stands.

**GM notes:** Phutra is the dungeon of Pellucidar. The labyrinthine tunnel system, the absolute darkness, the telepathic surveillance of the Mahars, and the Sagoth patrols all make it extremely hostile. A rescue mission into Phutra is a full campaign arc.

### Amoz

A coastal city on the shores of the Sojar Az, homeland of Dian the Beautiful and politically significant as the gateway between the inland sea and the eastern territories. Amoz trades with Mezop fleets and maintains an uneasy independence from both the Federated Kingdoms and the Mahars.

**Political situation:** Amoz's current chief (whoever the GM designates) may be allied with, neutral toward, or quietly hostile to the Federated Kingdoms. Dian's presence in Sari makes Amoz symbolically important to David Innes even if its chief proves difficult.

**Resources:** Fishing, coastal trade, boat construction.

### The Sojar Az (Terrible Sea)

The great inland sea of Pellucidar — immense, warm, and primeval. Its shores curve upward in every direction (because all shores in Pellucidar curve upward). The sea is rich in fish and sea-life, including things much larger and more dangerous than any surface shark.

**Mezop sovereignty:** The Mezops control the Sojar Az militarily. Any Korsar, Sagoth, or Mahar force attempting to cross it in force will encounter Mezop fleets.

**Sea creatures:** The Sojar Az contains marine reptiles of genuinely prehistoric scale — plesiosaur-equivalent creatures that make open-water crossings dangerous. Even Mezop fishermen give certain depths a wide berth.

### The Lidi Plains

Endless open grassland occupying a vast belt of Pellucidar's inner surface. The lidi herds that give the plains their name number in the thousands, and the ecosystem built around them — jalok packs, tarag solitary hunters, thipdar overhead, Gilak nomad tribes following the herds — is the closest Pellucidar comes to a stable equilibrium.

**Navigation:** The Lidi Plains are featureless to an untrained eye. Experienced Gilak guides navigate by a complex system of remembered rock outcrops, river crossings, and stand-out trees. Without a guide, parties become lost almost immediately.

**Gilak nomads:** Several tribes follow the lidi herds seasonally (or what passes for seasonal in a world without seasons — they follow herd movements). These nomads are among the most independent and least easily recruited of all Pellucidar's humans.

### The Mountains of the Thipdars

A range of highlands riddled with cliffs and thermal updrafts, the ideal environment for large flying reptiles. Thipdar nesting grounds make the mountain passes genuinely hazardous — a flock of thipdars defending a nesting territory is an aerial combat encounter of the worst kind.

**Resource:** The mountains contain mineral deposits that surface-worlders would recognize as valuable (iron, copper). Smelting is beyond most Gilak technology, but David Innes understands its potential.

**The Mahars' interest:** The Mahars patrol the mountain approaches. The mineral wealth here is not lost on them.

### The Korsar Az

The sea that mirrors the Sojar Az on the "other side" of Pellucidar (directions are meaningless, but the geographic relationship is consistent from any given point). The Korsars control this sea and the coastlines around it. Korsar ships are shallow-draught, fast, and crewed by experienced raiders.

**Access from Sari:** Reaching the Korsar Az requires crossing substantial overland territory or the Sojar Az and portaging.

### The Buried Empire

Deep below Phutra, accessible through passages the Mahars use rarely and guard carefully, lies what scholars from the surface world who have read David Innes's accounts call the Buried Empire — an even more ancient Mahar civilization, now abandoned, whose corridors hold records in a language the current Mahars cannot fully decipher. These archives may hold the original Sacred Formula, pre-dating the copy that was lost.

**GM notes:** The Buried Empire is a megadungeon opportunity. Rooms that predate the current Mahar civilization, creatures adapted to absolute lightlessness, and knowledge that could reshape Pellucidar's balance of power.

## Key NPCs

### David Innes — The Emperor

A Connecticut mining engineer in his late twenties who descended to Pellucidar in the Iron Mole, an experimental burrowing vehicle designed by his friend Abner Perry. David's practical intelligence, surface-world knowledge of chemistry and mechanics, and his refusal to accept any situation as hopeless have made him the driving force behind the Federated Kingdoms.

**As a PC contact:** David is the primary political patron for a group operating within the Federated Kingdoms. He issues missions, provides what resources Sari can spare, and expects results. He is approachable and genuinely egalitarian — he has no patience for hierarchy for its own sake.

**Complication:** David cannot navigate in Pellucidar without landmarks or a guide. This is a consistent vulnerability. He has been captured, separated, and lost more times than he can count. The player group may find themselves rescuing him rather than the other way around.

**Stats in bestiary:** david_innes_rq

### Dian the Beautiful — Princess of Amoz

Daughter of a coastal Gilak chieftain, now wife of David Innes and the political heart of his coalition. Dian is not a passive figure. She negotiates with coastal tribe chiefs as a peer, commands respect through lineage and force of personality, and fights when cornered with a ferocity that surprises people who underestimate her.

**As a PC contact:** Dian is more politically adept than David and more suspicious of strangers. Earning her trust takes longer but is worth more — her endorsement opens doors his cannot.

**Complication:** Dian will not accept rescue that she views as condescending. If the player group attempts to "save" her in a way that implies she couldn't have managed herself, they will have made an enemy.

**Stats in bestiary:** dian_beautiful_rq

### Ja — Chief of the Mezops

The greatest chief the Mezops have known in living memory, Ja is a brilliant naval tactician and a loyal ally to David Innes — once Innes proved himself worthy of that loyalty. Ja commands the Mezop fleet and has personally sunk Korsar vessels. He is proud, direct, and has no patience for politics that doesn't result in action.

**As a PC contact:** Ja is the gateway to Mezop alliance. Impressing him requires a deed, not words. Once impressed, he will commit Mezop warriors to a cause without hesitation.

**Role in campaign:** Naval transport, intelligence on the Sojar Az, access to coastal settlements.

### Ghak the Hairy One — King of Sari

A physically enormous Gilak warrior who became David Innes's first and most steadfast ally. Ghak is impulsive, courageous to a fault, and the kind of leader who fights in the front rank. He rules Sari with a directness that occasionally makes him a poor diplomat but an excellent general.

**As a PC contact:** Ghak provides local knowledge, garrison support, and the occasional blunt assessment that other NPCs are too polite to offer. He is completely loyal to David Innes and, by extension, to anyone Innes vouches for.

### Hooja the Sly One — Antagonist

The Pellucidaran villain. Hooja is a Gilak opportunist who has repeatedly allied with Mahars, Korsars, and anyone else willing to give him power over others. He is cunning, charming when it suits him, and utterly without scruple. He has escaped capture multiple times, each time leaving a trail of betrayed allies and dead innocents.

**As a campaign antagonist:** Hooja works best as a recurring antagonist who operates through proxies. The group defeats his plan; he escapes; he reappears with a new scheme. He is never the largest threat in the room but is consistently the most dangerous person in any negotiation.

### Abner Perry — The Inventor

David Innes's elderly companion and the designer of the Iron Mole. Perry is a brilliant, absent-minded inventor who has been adapting surface-world technology to Pellucidar with mixed results. He has produced a functioning printing press, several types of improved weapons, and at least three devices that exploded unexpectedly.

**As a PC contact:** Perry is the source of improvised technological solutions. Need something that doesn't exist in Pellucidar? Perry will attempt to build it. It will probably work. It will probably also cause one unexpected side effect.

## Campaign Arcs

### Arc 1: Break the Chains

**Summary:** A Sagoth slave column has captured thirty Gilaks from a tribe allied with the Federated Kingdoms. The column is three days' march from Phutra. Intercept and free the captives before they disappear into the slave pens.

**Complications:**
- The column is better armed than typical patrols — a Sagoth Captain and two sub-captains
- Three of the captives are too injured to run; they need carrying
- Freeing the captives alerts a Mahar who was travelling with the column, who immediately begins telepathically summoning reinforcements

**Resolution:** Getting the freed Gilaks to Sari opens a formal diplomatic channel with their tribe. The alerted Mahar's summons means a Sagoth sweep of the area over the next "sleep cycle" — the party has limited time before the region becomes extremely hostile.

**XP goals:** Liberate the captives; avoid (or defeat) the pursuing Sagoth sweep; establish contact with the freed tribe.

---

### Arc 2: The Sacred Formula

**Summary:** David Innes reveals that the Mahars possess a document — called the Sacred Formula — that describes the key to weakening or destroying Mahar civilization. This document is kept in Phutra's ritual archive. Steal it.

**The document's actual content:** The Sacred Formula describes the telepathic suppression field the Mahars use to prevent their slave population from coordinating resistance. Destroy the generator mechanism described in the formula and Mahar mind-control across a territory becomes impossible for a generation.

**Complications:**
- The archive is deep inside Phutra, past the slave pens and through the ritual chamber
- Perry can translate the document — but only if the party brings it back intact
- Hooja the Sly One also wants the formula, for entirely different reasons

**Resolution:** The formula, once translated, gives the Federated Kingdoms a tactical advantage that shifts the next major conflict decisively in their favor. But Hooja now knows the group took it, and he will act on that information.

---

### Arc 3: Unite the Tribes

**Summary:** David Innes needs three tribal chieftains to formally join the Federated Kingdoms before a diplomatic summit at Sari. Each chieftain has a different reason for hesitation. The party serves as his envoys.

**The three chieftains:**

*Chief Kor of the Mountain Gilaks:* Refuses to join because a neighboring tribe (already a Federated Kingdoms member) raided his hunting grounds two seasons ago. The dispute must be mediated.

*Chieftain Rela of the Swamp Tribes:* Her tribe lives in constant fear of Horib raids. She will join if someone demonstrates they can defeat a Horib ambush — she won't commit warriors to a federation that can't protect them.

*War-Chief Gron of the Northern Plains:* Believes the Federated Kingdoms is David Innes's personal empire, not a genuine alliance. Needs to be convinced that Gilak tribes retain their sovereignty. This is a roleplaying challenge — he's right to be suspicious and wrong to conclude the federation is dangerous.

**Resolution:** Success brings three new tribes into the federation, significantly expanding Sari's recruiting base. Failure (or partial success) leaves exposed flanks in the next Sagoth sweep.

---

### Arc 4: The Corsair Threat

**Summary:** A Korsar fleet has entered the Sojar Az and is raiding Mezop fishing villages. Ja needs the party's help coordinating a response — either a naval engagement or a diplomatic overture, depending on the group's approach.

**The Korsar admiral:** A pragmatic raider named Bohar who is genuinely interested in a trade agreement but cannot show weakness to his own crew. He will negotiate if approached in private; in public, he must appear to be negotiating from a position of strength.

**Naval option:** The Mezops can outmaneuver the Korsar fleet with the right tactics. Perry has designed a primitive catapult that can be mounted on Mezop outriggers. Installing and testing it under fire is a significant challenge.

**Diplomatic option:** Bohar wants access to Sari's trade goods (specifically Perry's metal tools) more than he wants to raid. A deal that lets him return to Korsar territory with something valuable avoids the battle entirely — but requires convincing Ja that this is not appeasement.

**Resolution:** Either route results in a temporary cessation of Korsar raiding on the Sojar Az. A naval victory leaves Bohar's rival (more aggressive) positioned to take his command. A diplomatic deal requires at least two trade convoys to actually arrive before it becomes stable.

---

### Arc 5: The Buried Empire

**Summary:** Deep below Phutra, in passages the current Mahars rarely enter, lies the archive of an even older Mahar civilization. David Innes has received fragmentary intelligence (from an elderly Gilak who survived a Phutra work detail) that this archive may contain a suppression mechanism for Mahar telepathy.

**Access:** The party must penetrate Phutra (a major challenge in itself), find the lower passages (no map exists; they must navigate by inference), and descend into the Buried Empire.

**What they find:** The Buried Empire is a megadungeon in three zones:
- *The Approach Corridors:* Abandoned, partially collapsed, home to ryths and hordes of giant cave insects
- *The Record Vaults:* Intact rooms full of Mahar writing that Perry can transliterate but not yet fully read
- *The Deep Mechanism:* A chamber containing a device of Mahar engineering whose function is either the telepathic generator (the target) or something far more dangerous

**Resolution:** Destroying or disabling the mechanism creates a weeks-long window during which Mahar telepathy in Phutra's territory is disrupted. This is the opportunity for the largest possible military operation of the campaign — a coordinated assault on Phutra itself while the Mahars are essentially blind.

---

### Arc 6: Hunt of the Tarag

**Summary:** A particularly large tarag has established a territory directly between Sari and a newly allied tribe's village. The tribe will not send warriors through the area until it's cleared. A relatively contained arc with room for wilderness and combat.

**The tarag in question:** Notably large (treat as a champion variant with +3 to all stats and an additional attack). It has already killed two Gilak scouts and one of Ghak's warriors.

**Complication:** The tarag is denning in a rocky outcrop that turns out to be one of the concealed entrances to Phutra's tunnel system. The Mahars are aware something is denning in their access point and have been avoiding it — but they won't tolerate it indefinitely.

**Resolution:** Killing the tarag clears the route. The discovery of the Phutra access point is actionable intelligence for the Sacred Formula arc.

---

### Arc 7: Stop the Mahar Ritual

**Summary:** Phutra's feeding ceremony is scheduled within the next sleep period. Thirty Gilak captives from the most recent slave column are designated as offerings. The party has intelligence on timing and (partial) access through the tarag entrance.

**The complication of timing:** "The next sleep period" is meaningless as a countdown without stars. The party must either get inside information (a freed Gilak who knows Phutra's rhythms) or accept they're working with imperfect timing.

**Options:**
- *Infiltration:* Enter disguised as a work detail, locate the captives, free them before the ceremony
- *Disruption:* Cause enough chaos in Phutra that the ceremony cannot proceed (fire, collapse, releasing the ryth from the food cache)
- *Frontal assault:* Not advised. But a party that has cleared several arcs may have the resources for a coordinated strike with Federated Kingdoms warriors

**Resolution:** Success saves the captives and demonstrates Phutra is not impenetrable — raising the morale of every tribe in the Federated Kingdoms. Partial success (some captives freed, ceremony delayed) still counts as a meaningful victory.

---

### Arc 8: Rescue from Phutra

**Summary:** An NPC important to the player group — a tribe's chieftain they recruited, a Mezop officer who sailed with them, or a fellow surface-worlder (Perry, if he wandered) — has been captured and taken to Phutra.

**Urgency:** The feeding ceremony cadence is irregular. The party does not know when the next one is scheduled.

**Available intelligence:** A Gilak who escaped Phutra two "sleep periods" ago knows the tunnel layout well enough to guide the party to the slave pens but not to the ritual chamber.

**Resolution:** Customized to the group. The core challenge is extraction under telepathic surveillance in absolute darkness against Sagoth patrols and at least one Mahar.

## Using This Setting with RuneQuest Rules

### Weapon Substitutions

Pellucidar's humans do not have bronze or iron weapons. Use the following substitutions:

| Standard RQ Weapon | Pellucidar Equivalent | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Spear (1d6) | Stone-tipped spear (1d6) | Identical stats |
| Dagger | Flint knife | -1 damage, +5% skill (lighter) |
| Short sword | Bone club | Same damage, different flavor |
| Bow | Bone bow | Identical stats; Mezop specialty |
| Medium shield | Hide-wrapped wicker shield | Reduce armor value by 1 |

David Innes and other surface-worlders may have firearms. Treat rifles as: Damage 2d8+2, range 200m, Reload: 2 rounds (single shot), Skill: INS×5 initially (learning curve). Pistols: 1d8+2, range 30m, Reload: 3 rounds.

### Magic

By default, Pellucidar has no recognized magic system. Mahars' telepathy functions as a non-magical power (it is a biological ability, not a spirit or rune power). However:

- **Shamanic Gilak healers** may use RuneQuest Spirit Magic at GM discretion, representing a tradition of spirit-communication that exists in isolated tribes
- **Mahar rituals** may incorporate effects that look like RuneQuest Rune Magic — treated as biological Mahar technology rather than divine connection
- If the group comes from Glorantha, they may retain their magic while in Pellucidar; Mahar telepathy does not distinguish between a spell and a skill

### Navigation Rules

When the party is moving overland without a guide who knows the territory, use the following:

- **With a Gilak guide:** No navigation roll required; the guide handles it
- **Without a guide, known route:** Navigate (INT×3) each day of travel; failure means a half-day detour
- **Without a guide, unknown route:** Navigate (INT×1); failure means the party is lost (GM determines direction of drift)
- **Surface-worlders:** Permanent -20% to any navigation roll in Pellucidar, regardless of skill

David Innes always fails his navigation roll unless guided. This is a story truth, not a mechanical quirk.

### Time and Scheduling

Pellucidar has no objective timekeeping. GMs should resist the temptation to impose surface-world time frames. Instead:

- Measure time by **sleep periods** (roughly 8-hour equivalent rest cycles)
- Measure travel by **landmarks** (three river crossings, past the twin peaks, etc.)
- Campaign events are not "in two days" but "before the Sagoth sweep reaches the southern passes"

This creates a sense of urgency without a ticking clock, which is more faithful to the source material.
