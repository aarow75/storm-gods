# Campaign Setting Ideas

A reference list of settings suitable for tabletop RPG campaigns — organized by copyright status, with source material and publication information.

---

## Public Domain Settings

### Edgar Rice Burroughs

All ERB works published before 1928 are in the US public domain. Later works (1928–1950) entered the public domain under the 1998 Sonny Bono Act as their terms expired.

#### Amtor (Venus) — *Carson of Venus* series

A world of perpetual cloud cover, towering tree cities, warring city-states, and airship pirates. Carson Napier, an Earthman, navigates Venusian politics, slavery, and revolution in a setting tonally similar to Barsoom.

| Title | Year |
|---|---|
| *Pirates of Venus* | 1932 |
| *Lost on Venus* | 1933 |
| *Carson of Venus* | 1938 |
| *Escape on Venus* | 1946 |
| *The Wizard of Venus* | 1964 (posth.) |

#### Caspak — *The Land That Time Forgot* trilogy

A hidden Antarctic island where biological evolution is compressed into geography: the further inland you travel, the more advanced the life forms. Dinosaurs, ape-men, and near-humans coexist in distinct bands. Survival, tribal politics, and the mystery of Caspak's evolutionary mechanism drive the action.

| Title | Year |
|---|---|
| *The Land That Time Forgot* | 1918 |
| *The People That Time Forgot* | 1918 |
| *Out of Time's Abyss* | 1918 |

#### The Moon — *The Moon Maid* series

A far-future Earth and an inhabited Moon with a subterranean civilization. Mixes planetary romance with future-history invasion narrative.

| Title | Year |
|---|---|
| *The Moon Maid* | 1926 |
| *The Moon Men* | 1926 |
| *The Red Hawk* | 1925 |

### H. Rider Haggard

Haggard died in 1925; all works are public domain.

#### Kukuanaland — *King Solomon's Mines*

A lost African kingdom ruled by a usurper king, discovered by a party of hunters following an ancient map. Rich in tribal politics, lost treasure, and underground spectacle. The prototype for the "lost world" adventure.

| Title | Year |
|---|---|
| *King Solomon's Mines* | 1885 |
| *Allan Quatermain* | 1887 |

#### Kor — *She* series

An ancient, immortal queen rules a hidden civilization in interior Africa from the ruins of a once-great empire. Themes of obsessive love, ancient history, and the cost of immortality. The hidden city of Kor is one of Victorian adventure fiction's most evocative locations.

| Title | Year |
|---|---|
| *She* | 1887 |
| *Ayesha: The Return of She* | 1905 |
| *She and Allan* | 1921 |
| *Wisdom's Daughter* | 1923 |

### A. Merritt

Merritt died in 1943; his works are in the public domain.

#### The Moon Pool

A Pacific island conceals a gateway to an ancient underwater civilization dominated by a mysterious, luminous entity called the Shining One. Lush, weird-fantasy atmosphere with an alien intelligence at its center.

| Title | Year |
|---|---|
| *The Moon Pool* | 1919 |
| *The Conquest of the Moon Pool* | 1919 |

#### The Ship of Ishtar

A man discovers an ancient Babylonian artifact — a model ship — that transports him to a world where a divine conflict between two gods is frozen in stalemate aboard a mystical vessel crewed by the living and the dead.

| Title | Year |
|---|---|
| *The Ship of Ishtar* | 1924 |

### H.G. Wells

Wells died in 1946; all works are public domain.

#### The Time Machine

Far-future Earth (802,701 AD) where humanity has diverged into two species: the passive, childlike Eloi above ground and the predatory Morlocks below. A unique temporal exploration setting with strong class-conflict subtext.

| Title | Year |
|---|---|
| *The Time Machine* | 1895 |

#### The First Men in the Moon

The Moon is hollow and inhabited by the Selenites — a vast insect civilization that has adapted its members biologically to specific social roles. An expedition from Earth discovers this world and faces the consequences.

| Title | Year |
|---|---|
| *The First Men in the Moon* | 1901 |

#### The Island of Doctor Moreau

A remote island where a disgraced surgeon creates human-animal hybrids through vivisection. Horror-adjacent; suits darker campaigns exploring the boundaries of science and identity.

| Title | Year |
|---|---|
| *The Island of Doctor Moreau* | 1896 |

### William Hope Hodgson

Hodgson died in 1918; all works are public domain.

#### The Night Land

The far future: the Sun has gone cold. The last remnant of humanity survives inside a miles-high pyramid fortress called the Last Redoubt, surrounded by endless darkness filled with ancient, malevolent entities that cannot enter while the fortress's energies hold. A deeply atmospheric dying-earth horror setting.

| Title | Year |
|---|---|
| *The Night Land* | 1912 |
| *The Dream of X* | 1912 (abridged version) |

---

### H.P. Lovecraft

Lovecraft died in 1937; US works are public domain.

#### The Dreamlands

A vast dream realm accessible to accomplished dreamers, with its own geography, history, and inhabitants. Includes the city of Celephais, the plateau of Leng, the Enchanted Wood, and the cavern of flame. Tonally surreal rather than horrific — closer to Dunsany than Lovecraft's cosmic horror.

| Title | Year |
|---|---|
| *The Dream-Quest of Unknown Kadath* | written 1926–27; published 1943 (posth.) |
| *Celephais* | 1922 |
| *The White Ship* | 1919 |
| *The Silver Key* | 1929 |
| *Through the Gates of the Silver Key* (with E. Hoffmann Price) | 1934 |

### Robert E. Howard

Howard died in 1936. Many of his original Conan stories from 1932–36 have entered or are entering the public domain. Note: while the *stories* become public domain, the Conan *character* has active trademark complications; consult current legal status before commercial use.

#### The Hyborian Age (Conan)

A fictional pre-Cataclysm age of Earth, roughly equivalent to the ancient world but with its own geography, nations, and history. Diverse cultures (Aquilonia, Stygia, Cimmeria, Turan) across a continent-spanning map. One of the most fully realized sword-and-sorcery settings in pulp fiction.

| Title | Year |
|---|---|
| *The Phoenix on the Sword* | 1932 |
| *The Scarlet Citadel* | 1933 |
| *The Tower of the Elephant* | 1933 |
| *Black Colossus* | 1933 |
| *Conan the Barbarian* (full bibliography) | 1932–1936 |
| *The Hyborian Age* (world-building essay) | 1936 |

## Contemporary Fiction Settings

These settings are **not** public domain and would require licensing for commercial use. Listed here as inspiration for homebrewed analogues or for personal/non-commercial campaigns.

| Setting | Author | Source Works | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Melnibonéan Empire / Young Kingdoms** | Michael Moorcock | *Elric of Melniboné* series (1961–) | Decadent dying empire, Law vs. Chaos cosmology, cursed soul-drinking sword |
| **Nehwon / Lankhmar** | Fritz Leiber | *Fafhrd and the Gray Mouser* series (1939–) | Prototype urban fantasy; thieves' city; gritty low-magic adventure |
| **The Dying Earth** | Jack Vance | *The Dying Earth* (1950), *Eyes of the Overworld* (1966) | Far-future entropy; baroque sorcery; mordant wit; directly influenced D&D spell system |
| **Glorantha** | Greg Stafford | *RuneQuest* (1978–), *HeroQuest* | Mythic Bronze Age; already integrated as the canonical RuneQuest setting |
| **The Continent** | Andrzej Sapkowski | *The Witcher* series (1986–) | Monster hunting, moral ambiguity, Slavic folklore base |
| **Westeros** | George R.R. Martin | *A Song of Ice and Fire* (1996–) | Medieval political intrigue, dynastic warfare, slow magic |
| **Discworld** | Terry Pratchett | *Discworld* series (1983–2015) | Comic fantasy; strong city-state (Ankh-Morpork) setting; estate is protective of IP |

## Notes on Public Domain

**US public domain rules (simplified):**
- Published before 1928: public domain in the US regardless of registration or renewal.
- Published 1928–1977: depends on copyright registration and renewal; many works fell into public domain due to non-renewal.
- Works by authors who died before 1955 (70 years after death): often public domain in many countries under life+70 rules, but US rules apply independently.

When adapting public domain material for published commercial content, verify the specific edition and publication date. Translations, introductions, and later editorial additions may carry their own copyright even when the original text is free.
