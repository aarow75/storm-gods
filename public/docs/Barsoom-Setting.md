# Barsoom — The World at the Center of the Solar System

*A Campaign Setting inspired by the Barsoom (John Carter of Mars) series of Edgar Rice Burroughs*

---

## The World

Barsoom is Mars — but not the cold, airless Mars of modern astronomy. It is an ancient, dying world of dwindling oceans, desiccated sea-bottoms, and civilizations so old that their golden ages predate human history on Earth. A world where the atmosphere itself must be maintained by a single, planet-spanning machine called the Atmosphere Factory, whose failure would suffocate all life on Barsoom within days.

**The dying seas:** What were once planetary oceans are now vast, flat basins of ochre dust called the Dead Sea Bottoms. Ancient sea-moss still grows in patches. Coral cities that once sat on ocean floors now stand open to the sky, their perfect stone corridors home only to banths, white apes, and the occasional Green Martian scouting party. Barsoom's remaining water exists in shallow seas, polar ice, and underground rivers, and is fiercely contested.

**Low gravity:** Barsoom's lower gravity has profound physical consequences. Red Martians are extraordinarily agile compared to surface-worlders, leaping distances and surviving falls that would be fatal on Earth. John Carter's Earth physiology, translated to Martian gravity, makes him effectively superhuman — the strongest and fastest man on Barsoom, despite being merely athletic by Earth standards.

**The atmosphere plant:** Somewhere on Barsoom's surface, a massive building of ancient manufacture continuously processes atmospheric gas, maintaining breathable air across the planet. The facility is ancient beyond reckoning; no living Martian built it or fully understands it. The only entities who know the master key to re-enter it in an emergency are the Jeddaks of Helium. The vulnerability of all Barsoomian life to this single point of failure is the planet's most closely held secret.

**No concept of death:** Red Martians live for roughly a thousand years before walking voluntarily to the Valley Dor, believing they travel to paradise. This pilgrimage is so deeply embedded in religion and culture that questioning it is heresy. The Therns have maintained this lie for millennia.

### Barsoomian Technology

Martian civilization has been sophisticated long enough that certain technologies appear almost magical:

- **Radium weapons:** Pistols and rifles that fire explosive radium projectiles. Effective at range but require loading (not semi-automatic). The eighth ray propels fliers; the ninth ray is used in certain ancient weapons.
- **Fliers:** Lighter-than-air craft powered by the eighth ray, ranging from one-person scouts to massive dreadnoughts. Not true aircraft — they use a repulsion principle against Barsoom's magnetic field. Altitude is easily adjustable; they do not require runways.
- **Universal language:** All Barsoomian races share a common tongue, enabling communication across all encounters. Learning it is the first challenge for a surface-world newcomer.
- **Telepathy:** Partial telepathy is common among Barsoomians. Green Martians are particularly adept; red Martians can project and receive emotional impressions. Full mind-reading is rare and associated with specific individuals like Matai Shang.

## Factions

### Red Martians — The City-States

The dominant civilization of Barsoom, copper-skinned and agile, organized into competing city-states each ruled by a Jeddak (king). The red Martians are fully human in appearance except for their coloring, their extraordinary physical capability in low gravity, and their lifespans measured in centuries. Their technology is ancient and sophisticated. Their politics are a web of rivalries, marriage alliances, and periodic wars.

**Key division:** Helium and Zodanga are the primary powers. Helium is broadly noble, Zodanga broadly aggressive and expansionist. Other city-states (Ptarth, Kaol, Gathol, Lothar) maintain varying independence. The red Martians collectively share a civilization that is thousands of years old and has already declined from a greater peak.

**What they want:** Security, trade, access to water, and the continuation of their civilization. Most red Martians simply want to live their thousand years in comfort and walk peacefully to the River Iss when the time comes. Their leaders want the same things human leaders always want: more of everything.

**Their blind spot:** The River Iss. The pilgrimage is so deeply woven into red Martian religion that exposing the Therns risks cultural catastrophe. John Carter's exposure of the truth in the second book reshapes the civilization's self-understanding and is genuinely traumatic for many.

### Green Martians — The Nomadic Hordes

The Green Martians are the oldest sentient species remaining on Barsoom — fifteen feet tall, four-armed, tusked, with antenna-like sensory organs at their temples. They are not savage; they are specifically cruel, having evolved a culture that systematically eliminates compassion because in their nomadic, competitive environment, compassion led to death. They do not build cities. They move between the ruins of old ones, using ancient structures as temporary gathering sites.

**Key tribes:**
- **Tharks:** The largest and most powerful horde, currently led by Tars Tarkas. They occupy the region around the city of Thark and the Dead Sea Bottoms to the south.
- **Warhoons:** Rivals to the Tharks, significantly more aggressive and less organized. They fight Tharks on sight and have no equivalent of Tars Tarkas's cautious leadership.
- **Torquasians, Tharkians, Okarians (Green division):** Other hordes mentioned across the series.

**What they want:** Survival, dominance over the Dead Sea Bottoms, and the occasional raid on red Martian caravans or settlements for supplies and slaves. The concept of alliance with red Martians is culturally foreign; Tars Tarkas's alliance with John Carter is explicitly described as unprecedented.

### The First Born — River Iss Pirates

Black-skinned, supremely arrogant, and hiding in plain sight as the gods of Barsoom's afterlife. The First Born operate from Omean, a subterranean ocean beneath the Valley Dor, accessible only through a tunnel concealed under the South Polar ice. They live by predating the pilgrims who descend the River Iss to the valley — stripping them of possessions, enslaving them, or simply killing them. The Therns deliver a steady supply.

**What they want:** The status quo maintained. The pilgrimage provides them with a renewable supply of slaves, wealth, and labor. Their goddess Issus is treated as a living deity — in reality a vain, unstable woman of extraordinary age whose divine pretensions are supported by the First Born's military control of the valley.

**Structure:** The First Born have a military hierarchy, a fleet of ships on the Omean, and a chain of command centered on their chief naval officers. Their greatest warriors are among the best fighters on Barsoom — centuries of predatory isolation have produced elite soldiers with nothing but contempt for every other species.

### The Therns — False Gods

White-skinned, golden-wigged priests who have maintained the Valley Dor illusion for so long that their oldest members may have forgotten it was ever a lie. The Therns live in the valley above the River Iss and below the First Born's domain, harvesting the pilgrims before the First Born take what's left.

**Their theology:** The Therns present themselves as the gods of Barsoom's afterlife. Pilgrims who reach the valley are told they have arrived in paradise. The Therns then enslave or consume them. The Therns in turn are subject to the First Born, whom the Therns worship as their own gods. The entire religious edifice is a predatory pyramid.

**What they want:** The perpetuation of their privileged position. Therns are terrified of exposure — their power depends entirely on red Martian civilization never learning the truth. Matai Shang, the Father of Therns, is the primary antagonist of the second book and a recurring enemy through the third.

**Tactical note:** Therns fight when necessary but prefer manipulation, escape, and the use of proxies. A Thern who draws a weapon is already in serious trouble.

### The Yellow Martians — Okar

The yellow-skinned Martians of the north polar region, sealed behind a magnetic field that keeps their domain safe from Green Martian incursion. Physically similar to red Martians but culturally distinct and isolated. Their ruler Salensus Oll is a major antagonist in the third book.

**Access:** The only reliable way into Okar is through a gap in the magnetic field kept secret by the Okarian ruling class. John Carter reaches it by thoat, surviving the open arctic.

**What they want:** Their isolation. Salensus Oll also wants Dejah Thoris, which creates the crisis of the third book.

### Helium — The Dominant City-State (Faction Within Red Martians)

Worth detailing separately as the primary allied faction. Helium consists of two domed cities — Greater Helium and Lesser Helium — connected by a causeway, with a combined population larger than any other red Martian state. It is ruled by the Jeddak Tardos Mors and his son Mors Kajak (Dejah Thoris's grandfather and father respectively).

Helium is explicitly described as more enlightened than most Martian states — more open to outsiders, more willing to engage with novel ideas. This is why John Carter finds acceptance there. The military of Helium is excellent; the navy, under Kantos Kan's command, is arguably the best on Barsoom.

## Locations

### Helium

The twin-domed city-state of Dejah Thoris, located on the edge of the Dead Sea Bottoms in the equatorial region. Greater Helium is the larger dome; Lesser Helium is the satellite. Both are surrounded by walls, aerial docks, and the residences of the ruling class.

**GM notes:** Helium is the primary safe haven for a group allied with John Carter. Resources, information, allies, and political contacts are available here. It is also a target — Zodanga besieged it before John Carter's arrival, and other threats recur across the series. The city has an active political life; not everyone in Helium's ruling class is aligned with Tardos Mors's policies.

**Key sites within Helium:**
- The royal palace (where Dejah Thoris lives and court politics happen)
- The aerial naval base (where Kantos Kan operates)
- The arena district (where John Carter first proves himself)
- The slave markets (where prisoners from Zodangan raids appear)

### Zodanga

Helium's great rival — a city that moves. Zodanga is built on enormous metal legs that carry it slowly across the Dead Sea Bottoms. The entire city migrates, following resources and maintaining strategic unpredictability. It is ruled by Than Kosis (during the first book) and his son Sab Than, whose marriage to Dejah Thoris was forced under threat of war.

**GM notes:** Zodanga is not a cartoonishly evil place — it is a city of millions of red Martians who mostly just want to live their lives. Its aggressive policies come from its ruling class, not its population. A group operating inside Zodanga will find ordinary citizens as well as soldiers. Zodanga's unique construction means infiltration and escape routes are complicated by the city's constant motion.

### Thark

The gathering site of the Thark horde — an ancient ruined city that the Green Martians use as a base camp during their periodic assemblies. Not a city in any red Martian sense: no markets, no permanent population, no maintained infrastructure. The Tharks bring their incubators (containing Green Martian eggs) to Thark, hatch them, and raise their young in the ruins before the horde moves on.

**GM notes:** John Carter first arrives on Barsoom near Thark and spends substantial time as a Thark captive. The internal politics of the Thark horde — the hierarchy of warriors, the status games, the testing of captives — are detailed in the first book. A group accepted by the Tharks gains access to Green Martian territory and resources, but at the cost of constant political management.

### The Dead Sea Bottoms

The vast dried basins that cover much of Barsoom's surface. Crossing the Dead Sea Bottoms on thoatback is the standard overland travel method. The bottoms contain:
- Ruins of ancient underwater cities, now open to the sky
- Patches of sea-moss (the only significant ground cover)
- Thoat herds (feral and domesticated)
- Green Martian patrol routes
- Banth territories
- The skeletal remains of ancient sea vessels

**Navigation:** Unlike Pellucidar's navigation challenges, Barsoom has a sun, two moons (Thuria and Cluros), and the stars — navigation by sky is possible. The challenge is scale: the Dead Sea Bottoms are continental in size, and a mounted party can travel for days without seeing another person.

### The Valley Dor and the River Iss

The River Iss flows from the equatorial region south to the Valley Dor at the South Polar ice cap. Every red Martian who reaches the age of a thousand years voluntarily walks into the river and drifts south, believing they journey to paradise. No one who enters the river is supposed to return — and the Therns and First Born enforce this rule violently.

**The valley:** A beautiful, fertile area near the south pole, in sharp contrast to the barren Dead Sea Bottoms. Flowers, trees, warmth. Also: plant men, white apes, banths, and Thern guards. The beauty is real; the danger is greater.

**The River Iss approach:** The river is accessible at various points along its course. Pilgrims are typically unarmed by the time they reach the valley, having given everything away before their departure. A combat-capable group arriving at the valley would be an extraordinary anomaly.

**GM notes:** The Valley Dor is the dungeon of the second Barsoom book. Infiltrating it, navigating its dangers, reaching the concealed passage to Omean below, and returning to civilization — when return is considered impossible — is the core campaign arc of "The Gods of Mars."

### Omean — The Underground Sea

Beneath the Valley Dor, accessible through a concealed tunnel below the South Polar icecap, the underground sea of Omean is the home base of the First Born. A vast subterranean ocean illuminated by phosphorescent organisms, dotted with islands that serve as the First Born's cities and shipyards.

**The fleet:** The First Born maintain a large navy on Omean — ships that have never had to face serious opposition, because no one outside the valley knows Omean exists. John Carter's introduction of surface-world tactics and Heliumite warships into this sealed environment is the central action of the second book's climax.

**Access:** The concealed passage is the only reliable entrance. The tunnel is guarded but known to John Carter after his escape. A second entrance may exist in the polar ice — the precise geography is left somewhat vague in the source material, giving GMs flexibility.

### Okar — The Yellow Martian Domain

The north polar region, sealed by a magnetic field that destroys metal objects and vehicles approaching from outside. Within, the Yellow Martians maintain a civilization protected from Green Martian incursion by this field. Their capital Kadabra is a domed city near the magnetic pole.

**GM notes:** Reaching Okar requires either knowing the gap in the magnetic field or traveling overland across the arctic (as John Carter does in the third book, with his calot Woola). Once inside, the Yellow Martians are insular but not initially hostile — they have enough internal politics to create story without immediate combat.

### Manator — City of Jetan

Manator is a self-contained city-state notable for its inhabitants' obsession with Jetan, the Martian form of chess. In Manator, political disputes, criminal sentences, and personal quarrels are settled through Jetan — played on a massive board with living pieces who fight and die on the squares. The game is more than entertainment; it is the organizing principle of Manatan life.

**GM notes:** Manator is the primary setting of "The Chessmen of Mars." It offers a different kind of adventure — political intrigue, arena combat structured around game rules, and an elaborate society where knowing Jetan's rules is a survival skill. The ruling class of Manator uses the Jetan games as both entertainment and judicial process.

### Dead Cities — Aaanthor, Horz, Lothar

Scattered across the Dead Sea Bottoms are completely abandoned cities from Barsoom's ancient past — perfectly preserved by the dry Martian air, inhabited only by occasional Green Martian squatters, feral animals, and whatever else has moved in. These cities are archaeological treasure troves and extremely dangerous.

**Lothar** is unusual: a dead city that appears entirely empty but is actually inhabited by the ancient Lotharians, who maintain their existence through mass hypnosis and collective mental projection. Their "reality" is partially constructed from will. This makes Lothar one of the most genuinely strange locations in the series.

**Aaanthor** and **Horz** are more straightforward ruins — good locations for dungeon exploration, ancient technology, and encounters with creatures that have made the ruins their territory.

### Toonol — The Laboratory of Ras Thavas

A city-state associated with scientific expertise and the morally unconstrained surgeon Ras Thavas, the self-styled Master Mind of Mars. Toonol is not particularly powerful politically, but the presence of Ras Thavas makes it a source of technologies and horrors that no other location on Barsoom can produce.

## Key NPCs

### John Carter — Warlord of Mars

A Virginia gentleman of uncertain but apparently extreme age, transported to Barsoom through a process he describes in deliberately vague terms (he was in a cave, he fell asleep, he woke on Mars). On Barsoom, his Earth musculature combined with Martian gravity makes him the most physically capable individual on the planet. His code of honor — specifically, the inability to stand by while someone is in danger — drives him into most of his adventures.

**As a patron:** John Carter issues missions through his political position (first as a war-chief allied with Tharks, then as Warlord of Helium). He is approachable, direct, and does not stand on ceremony. He will ask others to do things he would do himself; he will not ask others to do things he considers beneath his own honor.

**Complication:** John Carter is occasionally separated from Barsoom entirely, returning to Earth involuntarily. During these absences (which can last years by Barsoomian reckoning), the political situation deteriorates and Dejah Thoris typically needs rescuing. The player group may find themselves acting in John Carter's absence.

**Stats in bestiary:** john_carter_barsoom

### Dejah Thoris — Princess of Helium

The most celebrated beauty of Barsoom and the political heart of the Helium alliance. Her abduction by Sab Than of Zodanga triggers the first book; her subsequent captures trigger additional arcs. Despite being repeatedly in danger, Dejah Thoris is consistently portrayed as resourceful, knowledgeable, and capable of agency within the constraints her society imposes.

**As a patron:** Dejah Thoris has scientific and political knowledge that John Carter lacks. She can identify ancient technology, understand political relationships between city-states, and provide context that turns a blunt military problem into a solvable one. She also has the full force of Helium's loyalty behind her — access to her is access to Helium's resources.

**Complication:** Her captivity is the recurring story engine of the series. A campaign that uses her as a patron should expect periodic "Dejah Thoris has been taken again" crises.

**Stats in bestiary:** dejah_thoris_barsoom

### Tars Tarkas — Jeddak of Thark

The greatest warrior and the only emotionally fully-developed adult in the Thark horde. Tars Tarkas's capacity for friendship — carefully concealed, culturally forbidden — is what makes him John Carter's ally rather than his captor. He is the bridge between surface-world newcomers and the Green Martian world.

**As a patron:** Tars Tarkas provides access to Green Martian territory, intelligence on Dead Sea Bottom activity, and military muscle when John Carter's Heliumite allies are unavailable. His price is always respect — he expects to be treated as an equal, not an ally of convenience.

**Complication:** Tars Tarkas's position as Jeddak is not secure. There is always a challenger. Supporting him through internal Thark politics is as important as any external mission.

**Stats in bestiary:** tars_tarkas_barsoom

### Kantos Kan — Heliumite Officer

The reliable professional. Kantos Kan's friendship with John Carter is established in the first book when they are both captured and enslaved; it persists through every subsequent adventure. He is not the most dramatic character in the series, but he is the most consistently useful — the person John Carter can call on to command a fleet, organize a rescue, or hold the line while the heroes do something reckless.

**As a patron:** Kantos Kan commands the Heliumite navy and has extensive relationships throughout Barsoomian military circles. For any mission involving aerial warfare, espionage within the Heliumite military structure, or needing a reliable extraction, Kantos Kan is the contact.

**Stats in bestiary:** kantos_kan_barsoom

### Sola — Green Martian Woman

Unusual among the Tharks in possessing genuine compassion, inherited from a father (Tars Tarkas) she was never allowed to know was her father. Sola serves as John Carter's first guide to Thark culture and his protector during his initial captivity. She is not a warrior but is brave and morally courageous in a culture that punishes both qualities.

**As an NPC:** Sola represents the bridge between Green Martian culture and the humanizing force that Tars Tarkas embodies. She is an excellent guide character for groups navigating Thark society.

### Matai Shang — Father of Therns (Antagonist)

The supreme leader of the Thern hierarchy — manipulative, genuinely psychic, and willing to form alliances with the First Born or anyone else who will help him maintain the deception that sustains Thern power. He is the primary antagonist of the second and third books, recurring even after his apparent defeats because he is very good at surviving.

**As an antagonist:** Matai Shang works best at one remove — pulling strings, forming coalitions, using subordinates as proxies. A direct confrontation with him should be a late-campaign event, after the group has already disrupted the Thern organization significantly.

### Issus — False Goddess of the First Born (Antagonist)

The divine ruler of the First Born — in reality a vain, decaying woman of extreme age who has maintained her position through the First Born's military control of the valley. Issus is genuinely disturbing in the source material: she collects beautiful women and interesting men as pets, ages them rapidly through stress and captivity, then has them killed and eaten in a final ceremony.

**As an antagonist:** Issus is the horror at the center of the Valley Dor arc. She is not a combat threat — she is revealed as a hollow deity whose power depends entirely on others believing in her. Exposing her is more important than defeating her militarily.

### Ras Thavas — The Master Mind of Mars (Complex Antagonist)

A surgeon and scientist of breathtaking genius and almost total moral indifference. Ras Thavas has developed the ability to transplant brains between bodies, reanimate the dead, and create synthetic life forms. He does all of this with scientific curiosity and no ethical framework whatsoever. He is not evil in the conventional sense; he simply does not register harm to individuals as morally meaningful data.

**As a complex figure:** Ras Thavas can be an antagonist, an unwilling ally, or a source of resources — his laboratory contains technology available nowhere else on Barsoom. The problem is that working with him involves accepting his ethical vacuum, and he will betray any alliance the moment his scientific interests diverge from his ally's goals.

## Campaign Arcs

### Arc 1: Princess in Peril

**Summary:** Dejah Thoris has been captured by Sab Than of Zodanga and forced into a political marriage to prevent war. The alternative is Helium's total defeat. John Carter and his Thark allies must reach Zodanga, locate and extract Dejah Thoris, and destroy Zodanga's aerial fleet before it can obliterate Helium.

**The three-part challenge:**
1. Cross the Dead Sea Bottoms to Zodanga (navigation, Green Martian encounters, potential banth packs)
2. Infiltrate Zodanga and locate Dejah Thoris without triggering a citywide alert
3. Extract her and destroy or neutralize the Zodangan fleet with minimal time remaining

**Complications:**
- John Carter's Earth physiology is unknown to most Zodangans; disguise is initially viable but not indefinitely
- The fleet destruction requires coordinating with Thark warriors who have never operated in an urban assault
- Sab Than is not an idiot; he has contingency plans for exactly this kind of rescue

**Resolution:** Success lifts the siege of Helium and establishes John Carter's reputation on Barsoom. The political aftermath — integrating the Thark alliance into Heliumite policy — is a secondary story thread.

---

### Arc 2: The Pilgrimage of Iss

**Summary:** Someone important to the group — a patron, a contact, a loved one — has walked into the River Iss. The pilgrimage cannot be undone; the person is now considered dead by all of red Martian civilization. The group knows the truth about the valley and must go in to find and extract them.

**The challenge:** Entering the river as non-pilgrims requires concealment. The River Iss is monitored by Thern agents at key points. Once in the valley, the plant men hunt by night, white apes control certain ruins, and the First Born's presence makes the southern end of the valley effectively a war zone.

**Complications:**
- Return from the valley is culturally impermissible — extracting the captive means declaring them "alive" in a society that considers them dead and gone
- The Therns will pursue anyone who escapes and knows the truth
- Captive may have been in the valley long enough to have been moved to the First Born's domain

**Resolution:** Successful extraction creates a political crisis: the returned pilgrim's testimony, if believed, would shatter Barsoomian religion. The group must decide how much of the truth to reveal and to whom.

---

### Arc 3: Into Omean

**Summary:** With knowledge of the passage beneath the South Polar ice, the group must breach the underground sea of Omean, locate allies held captive by the First Born, and fight their way out against a fleet that has never faced serious opposition.

**The tactical situation:** The First Born's fleet on Omean is large and well-crewed. The group's advantages: surprise, knowledge that the fleet exists, and access to Heliumite fliers that can be introduced through the polar passage.

**Complications:**
- The passage entrance must be identified and cleared while avoiding First Born patrols
- The underground sea's geography is unmapped; navigation is by inference
- Issus controls significant leverage over the First Born's loyalty — defeating the fleet means also confronting her cult status

**Resolution:** Victory on Omean opens the possibility of exposing the entire Thern/First Born pyramid to red Martian civilization. Whether the group pursues this revelation is a major campaign decision with long-term consequences.

---

### Arc 4: Unmasking the False Gods

**Summary:** The group has evidence of the Thern deception — testimony, artifacts, or physical proof that the River Iss is not paradise. Presenting this evidence to red Martian civilization without being killed by Thern agents or dismissed as heretics is the challenge.

**The political landscape:**
- A Jeddak who believes the evidence would be politically powerful but is risking religious upheaval in their own city
- Religious leaders within red Martian cities have built careers on pilgrimage theology
- Matai Shang is actively working to discredit any testimony

**Complications:**
- Witnesses who escaped the valley are legally dead and therefore cannot testify in most red Martian courts
- Thern infiltrators within Helium and other cities create a counter-narrative
- Some red Martians would rather not know — the pilgrimage is comforting, and the alternative is confronting that their entire civilization has been prey for millennia

**Resolution:** Success reshapes Barsoomian religion and eliminates the Therns' power base. It also creates a political vacuum in the southern territories that other factions will rush to fill.

---

### Arc 5: The Gladiatorial Games of Manator

**Summary:** The group (or one of them) has been captured and brought to Manator, where they are designated as a living piece in the Great Jetan game. To survive and escape, they must win the games, navigate Manatar's rigid internal politics, and find allies in a city that has no interest in outsiders' survival.

**The Jetan games:** Jetan is played on a 10×10 board with 20 pieces per side. Living Jetan uses actual people as pieces; those taken by the opposing piece fight and the loser dies. The group must win enough games to earn the right to leave — or find another way out.

**Complications:**
- Manatan law is absolute within its walls; direct escape attempts are politically dangerous as well as physically difficult
- The ruling Jeddak of Manator has his own agenda that intersects with the group's situation in unpredictable ways
- Alliances within the prisoner population are possible but fraught — everyone here is trying to survive at everyone else's expense

**Resolution:** Escape from Manator requires either winning the games legitimately, engineering a political crisis that creates an opening, or a well-prepared breakout. Success should leave Manator's political structure in some disarray.

---

### Arc 6: The Mad Surgeon of Toonol

**Summary:** Ras Thavas has done something that cannot be ignored — an experiment, an atrocity, or a theft of technology that creates a threat requiring intervention. Reaching Toonol, dealing with Ras Thavas, and containing the damage without being experimented upon is the challenge.

**The Synthetic Men problem:** Ras Thavas's synthetic beings (Hormads) are nearly unkillable and can regenerate from pieces. If he has produced them in significant numbers, the military threat they represent is potentially civilization-ending. The group must find and destroy the production source before the Hormads spread.

**Complications:**
- Ras Thavas himself is brilliant, unpredictable, and has no genuine loyalty to anyone
- He may be willing to help contain the Hormads if the situation threatens his own survival — but his help comes at a price
- Toonol's political situation is complicated; the city is not unified in its relationship with Ras Thavas

**Resolution:** Destroying the Hormad production facility resolves the immediate threat. Ras Thavas remains a loose element — he will recreate his work eventually, and the group's relationship with him (enemy? reluctant ally? information source?) shapes future arcs.

---

### Arc 7: Aerial Warfare

**Summary:** Helium faces a major naval attack — a coalition of enemies has assembled a combined fleet larger than anything Helium can match individually. Kantos Kan has a plan, but executing it requires the group to take on specific high-risk roles: infiltration, sabotage, or tactical command of a portion of the fleet.

**The asymmetric approach:** Helium cannot win a straight fleet engagement. The group's options:
- Infiltrate the enemy flag vessel and eliminate the commanding officer before the battle
- Sabotage the enemy fleet's propulsion systems in port before they can assemble
- Coordinate a Thark ground attack that forces the enemy fleet to split its attention
- Find and reveal information about the coalition's internal tensions that will cause it to fracture

**Complications:**
- Kantos Kan's overall plan requires the group's piece to succeed; failure cascades
- The enemy admiral may not be entirely villainous — understanding their motivation could enable a non-combat resolution
- Aerial combat rules favor the larger fleet; the group's contribution must be qualitative, not quantitative

**Resolution:** Victory establishes Helium's aerial dominance for a generation. The political aftermath — what obligations does Helium now have to the groups that helped? — is a secondary thread.

---

### Arc 8: The Warhoon Hunt

**Summary:** The group is being hunted across the Dead Sea Bottoms by a Warhoon pursuit party of twenty warriors. They have a head start, minimal supplies, and need to reach the nearest red Martian outpost before the Warhoons catch them.

**The nature of the arc:** A chase across a continent. The Dead Sea Bottoms offer limited cover, and thoats (if the group has them) can only go so far before needing rest. The Warhoons are relentless and know the terrain. This arc is about resource management, difficult choices, and what the group reveals about itself under pressure.

**Complications:**
- A fellow escapee is injured and cannot travel at full speed
- A banth pack is following the same route, attracted by the noise of pursuit
- The nearest red Martian outpost turns out to be Zodangan, not Heliumite — the enemy

**Resolution:** Surviving the pursuit opens a relationship with whatever settlement the group reaches. If it's Zodangan, the political situation is complex and interesting. If Heliumite, they arrive as heroes with a story to tell.

## Using This Setting with Any Rules System

### Weapons of Barsoom

| Barsoom Weapon | Rules System Equivalent | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Long sword | Standard sword (1d8) | The universal Barsoomian sidearm |
| Short sword | Short sword (1d6) | Common backup weapon |
| Dagger/knife | Dagger (1d4) | Universal concealed weapon |
| Radium pistol | Pistol (1d8+2) | Explosive projectile; limited range |
| Radium rifle | Rifle (3d6) | Long range, must reload between shots |

**Radium weapon notes:** Radium projectiles explode on contact with light. A weapon that is fired in darkness and then exposed to light (or fired into shadow, then into sunlight) may detonate unexpectedly. GMs can use this detail for dramatic moments.

### Martian Gravity Effects

Characters from Earth (or any higher-gravity world) operating on Barsoom gain:
- STR effectively doubled for feats of physical effort
- DEX-based rolls at +20% for all jumping and acrobatic actions
- Movement increased by 4 (or equivalent in your system)
- Falling damage halved

Native Barsoomians have adapted to these conditions — their stats already reflect Martian-normal. John Carter's stats represent the full Earth-advantage applied.

### Calot Bonding

A calot that has bonded to a character will not leave them, will interpose itself between them and danger, and communicates through body language and simple vocalizations. Mechanically:
- Once per combat, a bonded calot may automatically intercept one attack targeting its bonded person (the calot takes the hit)
- The calot will track its bonded person by scent across any distance
- Calots cannot be intimidated, persuaded, or magically compelled to abandon their bond

Earning a calot's bond requires an initial period of kind treatment — specifically, not treating the calot as a tool or weapon. Once bonded, the relationship is permanent.

### Thoat Riding

Thoats respond to telepathic commands from trained riders. For non-telepathic characters (including surface-worlders), this means:
- Trained rider (has spent at least 1 week with a specific thoat): normal riding; the thoat follows voice commands learned during training
- Untrained (commandeered thoat): must use STR to control; the thoat resists and may bolt
- Panicked thoat: STR+CON combined check to stay mounted

A thoat that has known its rider for months develops something approaching loyalty — it will return to a fallen rider's location if released, and will not flee during combat unless struck.

### Flier Mechanics

Martian fliers are not aircraft in the conventional sense — they use the eighth Barsoomian ray to repel against the planet's magnetic field. They can hover, descend, and ascend without forward movement, making them more like elevators than planes in certain situations. Combat aboard a flier is ship-to-ship combat adapted to three dimensions:
- Speed: Small scout (fast), medium patrol (moderate), large dreadnought (slow but overwhelming)
- Cover: Crew are partially exposed on open decks; most small fliers have no enclosed crew area
- Boarding: Grappling hooks and ropes are standard; boarding actions are fought hand-to-hand on the decks
- Weapons: Radium cannons (large versions of radium rifles) mounted at bow and stern
